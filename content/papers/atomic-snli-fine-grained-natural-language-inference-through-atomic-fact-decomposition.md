## TL;DR

Atomic-SNLI converts SNLI hypotheses into atomic facts and augments them with filtered entailment, neutral, and contradiction examples. Standard NLI models are 0.61–0.79 percentage points less accurate under atomic aggregation than on sentence-level inference, but training on Atomic-SNLI improves multi-fact reasoning: DeBERTa-v3-xsmall gains 10.07 accuracy points on three-fact hypotheses while exposing which facts drive the final label. The gains do not extend reliably to four-fact cases, whose test subset contains only 25 examples.

## Research Question

Can [[concepts/atomic-fact-decomposition|atomic fact decomposition]] support accurate and transparent natural language inference, and can training on decomposed examples close the gap between atomic and sentence-level reasoning?

## Motivation

Sentence-level NLI assigns entailment, contradiction, or neutral to a premise-hypothesis pair without identifying which parts of the hypothesis determine the judgment. Decomposing a hypothesis into minimal claims can localize supporting, missing, or conflicting information. In practice, however, a hard compositional rule magnifies atomic classification errors: entailment requires every atom to be correct, while one predicted contradiction can veto the entire hypothesis.

## Contributions

- Measures the atomic-versus-sentence-level performance gap for four pretrained NLI cross-encoders on a decomposed SNLI test set.
- Introduces Atomic-SNLI, whose training split contains 625,281 premise-atom instances derived from 150,735 unique premises.
- Builds label-specific data pipelines: filtered decomposition for entailment, direct filtering plus BM25 retrieval and reranking for neutrality, and direct extraction plus validated Qwen3-32B generation for contradiction.
- Evaluates atomic training with probability-sum aggregation across DeBERTa-v3-xsmall, ELECTRA-small, and BERT-base.
- Shows how atom-level predictions provide a traceable explanation of sentence-level NLI decisions.

## Method

DEC-MODEL decomposes each SNLI hypothesis into a set of self-contained facts. For entailment examples, all resulting atoms inherit entailment and are retained when an NLI model assigns entailment confidence above 0.5. Neutral examples combine directly filtered pairs with atomic facts retrieved by BM25, reranked by a cross-encoder, and retained above the same class-confidence threshold. Contradictions combine direct filtering with minimally edited contradictions generated by Qwen3-32B and kept only when an NLI-model ensemble unanimously predicts contradiction above 0.5 confidence.

Training and validation use premise-atom-label triples. The test set retains each premise, its set of hypothesis atoms, and the original sentence label. Rather than applying only hard logical rules, the main experiments sum the models' class-probability vectors across atoms and choose the largest aggregate probability. This soft aggregation is intended to reduce the effect of a single atomic error while preserving atom-level outputs for inspection.

## Experiments

On 9,824 decomposed SNLI hypotheses, four existing NLI models lose 0.61–0.79 accuracy points when their atomic predictions are composed, compared with direct sentence-level prediction. The test distribution is highly skewed: 8,767 hypotheses have one atom, 881 have two, 149 have three, 25 have four, and only two have five.

The training comparison fine-tunes DeBERTa-v3-xsmall, ELECTRA-small, and BERT-base for three epochs on either SNLI or Atomic-SNLI. On two-fact hypotheses, atomic training improves accuracy by 1.71, 1.48, and 0.45 points, respectively. On three-fact hypotheses, the gains are 10.07, 8.06, and 7.38 points; DeBERTa-v3-xsmall also gains 8.16 F1 points. Results reverse on the 25 four-fact examples: accuracy falls by 16, 12, and 24 points. The two five-fact examples are too few to support a substantive conclusion.

Hard logical aggregation exhibits the expected error asymmetry. For three-fact entailment, DeBERTa-v3-xsmall reaches 100% precision but only 20.69% recall; for contradiction it reaches 100% recall but 70.97% precision. Probability summation produces more balanced class metrics and lets individual atom labels identify the evidence behind entailment, neutrality, or contradiction, although direct sentence-level inference remains stronger on several reported slices.

## Limitations

- Contradiction augmentation depends on LLM-generated minimal edits and model-based filtering, which may not represent the range of real contradictions.
- Decomposition errors from DEC-MODEL propagate into both training labels and explanations.
- The aggregation rules treat atoms as independent even when their meanings or referents interact.
- Most test hypotheses contain one atom; four- and five-atom results are based on 25 and two examples, respectively.
- The same NLI confidence threshold of 0.5 is used in several construction stages, and the paper does not isolate how filtering affects coverage or residual label noise.
- Probability summation is more error-tolerant than strict rules but is not itself a guarantee of logically valid composition.
