## TL;DR

Parties requiring sustained membership or supporting signatures tend to exhibit greater legislative unity and candidate loyalty. Rehmert argues that these eligibility rules screen out opportunists and encourage socialization into party norms. Roll-call and candidate-survey analyses support these associations, but do not identify causal effects or cleanly separate the two mechanisms.

## Research Question

Does restricting who may seek a party nomination help explain party unity, beyond who selects candidates and how centralized selection is?

## Motivation

Candidate-selection research emphasizes selectors' control over renomination. Eligibility rules can also shape the pool of aspirants before selection: membership requirements and internal networks may recruit people already committed to the party or encourage that commitment to develop.

## Contributions

- Develops an adverse-selection account of formal candidacy requirements, distinguishing deterrence from socialization.
- Combines Political Party Database rules with nearly 500 historical party constitutions and statutes.
- Examines parliamentary voting and candidates' stated willingness to follow the party when their own opinions conflict with its position.

## Method

Formal requirements include party membership, minimum membership duration in months, signatures, loyalty pledges, union membership, religious affiliation, and monetary deposits. These are distinct institutional provisions rather than a single strictness scale.

The voting analysis uses fractional logit models of the Agreement Index, which includes yea, nay, and abstention votes. Country and party-family fixed effects accompany controls for party age, size, government status, and selectorate centralization; standard errors are clustered by country and party.

Two candidate-survey logit analyses match rules either to the time candidates joined their parties or to nomination. The historical analysis uses party fixed effects and party-clustered errors, controlling for membership duration, party employment, and electoral personal-vote incentives. The nomination analysis excludes candidates who competed in the preceding election and uses country and party-family fixed effects.

## Experiments

These are observational comparisons, not randomized experiments.

| Analysis | Sample | Main reported findings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parliamentary voting, Table 2 | 166 observations, 89 parties, 16 countries | Membership, minimum duration, and signatures are positively associated with unity in some specifications, sometimes only marginally significantly. Deposits and absence of formal criteria have negative associations in some models. |
| Rules at joining, Table 3 | 639 candidates, 10 parties in the estimation table | Longer required membership predicts greater loyalty. Pledges and absence of formal criteria are not significant with party fixed effects. |
| Rules at nomination, Table 4 | 1,220 candidates, 37 parties, six countries | Minimum membership duration and, in some specifications, signatures predict greater loyalty. Absence of formal criteria is not significant. |

On the 0–100 Agreement Index scale, the paper reports an average marginal effect of 0.512 for requiring membership, against mean unity of about 98.7. For rules at joining, the reported membership-duration contrast corresponds to a 3.2 percentage-point reduction in choosing one's own opinion. For nomination rules, the narrative reports roughly 11- and 10-point probability reductions for a one-standard-deviation increase in required duration and a signature requirement, respectively (Figures 2–3).

## Limitations

- Rules may respond to electoral strategy, internal conflict, or the available candidate pool; fixed effects do not remove all endogeneity.
- Recorded roll calls are selected and influenced by whipping, so agreement does not isolate preference similarity or loyalty.
- Contemporary rules may differ from those under which incumbent MPs entered. Historical coverage primarily concerns social democratic parties and contains limited rule variation.
- Formal criteria omit informal vetting and possible waivers. No formal criteria need not mean no screening.
- The supplied text inconsistently labels survey outcome coding: table headings use party position over own opinion, while some prose and appendix labels use the reverse. Results above follow the substantive narrative rather than treating coefficient signs as unambiguous. Figure 2's narrative also calls its duration contrast about six months, whereas Table A2 reports a standard deviation of 8.458 months.
