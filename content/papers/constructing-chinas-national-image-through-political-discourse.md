## TL;DR

Analyzing 25 English Government Work Reports from 2001–2025, Ma and Crosthwaite interpret China's projected national image as moving through global integration, domestic stabilization, and global engagement. Their [[concepts/corpus-assisted-discourse-studies|Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies]] approach combines comparative keyword statistics with collocations and contextual readings. The findings concern official self-representation, rather than measured international perceptions.

## Research Question

Which keywords remain salient in China's Government Work Reports across 2001–2025, and how do their changing contexts construct national image?

## Motivation

Short-period studies can miss shifts spanning China's Five-Year Plans. The authors connect quantitative lexical patterns to qualitative interpretation over five planning cycles, using contemporaneous U.S. State of the Union addresses as a reference corpus.

## Contributions

- A longitudinal analysis of 25 reports grouped into the 10th through 14th Five-Year Plans.
- A stability-based selection of 28 keywords, followed by collocation analysis and coding into economic, social, political, ecological, and cultural domains.
- An interpretive three-phase account of changing national self-presentation.

## Method

The authors lowercase and clean English reports and reference speeches, then compare each five-year GWR subcorpus with its time-aligned U.S. counterpart using AntConc 4.3.1. They retain the top 100 keywords per period and rank candidates using $S=\overline{LL}(1-CV)$, where CV measures variation in log-likelihood across periods. Keywords must occur in at least three periods; two function words are excluded, leaving 28.

Collocates require mutual information ≥ 2 and frequency ≥ 5. Concordance lines inform interpretation and independent coding by two trained coders into five policy domains; reported Cohen's κ is 0.87. Domain distributions and historical context underpin genre-level interpretation (Sections 3–5).

## Experiments

This is an observational corpus study, not an intervention. Each five-year bin contains five GWRs and five reference speeches. GWR token counts range from 68,102 to 76,774; reference counts range from 23,719 to 43,851 (Tables 1–2).

- **Lexical continuity:** development has the highest stability score, 294.35. Its collocates shift from acceleration and industrial expansion toward high-quality development.
- **Domain patterns:** economic development remains dominant; social development peaks in the 12th Five-Year Plan, while political development becomes more prominent from the 13th. Ecological and cultural domains occupy smaller shares.
- **Projected trajectory:** 2001–2010 emphasizes global integration and growth; 2011–2015 emphasizes domestic balance and stability; 2016–2025 emphasizes quality, cultural confidence, and an international solution-provider role. These phases are the authors' synthesis of lexical evidence and contextual interpretation.

## Limitations

Official English translations filter the original Chinese rhetoric. The authors acknowledge that keyword statistics can overlook historical nuance and power relations, and propose bilingual alignment and event-focused qualitative work. Reference-corpus contrasts are sensitive to institutional and stylistic differences; they do not measure foreign audience reception. Stability filtering favors recurrent vocabulary and can exclude newly emerging themes. Section 3.2.1 reports LL ≥ 3.84 alongside a Bonferroni-correction claim without explaining an adjusted threshold, leaving the significance procedure unclear.
