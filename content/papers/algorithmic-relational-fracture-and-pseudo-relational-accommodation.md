## TL;DR

Communication breakdowns with [[Relational AI|relational AI]] force users to reconcile two views of the agent: a flawed social partner and a faulty machine. In 211 public RedNote narratives, users often nested personified interpretations under technical explanations, then performed one-sided repair work ranging from system calibration to emotional distancing or renewed relational commitment. The authors model these linked processes as [[Algorithmic Relational Fracture]] and [[Pseudo-Relational Accommodation]].

## Research Question

How do users interpret and respond to communication breakdowns in intimate human-AI relationships, how do their interpretations co-occur, and how are those interpretations related to subsequent repair or accommodation strategies?

## Motivation

Research on conversational breakdowns commonly treats failure as an obstacle to task completion. That framing does not capture breakdowns in [[Relational AI]], where companionship, validation, intimacy, and partner identity can matter more than instrumental success. Interpersonal repair theories also presume reciprocal adjustment, while a non-sentient system cannot intentionally participate in relational repair. The paper therefore asks how users make sense of failures and shoulder the resulting maintenance work when reciprocity is structurally absent.

## Contributions

- Defines [[Algorithmic Relational Fracture]] (ARF) as acute sensemaking triggered when a breakdown makes an AI's simultaneous status as social partner and programmed artifact difficult to sustain.
- Shows how users form nested attributions: personified symptoms such as amnesia or stubbornness are often explained through memory limits, contextual drift, model updates, or moderation constraints.
- Defines [[Pseudo-Relational Accommodation]] (PRA) as unilateral cognitive, emotional, and behavioral adjustment, and organizes it into restorative, adaptive, and endurant forms.
- Connects attribution patterns to two broad trajectories: relational detachment ("walking away") and relational reaffirmation ("doubling down").

## Method

The authors collected public RedNote posts and comments in April 2025 using Selenium. Hashtag searches for human-AI love, romance, companionship, and partnership were expanded with breakdown-related keywords. After removing duplicates and irrelevant items, the final corpus contained 211 narratives that included both an interpretation of a breakdown and a response to it. Narratives averaged 268 words, with a reported range of 111-307 words.

Analysis proceeded in two qualitative phases. Reflexive thematic analysis identified interpretations and responses through repeated reading, memoing, theme development, investigator review, negative-case analysis, and exemplar selection. Thematic co-occurrence analysis then recorded theme presence and forcefulness for each narrative and characterized recurring relationships as sporadic or pervasive, unilateral or bilateral, and balanced or unbalanced. A second author reviewed samples of the coding and co-occurrence patterns.

## Experiments

This is a qualitative observational study rather than a controlled experiment, and it reports thematic patterns rather than effect sizes.

The interpretation analysis separated four personified shortcomings (episodic amnesia, attentional neglect, cognitive rigidity, and emotional neglect) from four technological challenges (memory limitation, contextual drift, disruptive model updates, and content moderation overreach). Users did not simply choose one frame. Cognitive rigidity was pervasively but unilaterally associated with model updates or moderation overreach, while episodic amnesia and attentional neglect were pervasively but unilaterally associated with memory limits or contextual drift. Emotional neglect was the important negative case: it showed no discernible co-occurrence with technical explanations.

Response themes comprised restorative accommodation (diagnostic probing, system calibration, and relationship retraining), adaptive accommodation (relationship downgrade, agent diversification, strategic de-anthropomorphism, and anthropomorphic parallelism), and endurant accommodation (breakdown normalization). Diagnostic probing and system calibration co-occurred across all interpretation themes. Persistent technical attributions aligned with detachment strategies, whereas personified attributions aligned with anthropomorphic reframing and renewed investment.

## Limitations

The data are retrospective public narratives rather than direct records of conversations, so they omit in-the-moment adjustments and private experiences that users did not post. Sampling is self-selected and engagement-oriented, and anonymous posts do not provide reliable demographics; linguistic cues only suggest that many contributors presented themselves as heterosexual women with male-personified AI partners. The RedNote setting also limits cultural transferability. The cross-sectional design cannot show how accommodation develops over time or whether repeated reaffirmation eventually produces burnout or detachment. Finally, the source data cannot be publicly shared because of platform terms and participant privacy.
