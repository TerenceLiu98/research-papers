## TL;DR

Using thematic analysis of ten highly engaged Bilibili videos and their comments, Guo and Yu argue that transnational bloggers and Chinese audiences jointly produce [[concepts/digital-geopolitics|Digital Geopolitics]]. Bloggers translate experiences of China into global, regional, and bodily [[concepts/geographical-imagination|Geographical Imaginations]], while audiences reinforce or contest those representations from their own [[concepts/territorial-subjectivity|Territorial Subjectivities]]. Scale is therefore both a medium of geopolitical friction and a discursive resource for attracting engagement and revenue under platform capitalism.

## Research Question

How do the territorial subjectivities of transnational bloggers and their Chinese audiences shape competing geographical imaginations on Bilibili, and how do their interactions construct digital geopolitics within a platform economy?

## Motivation

Research on foreign influencers in China has emphasized communication effectiveness, identity strategy, and their possible role in state narratives. The authors argue that this work insufficiently explains how representation is anchored in territory, how subjects are formed across real, digital, and representational spaces, or why creator-audience disagreements become geopolitical friction. Connecting these questions also exposes the commercial conditions under which emotionally resonant spatial narratives circulate.

## Contributions

- Extends digital geopolitics from statecraft and infrastructure to everyday creator-audience interactions and popular cultural production.
- Connects geographical imagination to a multi-layered and multi-scalar account of territorial subjectivity.
- Shows how apparently local experiences can be generalized into national or global comparisons, then challenged by regionally situated and embodied audience knowledge.
- Treats scale not only as an analytical hierarchy but as a collaboratively produced discursive resource that creators can reconfigure for visibility, emotional resonance, and monetization.
- Positions audience criticism as constitutive of digital geopolitics rather than as noise outside the creator's narrative.

## Method

The study examines ten videos by transnational bloggers on Bilibili, selected through snowball sampling beginning with one video and following platform recommendations. Selection favored thematic relevance, millions of views and thousands of comments, and creator diversity. The sample spans food, rural customs, markets, provincial development, public safety, urban landscapes, festivals, school lunches, waste, and climate.

The authors scraped comments, downloaded the videos, transcribed their dialogue, and conducted qualitative thematic analysis in NVivo. Coding covered geographical imagination, identity, tourist gaze, blogger-audience interaction, and blogger-local interaction. Geographical imagination was the most frequent theme. The analysis draws on all ten cases but foregrounds a British blogger's account of a rural Chongqing banquet and another blogger's night walk about public safety because these videos generated especially rich narratives and discussion.

The interpretive framework distinguishes three territorial layers (real, digital, and representational) and three scales (global, regional, and bodily). It examines how movement among these layers and scales forms subject positions and produces friction between representations.

## Experiments

This is a qualitative study rather than a controlled experiment. Its evidence shows three recurring scalar dynamics:

- **Global:** creators frequently translate situated encounters into binary comparisons between China and an undifferentiated foreign world, especially around safety, freedom, or cultural difference.
- **Regional:** commenters contest national generalizations by locating practices such as kowtowing, food preferences, prices, or climate in particular provinces, communities, or rural settings.
- **Bodily:** sensory experience and personal biography support both creator claims and audience rebuttals, making the body a site where larger territorial narratives are affirmed or challenged.

The public-safety case also illustrates strategic scalar movement: a geopolitical contrast between China and foreign countries shifts to a joke about the bodily "danger" of overeating street food. The authors interpret such reconfiguration as a way to make geopolitical content relatable and commercially effective. Comments that identify a repetitive pro-China "traffic formula" show that audiences can recognize, criticize, and further circulate this strategy.

## Limitations

- The ten videos were identified through snowball sampling, platform recommendations, and researcher judgment rather than a probability-based design.
- The sample prioritizes unusually popular videos with high engagement, so it does not establish how common the identified dynamics are across Bilibili or among less visible creators.
- Although all ten videos inform the analysis, two cases receive most of the close interpretation.
- The paper analyzes one Chinese platform and a predominantly young audience; cross-platform and international-audience comparisons remain outside its empirical scope.
- Claims about strategic scale manipulation and monetization are interpretive. The study does not directly observe creators' intentions, revenue, or algorithmic ranking mechanisms.
- The analysis of posted comments cannot determine how viewers who did not comment interpreted the content.
