## TL;DR

Positional ambiguity and policy vagueness are distinct dimensions of party communication. In Austrian manifesto data they are essentially uncorrelated, and perceived issue competence relates negatively to ambiguity but positively to vagueness.

## Research Question

Does mixing ideologically different signals differ conceptually and empirically from avoiding objectively testable policy commitments?

## Motivation

Research on position-blurring often combines positional dispersion with lack of specificity. These require different measurements: individually concrete promises can collectively point in conflicting ideological directions, while consistently left- or right-wing statements can remain noncommittal.

## Contributions

- Distinguishes aggregate positional variance from statement-level policy specificity.
- Connects party-competition research with the pledge-fulfillment literature through a measure of noncommitment.
- Demonstrates opposite associations with issue ownership, showing why a single clarity measure can obscure substantive relationships.

## Method

AUTNES coders split manifesto sentences into statements and separately code issue, position, and pledge character. The paper describes a source corpus of 34 manifestos from seven parties across nine elections during 1990–2008. The analysis covers eight socioeconomic domains: budget, economy and business, family, health, housing, labor market, pensions, and taxation. Requiring at least 30 statements per manifesto-domain yields 86 cases from 28 manifestos.

Ambiguity is the negative of Van der Eijk’s agreement measure across left, neutral, and right statements. The authors' verbal definition implies scores of −1 for complete agreement, 0 for equal shares in all three categories, and +1 for equal shares at opposing extremes. The extracted numerical example loses some minus signs; this interpretation follows the explicit instruction to multiply agreement by minus one.

Vagueness is one minus the share of statements containing objectively testable pledges. Such pledges specify actions or outcomes whose fulfillment can be judged without value judgments; objectively testable status-quo promises also qualify. Subjective promises and statements without pledges both count toward vagueness. This measures pledge-making, not fulfillment.

Issue ownership is the election-year survey share naming a party most competent in a domain. Comparable coverage is available for the SPÖ, ÖVP, FPÖ, and Greens. The analysis uses descriptive comparisons and bivariate correlations.

## Experiments

This is observational evidence, not a randomized experiment.

| Relationship | Reported result |
| --- | --- |
| Perceived competence and ambiguity | r = −0.24, p = 0.03 |
| Perceived competence and vagueness | r = 0.27, p = 0.01 |
| Ambiguity and vagueness | r = 0.01, p = 0.90 |

Opposition parties are somewhat more ambiguous, while government parties are substantially more vague. Between-party distributions overlap considerably. The authors suggest that incumbents anticipate having to deliver promises and therefore avoid commitments; this mechanism is an interpretation rather than an identified causal effect.

## Limitations

The evidence concerns Austrian socioeconomic manifesto statements and does not establish generality across countries, issues, or communication channels. Bivariate associations do not identify intentional strategy or causal effects of issue ownership and incumbency. A near-zero correlation does not establish statistical independence. The threshold excludes less-discussed domains, and manifesto content does not directly measure voter reception.
