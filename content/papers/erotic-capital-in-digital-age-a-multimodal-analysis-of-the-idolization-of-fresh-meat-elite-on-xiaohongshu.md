## TL;DR

Analyzing 64 Xiaohongshu posts about Shouzi Chew, Ge, Zheng, and Tian argue that users construct a “fresh meat elite” ideal combining business success, cultural refinement, and physical and interpersonal appeal. Women's active evaluation of men asserts agency, yet the authors interpret accompanying marriage and self-improvement discourse as reproducing class hierarchy and gender expectations.

[[index|Library home]]

Published in *Social Semiotics* 36(4), 734–757 (2026); first published online March 7, 2025. [Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10350330.2025.2473996).

## Research Question

How do Chinese female social-media users construct Chew as an idolized “fresh meat elite,” which textual and visual resources realize that image, and how does it relate to contemporary Chinese consumer culture and gender relations?

## Motivation

The authors connect Chinese wen–wu masculinity traditions, transnational business masculinity, and Pan-East Asian soft masculinity. They identify a gap in social-semiotic accounts of how women combine elite status and embodied appeal in digital representations of men. The concept of [[concepts/erotic-capital|Erotic Capital]] helps explain why attractiveness is discussed alongside education and wealth.

## Contributions

- Develops “fresh meat elite” as a hybrid masculine image joining youthful aesthetic appeal with mature commercial and cultural distinction.
- Shows how captions, photographs, gaze, framing, and romance conventions jointly construct desirability.
- Connects evaluations of Chew with representations of his spouse Vivian Kao and advice about women's advancement and partner selection.
- Interprets female agency and the reproduction of neoliberal, patriarchal expectations as coexisting features of the discourse.

## Method

The corpus comprises the top 64 posts under the Shouzi Chew topic on Xiaohongshu, collected April 7–8, two weeks after the March 23, 2023 congressional hearing. Posts were anonymized as A1–A64. This is a snapshot sample of prominent discourse.

Using [[concepts/multimodal-critical-discourse-analysis|Multimodal Critical Discourse Analysis]], the authors first apply semantic macro-analysis, open coding, and axial coding to identify themes. They then closely examine lexical choices, social-actor descriptions, visual gaze, camera angles, body language, layout, and intertextual references. Three themes organize the analysis: the combination of capitals, women's erotic gaze, and entrepreneurial feminism.

## Experiments

This is a qualitative interpretive study, with 11 numbered discourse examples and seven figures, rather than an experimental evaluation. Sections 4.1–4.3 report:

- **Combining capitals:** descriptions of physique and grooming coexist with educational credentials and corporate titles. Direct gaze, smiles, corporate settings, and references to scholarly refinement make business prestige and attractiveness mutually reinforcing.
- **Female gaze:** attention to jawlines, slimness, and clothing accompanies romantic CEO narratives. Some posts explicitly welcome applying appearance and self-management expectations to men, reversing familiar gendered scrutiny.
- **Entrepreneurial feminism:** portrayals of Kao as a high-quality partner emphasize credentials and career achievements. Advice to ordinary women advocates self-improvement and strategic partner selection, sometimes using transaction and competition language.

The authors interpret these patterns as both an assertion of women's preferences and a possible reinforcement of bodily discipline, elite marriage ideals, and individual responsibility. These are readings of representations, not measured effects on users' behavior.

## Limitations

The following scope limits follow from the reported design: one prominent figure, one platform, and a short snapshot of top posts cannot establish population-wide preferences or change over time. The paper does not report a reproducible definition of the top-post ranking, intercoder reliability statistics, or systematic verification of poster demographics. Its qualitative readings do not establish causal empowerment, audience reception, or actual marriage outcomes. The authors themselves emphasize that apparently empowering narratives may reproduce pressures around appearance, achievement, and heterosexual partnership.
