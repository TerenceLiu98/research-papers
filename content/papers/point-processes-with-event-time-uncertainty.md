## TL;DR

The paper derives a Hawkes-process model for events observed only within nonoverlapping time windows, rather than at exact timestamps. Grid-aligned, unit-width windows yield an autoregressive Bernoulli process with the point-process-derived link $\phi(x)=1-e^{-x}$ and a learnable, potentially nonstationary influence matrix or network tensor. A monotone variational-inequality estimator has an in-expectation $O(1/k)$ parameter-recovery bound under bounded-intensity and independent-trajectory assumptions. TULIK-VI gives the best reported prediction errors in simulations and the highest balanced accuracy on the supplied sepsis and Atlanta burglary tasks, though both real-data studies are small or narrowly defined and do not isolate the value of timing uncertainty from the flexible kernel.

## Research Question

How can self-exciting point processes be estimated and used for prediction when each event is localized only to a time interval, possibly together with a network node, and when the influence pattern may change over absolute time?

## Motivation

Medical measurements, diagnoses, and crime reports often locate an event within a reporting or measurement window rather than at an exact instant. Assigning such events to arbitrary timestamps discards the observation mechanism, while standard interval-censored Hawkes models target counts that may include multiple events per bin. The paper instead studies sparse observations whose ordered, nonoverlapping uncertainty windows each contain one event, then derives a computational model from the continuous-time process rather than selecting a generic discrete-time link (Sections 1 and 3).

## Contributions

- Defines a continuous-time likelihood using the filtration generated by uncertain event windows, without placing a prior distribution on the latent timestamp.
- Shows that grid-aligned windows reduce the likelihood and influence function to interval averages; unit-width windows produce a [[concepts/discrete-time-hawkes-processes|Discrete-Time Hawkes Process]] represented as an autoregressive Bernoulli model with link $1-e^{-h\Lambda_t}$.
- Learns stationary or time-varying kernels through maximum-likelihood gradient descent or a monotone variational inequality, and extends both constructions from time-only events to events on network nodes.
- Proves an in-expectation $O(1/k)$ squared-error recovery bound for the stochastic VI iteration under bounded physical intensities and independent trajectories.
- Evaluates the Time Uncertain Latent Influence Kernel (TULIK) model on synthetic recovery tasks, sepsis-associated derangements, and Atlanta burglary records.

## Method

### From Event Windows to a Bernoulli Process

Each event is known to fall in an ordered, nonoverlapping interval $\Delta_i=(t_i^l,t_i^r]$, with at most one event in a window. Under a regular grid $I_j=((j-1)h,jh]$, the method represents the continuous intensity and influence kernel by interval averages $\Lambda_j$ and $K_{i,j}$. In the unit-uncertainty case, each event window is exactly one grid interval and the data become binary observations $y_t$ with

$$
\Lambda_t=\mu+\sum_{i<t}y_iK_{i,t},
\qquad
\mathbb E[y_t\mid y_{1:t-1}]=1-e^{-h\Lambda_t}.
$$

This link follows from the survival probability of the underlying point process. The kernel may contain positive excitation and negative inhibition, provided the total conditional intensity remains positive. A finite memory horizon limits the estimated entries. A stationary kernel depends only on lag; the general kernel depends on both source and target time and can therefore represent nonstationarity (Sections 3 and 4).

### Estimation and Structure

The MLE route optimizes the Bernoulli log likelihood by gradient descent. The VI route updates parameters using prediction residuals multiplied by history features, then projects onto a feasible set. If intensities are bounded between positive constants and trajectories are independent, the population VI field is strongly monotone. With step size proportional to $1/(k+1)$, Theorem 4.5 bounds expected squared kernel error by a constant times $1/(k+1)$. The result is conditional on the physical-intensity domain and is not a finite-sample high-probability guarantee.

Time-varying kernels are rearranged into a source-time by lag matrix. Smoothness penalties and truncated SVD can regularize this representation; stationary kernels are rank one in that arrangement. In practice, a barrier discourages negative intensities rather than performing the theorem's exact projection (Sections 4 and 6).

### Network Extension

For node-labeled events, the scalar baseline becomes a node vector and the influence matrix becomes a four-way tensor indexed by source time, target time, source node, and target node. The same discretization gives a multivariate Bernoulli process, and VI or GD estimates the tensor. Learned blocks can be interpreted as time-varying predictive influences between nodes, but the fitted dependencies alone do not establish causal effects (Section 5).

## Experiments

### Synthetic Recovery

The time-only studies use 16,000 training and 500 test trajectories at grids $(N',N)=(8,32)$ and $(80,320)$. At the smaller grid, TULIK-VI reports prediction relative errors of 2.85%, 3.96%, and 6.02% in $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, and $\ell_\infty$, compared with 3.20%, 4.43%, and 6.73% for TULIK-GD. At the larger grid, VI reports 3.34%, 4.50%, and 8.64%, while the best non-TULIK values among the supplied baselines are substantially larger (Tables 1-2). These data are generated from the model family.

The five-node network simulation uses 40,000 training and 500 test trajectories. TULIK-VI has $\ell_1$ prediction error 6.36%, versus 7.47% for TULIK-GD, 9.29% for linear-link GLM, 10.29% for sigmoid-link GLM, and 31.55% for exponential Hawkes. After low-rank truncation, learned tensor blocks also recover the simulated directed-edge pattern (Table 3; Figures 8-10).

### Sepsis and Burglary

The sepsis-associated-derangements experiment represents 13 medical indices as nodes over the first 24 ICU hours, assumes one-hour uncertainty for both hourly vital signs and laboratory results, and uses 1,070 training and 71 test patients. TULIK-VI reports balanced accuracy 0.6873, compared with 0.4575 for stationary TULIK, 0.5779 for linear GLM, 0.6523 for sigmoid GLM, and 0.0096 for exponential Hawkes (Table 5).

The proprietary Atlanta data contain more than 213,000 burglary records from 2013-2019; the evaluated downtown subset is divided into 16 regions and 2,548 daily trajectories, with 2,250 used for training and 298 for testing. Balanced accuracy is 0.6707 for TULIK-VI, 0.6684 for stationary TULIK, 0.6187 for linear GLM, 0.6563 for sigmoid GLM, and 0.4633 for the Hawkes baseline (Table 6). The small gap between stationary and nonstationary TULIK contrasts with the larger sepsis gap.

## Limitations

- The implemented algorithms focus on unit-width uncertainty windows. Arbitrary grid-aligned windows are derived but left for future algorithmic development.
- Every uncertainty window is assumed to contain exactly one event and windows do not overlap. This targets sparse events and does not cover interval counts with multiple events.
- The baseline intensity is constant in time. A time-varying baseline is proposed as an extension and could otherwise be confounded with a time-varying influence kernel.
- The $O(1/k)$ theorem is an expected-error result under bounded intensities, correct specification, and independent trajectories; concentration and relaxed-dependence results are not supplied.
- Practical training replaces exact feasibility projection with a tuned barrier and adds smoothness or low-rank regularization. The theoretical guarantee does not directly analyze that full training procedure.
- Synthetic comparisons favor data generated by the proposed Bernoulli model. In the real-data comparisons, TULIK changes both the point-process-derived link and kernel flexibility, so the experiments do not separately identify the benefit of modeling timestamp uncertainty.
- The sepsis test set has only 71 patients, all eventually diagnosed with sepsis, and laboratory uncertainty is simplified to one hour despite results potentially reflecting a 24-hour condition window. The crime data are proprietary and the evaluated region and prediction protocol are narrow.
- Fitted network influences are predictive associations. Medical plausibility and recovered synthetic edges do not establish causal or Granger-causal effects in the observational applications.
