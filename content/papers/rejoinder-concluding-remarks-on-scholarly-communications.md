## TL;DR

Katz, King, and Rosenblatt argue that scholars communicating technical research to judges, officials, journalists, and the public should teach the underlying reasoning while preserving its substance. This short rejoinder offers a normative communication principle within a debate about electoral fairness, rather than new statistical results.

## Research Question

How should scholars explain sophisticated methods and theories to audiences without substantial social-science or statistical training?

## Motivation

Expert witnesses and consultants must make research accessible to people who use it in public decisions. The authors warn that removing ambiguity and adopting simple bright-line rules can discard assumptions and qualifications essential to the scholarly conclusion.

## Contributions

- Contrasts oversimplification with teaching as approaches to public explanation.
- Frames consistency between scholarly conclusions and public understanding as an academic responsibility.
- Clarifies that the authors' earlier response addressed claims in DeFord et al.'s critique, rather than other literature or other uses of their work.

## Method

The argument is conceptual and normative. The authors contrast simplifying a technical account until it would no longer meet scholarly standards with teaching audiences enough background to understand the predominant scholarly viewpoint. They invoke academics' experience teaching students and colleagues as a basis for extending this practice beyond universities.

They also state that DeFord et al.'s subsequent response did not address or contradict their corrections. This is the authors' characterization of the exchange; the rejoinder does not reproduce the disputed corrections or adjudicate them with new evidence.

## Experiments

No experiments, datasets, quantitative comparisons, or new statistical analyses are reported. The authors refer generally to their own public communication experience and that of other scholars, without presenting a systematic evaluation.

## Limitations

The rejoinder does not test whether teaching improves audience comprehension relative to simpler communication strategies. It provides a principle rather than an operational teaching protocol or criteria for deciding which simplifications preserve a conclusion. The technical electoral-fairness dispute cannot be resolved from this short text alone.
