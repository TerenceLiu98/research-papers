## TL;DR

GWMAC jointly aligns and clusters unpaired modalities by learning a weighted Gromov-Wasserstein barycenter of their kernel matrices. Modality-to-barycenter transport plans assign samples to shared clusters, while learned encoders and modality weights adapt the relational geometry. It improves on unaligned-data baselines in many reported settings, but does not dominate on every dataset.

## Research Question

Can heterogeneous modalities be clustered jointly without known sample correspondences or a requirement that their latent coordinates follow the same distribution?

## Motivation

Ordinary kernel fusion assumes aligned rows and columns. Aligning modalities first can propagate matching errors into clustering and produce inconsistent pairwise matches across multiple views. A shared structural representative provides a common destination for all modalities without selecting a reference view.

## Contributions

- A joint alignment–clustering formulation using weighted GW kernel fusion.
- A cluster-sized barycenter whose transport plans provide modality-specific sample assignments in a common cluster system.
- Alternating learning of transport plans, modality encoders, and entropy-regularized modality weights.
- Evaluation under partial and complete permutation of sample correspondences, plus unseen-data and noisy-modality experiments.

## Method

Each modality has an MLP encoder and an RBF kernel on its latent codes. The inner optimization minimizes the weighted sum of GW discrepancies between these kernels and a shared barycenter. Couplings have fixed sample and barycenter marginals; modalities may have different sample counts and feature dimensions.

The barycenter is chosen to have size equal to the predefined number of clusters, with a uniform marginal. The outer objective encourages it to resemble an identity matrix, connecting kernel fusion to modularity-based clustering. The paper evaluates GW, mean-squared-error, and cross-entropy clustering losses. A negative entropy term on the modality weights discourages collapse onto a single view while retaining the ability to downweight noise.

Training alternates conditional-gradient transport optimization and barycenter updates with stochastic-gradient encoder and weight updates; weights are projected onto the simplex. Cluster labels are obtained from the strongest sample-to-cluster transport entries. The paper reports complexity of order $O(LMB^2d)$ for barycenter iterations $L$, modalities $M$, batch size $B$, and clusters $d$.

This is structural alignment through shared clusters, rather than evidence that exact individual correspondences are uniquely recovered. See [[concepts/gromov-wasserstein-distance|Gromov-Wasserstein Distance]] and [[concepts/optimal-transport|Optimal Transport]].

## Experiments

Five datasets cover HandWritten (2,000 samples, six views), Caltech 7 (1,474, six), ORL (400, two), Movies (617, two), and Prokaryotic (551, three). Unalignment is generated by randomly permuting 50% or 100% of each modality's samples. ACC and NMI are averaged over five random seeds after hyperparameter grid search; the stated training batch size is 400.

| Caltech 7 | 0.3568 / 0.4945 | 0.3112 / 0.2456 |

| Movies | 0.1928 / 0.2138 | 0.1841 / 0.1953 |
| Prokaryotic | 0.5479 / 0.3259 | 0.4451 / 0.0554 |

At 50% unalignment, MVC-UM also beats GWMAC on ORL and on Caltech 7 ACC; CPM-GAN slightly exceeds GWMAC on Prokaryotic NMI. Aligned baselines include MCCA, DCCAE, AttnAE, MVKSC, and MultiNMF; Table 2 does not provide a separate fully aligned GWMAC row.

For generalization, the authors train on 80% unaligned data and test on 20% **well-aligned** data. GWMAC encodes the test samples, concatenates their modality codes, and applies K-means. Table 3 reports stronger ACC and NMI than MVC-UM wherever both return results, including ORL ACC of 0.5624 versus 0.2118.

Adding a random-noise modality to ORL and HandWritten causes its learned weight to decline. Entropy regularization improves the visual cluster structure, while GW and MSE losses perform comparably; cross-entropy is weaker in Table 4.

## Limitations

- The cluster count is supplied, and uniform cluster marginals impose a balanced soft-assignment structure.
- The main benchmarks simulate missing correspondences by permutations; they do not establish performance for every real-world missing-view or unequal-sample regime supported by the formulation.
- The unseen-data experiment requires aligned test views for concatenation.
- GW optimization is nonconvex, and the reported results do not establish global optimality or unique sample matching.
- Noisy-modality robustness is tested using added random noise, not arbitrary structured corruption. Partial and fused GW extensions are proposed as future work.
