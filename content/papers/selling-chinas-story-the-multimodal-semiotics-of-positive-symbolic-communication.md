## TL;DR

Through close analysis of five videos drawn from a collection of 236 YouTube videos by three Laowai Wanghong accounts, Li, Wu, and Fitzgerald show how camera work, settings, embodied performance, speech, and cultural symbols jointly portray China as technologically modern, culturally rich, and welcoming to foreigners. Their [[concepts/multimodal-critical-discourse-analysis|Multimodal Critical Discourse Analysis]] interprets these apparently personal accounts as selective symbolic communication aligned with China's national image campaign: spectacular factories, accessible traditions, and pleasant work experiences are foregrounded while structural and social tensions are largely omitted.

## Research Question

How do foreign internet celebrities in China use multimodal and symbolic resources in YouTube vlogs to construct positive representations of China for international audiences, and how do those representations relate to state-endorsed national image narratives?

## Motivation

Research on China's international image has concentrated on state media, official social accounts, and professionally produced documentaries. Work on foreign influencers has identified themes and self-presentation strategies but has paid less attention to the performances, camera choices, settings, and combinations of modes through which apparently personal experiences acquire ideological meaning. The paper addresses this gap by treating Laowai Wanghong videos as both individualized social-media content and symbolic acts situated between personal expression, platform appeal, and national strategy.

## Contributions

- Integrates symbolic communication theory with [[concepts/multimodal-critical-discourse-analysis|Multimodal Critical Discourse Analysis]] to study social-media video.
- Organizes the analysis around four dimensions: symbolic stage (setting), symbolic performance (embodied action), symbolic interpretation (speech), and symbolic gaze (camera perspective and technique).
- Identifies three recurring representational themes: modern manufacturing and technology, cultural richness and openness, and positive experiences of living and working in China.
- Explains how foreign vloggers act as witnesses and audience proxies whose reactions lend authenticity to broader claims about the nation.
- Develops the idea of a "mirror effect": content designed around presumed foreign perceptions can reproduce a curated and stereotyped image of China even while attempting to counter negative stereotypes.

## Method

The authors collected 236 YouTube videos between November 2020 and May 2023 from three China-based foreign-influencer accounts: Barrett visiting China, Jared, and Foreigners Research Association. Account selection considered residence in China, continued publication, popularity, engagement, content richness, and production quality. Online observation and inductive content analysis identified three recurrent topics: manufacturing, culture, and social life.

Five high-engagement, topic-relevant, multimodally rich videos were then selected for close analysis: one by Barrett and two each by Jared and Foreigners Research Association. The authors combine critical discourse analysis with analysis of image, sound, layout, color, speech, gesture, setting, camera angle, and perspective. Symbolic communication supplies the interpretive lens for examining how everyday scenes become signs of national values; the four-part stage-performance-interpretation-gaze framework structures the readings.

## Experiments

This is an interpretive qualitative study rather than a controlled experiment. Its five close readings support three main findings:

- **Manufacturing and technology:** selfie shots, panoramic factory views, enthusiastic reactions, and science-fiction comparisons position the foreign creator as an external witness to automation and innovation. The authors argue that concentrating on advanced, well-managed factories turns selected sites into metonyms for Chinese manufacturing while excluding less favorable labor and production conditions.
- **Culture and food:** ethnic clothing, market settings, close-ups, embodied participation, and positive tasting reactions present cultural difference as colorful, accessible, and enjoyable. Familiar comparisons help mediate unfamiliar food, but the focus on exotic dishes and spectacular traditions can flatten cultural diversity and reproduce stereotypes for foreign consumption.
- **Living and working in China:** street interviews use urban settings, humor, and minor etiquette mistakes to depict a supportive multicultural environment. A creator's short stint as a delivery rider uses first-person and observer views to frame gig work as social, free, and enjoyable, while omitting low wages, long hours, and algorithmic pressure.

Across the cases, the authors interpret the vloggers' first-person witnessing as a source of apparent authenticity. The videos invite audiences to experience China vicariously while selectively producing an image of modernity, harmony, openness, and cultural abundance consistent with the "Telling China's Story Well" campaign.

## Limitations

- The close analysis covers five purposively selected videos from three popular accounts, so it does not establish how prevalent these strategies are across foreign influencers or YouTube.
- Selection favors highly viewed, interactive, content-rich videos and therefore may overrepresent polished or spectacular examples.
- The analysis is interpretive and does not independently establish creator intentions, government direction, or funding; the paper explicitly notes that direct evidence of government control is absent.
- Audience reception is outside the study's main scope. A note mentions supportive and skeptical comments, but comments are not systematically sampled or analyzed.
- The data end in May 2023 and concern YouTube content by creators resident in China; findings may not transfer to other periods, platforms, or creator populations.
- Claims about national-image effects concern the videos' representational work rather than measured changes in international attitudes.
