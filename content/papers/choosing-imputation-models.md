## TL;DR

Compare imputed values with observed values after weighting the observed cases to balance covariates against cases with missing outcomes. Under missing at random (MAR), smaller adjusted distributional discrepancies favor an imputation model. Simulations favor hot-deck imputation, whereas an ANES application favors predictive mean matching, illustrating dataset-dependent selection.

## Research Question

How can researchers choose among imputation models for a particular dataset when point-prediction accuracy alone is insufficient?

## Motivation

Observed and missing cases can have different covariate distributions under MAR. Consequently, unadjusted differences between observed and imputed outcomes need not indicate poor imputations. Covariate adjustment makes distributional comparisons more informative.

## Contributions

- Proposes a model-comparison diagnostic based on covariate-balanced observed and imputed distributions.
- Uses stable balancing weights and several complementary discrepancy statistics.
- Illustrates the diagnostic with simulated interaction effects and mixed-scale survey data, with implementation in the R package missDiag.

## Method

Let M = 1 denote a missing outcome and M = 0 an observed outcome. Under i.i.d. sampling and MAR, the conditional distributions of Y given X coincide across missingness groups. The diagnostic approximates this comparison by weighting observed cases to match covariate moments among missing cases (§2).

Stable balancing weights minimize weight dispersion subject to nonnegative weights summing to one and covariate-mean differences within specified tolerances. Higher moments and interactions require explicit additional balancing terms. Entropy balancing is discussed as an alternative.

Compare weighted observed outcomes with imputations using standardized mean differences (SMD), log variance ratios, Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) statistics, and one minus the density overlap coefficient (1-OVL). Values nearer zero indicate greater agreement; binary outcomes require only mean comparisons. Average diagnostics over completed datasets or inspect their distributions. When covariates are themselves incomplete, balance their filled-in values and repeat diagnostics for each incomplete variable. This supports [[concepts/multiple-imputation|Multiple Imputation]] model choice but does not directly compare the full joint distribution.

## Experiments

**Simulation (§3):** 1,000 datasets each contain 1,000 observations generated by y = xz + error, with uniform x, Bernoulli z, and normal error variance one. Missingness depends on z. Each dataset receives five imputations using hot-deck, random forest, predictive mean matching, or normal-model imputation. Hot-deck and random forest yield smaller discrepancies than the additive-model alternatives. Hot-deck removes average bias in main and interaction effects; random forest retains some interaction bias.

**ANES 2008 (§4):** The application uses 2,265 respondents and 11 variables, with 100 completed datasets per method. Diagnostics cover variables with at least 25 missing values; response time is log-transformed and government support treated as continuous. Table 2 reports averages across variables and completed datasets:

| Method | SMD | log(VR) | KS | 1-OVL |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Predictive mean matching | 0.055 | 0.081 | 0.030 | 0.028 |
| Hot-deck | 0.058 | 0.085 | 0.031 | 0.030 |
| Random forest | 0.063 | 0.079 | 0.033 | 0.032 |
| Multivariate normal | 0.100 | 0.082 | 0.061 | 0.061 |

Predictive mean matching performs best on SMD, KS, and 1-OVL. Random forest has the smallest log variance ratio, computed only for continuous variables. The author reports that balancing demographic interactions leaves conclusions largely unchanged; the supplementary figures were not included in the supplied Markdown.

## Limitations

The criterion assumes MAR and i.i.d. observations; it does not establish that MAR holds or resolve nonignorable missingness. Balancing selected moments is not equivalent to matching full covariate distributions. Variable-by-variable diagnostics do not assess the full joint distribution, and filled-in covariates introduce dependence on the imputation model being assessed. Smaller discrepancies coincide with reduced regression bias in the illustrated simulation, not a general guarantee for every downstream estimand.
