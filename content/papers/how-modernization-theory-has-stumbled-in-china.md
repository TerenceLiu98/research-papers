## TL;DR

Five waves of Chinese Asian Barometer Survey data show a rise and subsequent decline in liberal-democratic orientation, with a larger reversal among highly educated, highly media-exposed respondents. The authors interpret this as state ideological control interrupting modernization's cultural effects. Their matched difference-in-differences analysis reports an approximately 14-percentage-point conditional contrast; exposure is proxied rather than directly measured.

## Research Question

Why did economic development in China fail to produce steadily increasing demand for liberal democracy, and why did cognitive elites lead the reversal after the mid-2010s?

## Motivation

Development can expand education and information access without ensuring that these channels carry pluralistic ideas. The paper asks how changing state control over those channels conditions the development–values relationship, beyond explanations based on governance performance, Confucian culture, or repression alone.

## Contributions

- Distinguishes earlier liberalization from subsequent reversal rather than treating China as a timeless exception to modernization theory.
- Identifies differential trajectories among more and less educated and media-exposed groups.
- Develops a political interception account linking those trajectories to renewed ideological control.

## Method

The study uses repeated cross-sectional ABS surveys: 2002 (3,183 respondents), 2007–08 (5,098), 2011 (3,473), 2015–16 (4,068), and 2019 (4,941). These are survey sample sizes, not final regression counts.

The binary outcome requires both a positive latent liberal-values score, estimated with a graded response model from eight items, and dissatisfaction with how democracy works in China. It therefore combines liberal orientation with regime dissatisfaction rather than measuring liberal values alone.

The treated group combines higher education with daily Internet use and daily political-news consumption. Waves 4 and 5 constitute the post-2013 period. One-to-three nearest-neighbor propensity-score matching, with and without replacement, precedes logistic difference-in-differences estimation. Covariates include age, gender, urban residence, CCP membership, ethnicity, marital status, and household size. Income is omitted because of missing data.

## Experiments

This is an observational survey analysis, not a randomized experiment.

- Descriptive liberal-democracy support declines from 40% (125/312) to 23% (192/815) among cognitive elites, compared with 21% (200/936) to 16% (379/2,445) among non-elites in the reported before/after comparison.
- Trends are roughly inverted U-shaped, with a dip in Wave 2 and a larger decline between Waves 4 and 5 than between Waves 3 and 4.
- Both matching specifications improve balance and yield similar DID results. The authors assess overlap and parallel trends graphically.
- Figure 9's conditional predictions imply an approximately 11-point decline among treated respondents against a roughly 3-point increase in controls, producing a negative DID contrast of about 14 percentage points. This illustrated contrast is conditional on selected covariate values, not an unconditional population average.
- Confidence intervals use 16,000 bootstrap replications. A further 16,000 treatment-label permutations produce placebo coefficients centered near zero; the reported original coefficients are −0.38 and −0.66 under matching with and without replacement.

## Limitations

Media use and education proxy indoctrination without measuring consumed content. All groups encounter the changing political environment, so controls represent lower exposure rather than no exposure. The design relies on comparability and counterfactual trend assumptions; matching and randomized-label placebos do not exclude differential unobserved shocks. The authors acknowledge slightly faster pre-treatment liberalization among elites.

Wave 1 has different Internet-use wording and questionable education composition. Sampling changes from household-registration-based selection to GPS-assisted area sampling in later waves. Missing income and evolving questionnaires restrict adjustment and longitudinal comparability. The outcome's dissatisfaction requirement also means changes in regime evaluations can alter measured support independently of liberal principles.
