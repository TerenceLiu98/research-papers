## TL;DR

This paper distinguishes statistical identifiability, meaning consistency across independently trained models, from structural identifiability, meaning recovery of a representation tied to an unobserved data-generating factor. It introduces epsilon-near versions of both notions, proves near-identifiability for intermediate representations under local bi-Lipschitz decoder assumptions, and shows that whitening plus independent component analysis (ICA) can reduce the remaining ambiguity to signed permutations. Experiments find improved alignment, competitive disentanglement from vanilla autoencoders, and better held-out-batch perturbation classification for cell-microscopy embeddings.

## Research Question

Under what conditions are learned representations stable across retraining, and when can that stability extend to recovery of the latent variables that generated the observations? Can ICA make these guarantees more useful for practical representation alignment and disentanglement?

## Motivation

Neural representations can be useful across many downstream tasks, yet different training runs may encode the same information in different coordinates. Existing identifiability results often concern a final linear layer or rely on strong assumptions about the data-generating process. The paper separates model-level stability from recovery of a particular latent structure and introduces an error tolerance for the near-identifiable regime relevant to finite, imperfect models.

## Contributions

- Defines statistical and structural identifiability of representation functions, including epsilon-near versions that allow bounded representation error.
- Proves that statistically identifiable end-to-end models can have intermediate representations that are statistically near-identifiable up to rigid transformations when their decoders are smooth, injective, and locally bi-Lipschitz.
- Shows that whitening and a converged contrast-based ICA procedure can reduce linear or rigid ambiguity to signed permutations while preserving near-identifiability up to new error constants.
- Extends the statistical result to structural identifiability when the data-generating process is a smooth, locally bi-Lipschitz map from independent non-Gaussian factors and the autoencoding model achieves perfect reconstruction.
- Validates the theory with MNIST autoencoders, pairs of pretrained GPT-class, masked-autoencoder, and supervised models, synthetic disentanglement benchmarks, and OpenPhenom cell-microscopy embeddings.

## Method

The paper represents a model as a loss, parameter space, and deterministic map from parameters to a representation function. Statistical epsilon-near-identifiability requires any two optimal representations to be within epsilon after a transformation from a chosen group, such as invertible linear, rigid, or signed-permutation transformations. Structural epsilon-near-identifiability instead compares every optimal representation with an unobserved target structure from the data-generating process.

The central theorem considers an encoder representation $f$ and decoder $g$. If the end-to-end map $g \circ f$ is statistically identifiable and the decoder is smooth, injective, and locally $(1+L)$-bi-Lipschitz, then $f$ is statistically near-identifiable up to a rigid transformation. The stated bound is proportional to $c_D\sqrt{2L+L^2}\Delta$, where $c_D$ depends on latent dimension and $\Delta$ bounds the latent diameter. Whitening first removes general linear scale and shear, and ICA then uses a contrast objective to resolve the remaining rigid ambiguity up to signed permutations.

For the structural result, the true data are generated by a smooth bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism from independent, non-Gaussian, standardized factors. Perfect reconstruction lets the inverse data-generating map serve as the structural target; the statistical result and ICA argument then transfer the guarantee to the learned latent representation.

## Experiments

In the MNIST warmup, three-layer autoencoders use orthogonal linear layers and LeakyReLU activations. Increasing the leak parameter drives the decoder toward a smaller bi-Lipschitz distortion and is associated with lower representation alignment error, even when reconstruction is imperfect.

For independently trained pretrained models, normalized alignment error is reported after permutation, rigid, linear, and ICA transforms. ICA reduces the error relative to permutation by 25% for Pythia-160M, 59% for the MAE pair, 38% for CheXpert, and 40% for the ResNet-18 pair. The MAE and supervised pairs exhibit the rigid alignment behavior predicted by the theory, while the GPT pair shows strong linear alignment.

On Shapes3D, MPI3D, Falcor3D, and Isaac3D, a vanilla autoencoder followed by ICA obtains aggregated InfoM, InfoE, and InfoC scores of 0.65, 0.83, and 0.40. It outperforms or matches specialized baselines on several datasets, but the gains are uneven: for MPI3D, AE + ICA and the reproduced AE have the same point estimates for InfoM and InfoE within the reported uncertainty.

At foundation-model scale, ICA is applied to OpenPhenom embeddings from Rxrx3-core. In held-out-plate perturbation classification, PCA + ICA improves AUROC over the untransformed embedding for all six reported genes, for example from 0.663 to 0.709 for CYP11B1 and from 0.803 to 0.815 for PLK1. The concentration of biological signal in the top 25% of features also rises from 0.163 for the base embedding to 0.386 after PCA + ICA; a random rotation reaches 0.287.

## Limitations

The local bi-Lipschitz assumptions are difficult to test empirically, so the experiments assess predicted downstream consequences rather than directly verifying the theorem's hypotheses. The theory is stated in an idealized infinite-data and optimization regime and does not treat finite samples or imperfect optimization. Structural identifiability additionally depends on strong assumptions about the data-generating process, independent non-Gaussian factors, sufficient model capacity, and perfect reconstruction. ICA can still be affected by model misspecification, optimization quality, covariance conditioning, and its irreducible signed-permutation ambiguity. The microscopy application improves the reported held-out-batch classification metrics, but does not by itself prove that every separated component corresponds to a biologically meaningful factor.
