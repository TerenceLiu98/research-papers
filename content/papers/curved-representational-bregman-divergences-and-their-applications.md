## TL;DR

The paper treats divergences on nonlinear parameter subsets as [[concepts/curved-bregman-divergences|Curved Bregman Divergences]]. Its central theorem reduces a constrained right centroid to a Bregman projection of the ordinary weighted mean. Monotonic representations extend this viewpoint to [[concepts/alpha-divergences|Alpha Divergences]], enabling a geometric construction for intersections of divergence spheres. The evidence consists of proofs, worked examples, and geometric illustrations rather than benchmark experiments.

## Research Question

How can Bregman geometry describe divergences restricted to nonlinear parameter spaces, and which centroid and sphere-intersection constructions survive these restrictions and changes of representation?

## Motivation

Probability normalization, statistical submodels, and nonlinear feature maps constrain parameters to subsets of a larger space. A divergence can retain an ambient Bregman representation even when it is not an ordinary Bregman divergence in its original coordinates. Making the embedding explicit allows centroid identities and geometric lifting constructions to be reused.

## Contributions

- Distinguishes nonlinear restrictions, called curved Bregman divergences, from affine restrictions, called sub-dimensional Bregman divergences; derives reparameterizations on simplicial domains (Section 2.2).
- Proves that a constrained right centroid is a right Bregman projection of the ambient mean, including a pointwise density version (Theorems 1 and 2).
- Represents symmetrized Bregman divergences in a joint primal-dual space and interprets generalized left centroids using a weighted product-space generator (Sections 2.4 and 2.8).
- Describes symmetric Jensen approximations to Jeffreys-Bregman centroid objectives using convex-concave optimization (Section 3).
- Represents alpha divergences through monotonic embeddings and uses lifted hyperplane intersections to characterize intersections of alpha-divergence spheres (Propositions 4 and 6).

## Method

For a differentiable strictly convex potential on a convex ambient domain,

$$
B_F(x:y)=F(x)-F(y)-\langle x-y,\nabla F(y)\rangle.
$$

Restricting both arguments to an embedded set gives $D(u:v)=B_F(\theta(u):\theta(v))$. A nonlinear embedding generally does not yield the ordinary Bregman divergence generated by $F\circ\theta$. For example, $F(x)=\tfrac12\|x\|^2$ restricted to the unit circle gives $1-\cos(u-v)$, although $F\circ\theta$ is constant. An injective affine parameterization instead yields a Bregman divergence with the restricted generator in intrinsic coordinates.

For nonnegative weights summing to one, write $\bar\theta=\sum_i w_i\theta_i$. Theorem 1 follows from

$$
\sum_i w_i B_F(\theta_i:y)
=\sum_i w_i B_F(\theta_i:\bar\theta)+B_F(\bar\theta:y).
$$

Thus minimizing over $y=\theta(u)$ amounts to projecting $\bar\theta$ onto the admissible set with the candidate in the second argument. The projection penalty measures the extra loss imposed by the constraint. The pointwise density analogue projects the mixture $\bar p=\sum_i w_i p_i$ onto a chosen density family. The paper also relates curved exponential-family maximum likelihood to projection of empirical sufficient statistics in dual coordinates.

For Legendre-type $F$, embed $x$ as $\xi(x)=(x,\nabla F(x))$ and use the ambient potential $G(a,b)=F(a)+F^*(b)$. Then

$$
B_G(\xi(x):\xi(y))=B_F(x:y)+B_F(y:x).
$$

This makes symmetrized centroid estimation another constrained projection problem. Examples include Jeffreys centroids, scalar Itakura-Saito centroids, and log-determinant divergences on positive-definite matrices. Section 2.6 also studies symmetrized divergences augmented by quadratic terms; suitable weights admit a Euclidean feature-space representation.

For alpha divergences, the construction first changes representation on the positive measure cone and then restricts to the embedded probability simplex. A right Bregman sphere $B_F(x:c)=r$ lifts to the intersection of the graph $z=F(x)$ with

$$
z=F(c)+\langle x-c,\nabla F(c)\rangle+r.
$$

Section 5 intersects these supporting hyperplanes, intersects the resulting affine set with the potential graph, and projects back into representation coordinates. For probability distributions, the result must also lie on the represented simplex; applying the inverse representation returns probability coordinates. Left alpha spheres can be handled by reversing the sign of alpha. This is a geometric characterization, with feasibility and degeneracies still requiring attention.

## Experiments

There are no dataset benchmarks, timing comparisons, or statistical performance tests. Figures illustrate the circle example, categorical centroids for sets of two and eight distributions, scalar Itakura-Saito centroids, matrix centroids, and lifted sphere geometry. The appendices supply Maxima snippets illustrating the circle identity and the two-point scalar Itakura-Saito case; these snippets were not executed during ingestion. Section 3 proposes iterative centroid approximations without a numerical convergence or runtime evaluation.

## Limitations

- Curved projections need not be unique. With an ambient mean at the center of a unit circle, every circle point minimizes the squared-Euclidean projection objective (Remark 1). The reduction does not itself supply a general global optimizer or ensure existence on arbitrary subsets.
- The dual constructions assume suitable Legendre generators and admissible domains. Affine simplex reparameterization needs independent coordinates; normalization and representation constraints cannot be discarded.
- Sphere intersections can be empty, tangent, coincident, or otherwise degenerate. The generic dimension discussion should not be read as an unconditional nonempty-intersection guarantee.
- The supplied Markdown has inconsistent notation and formulas, particularly in the alpha potentials, convex-concave updates, and matrix examples. Those expressions are not reproduced as implementation-ready recipes. In Section 2.4.2, $\sqrt{ah}$ is the geometric mean of the arithmetic and harmonic centroids, not generally the weighted geometric mean of all input values.
- The year 2025 is supported by the same-title conference entry in reference 39 (International Conference on Geometric Science of Information, pages 131-140). The supplied manuscript's own revision date and stable identifier are not given.
