## TL;DR

Zaks evaluates four promises of explicit Bayesian process tracing: systematic iteration, timing-independent inference, engagement with rival explanations, and protection against cognitive bias. She argues that the contemporary implementation guidance does not deliver these promises reliably, especially when hypotheses evolve or causal explanations overlap. The article values Bayesian updating as a framework for iterative inquiry while calling for clearer procedures and evidence of practical benefits.

## Research Question

Do the inferential and transparency claims made for explicit Bayesian process tracing withstand scrutiny of its practical research procedures?

## Motivation

Bayesian process tracing had gained prominence in qualitative-methods publications and training. Its proposed benefits therefore warrant evaluation against implementation difficulties and the opportunity costs of methodological training, including time otherwise available for interviewing, archival research, and language skills.

## Contributions

- Separates updating confidence in fixed hypotheses from revising the hypotheses themselves.
- Examines timing across hypothesis generation, refinement, testing, and reporting.
- Identifies risks of interpreting coexisting causal explanations as exclusive competitors.
- Distinguishes ad hoc tailoring from complexity and questions whether prior penalties adequately address either.
- Proposes a research agenda on coherent updating, evidence chronology, probability elicitation, and relationships among alternatives.

## Method

The article critically examines methodological prescriptions, particularly those of Fairfield and Charman and Bennett, using conceptual analysis and illustrative examples. The organizing equation expresses posterior odds as prior odds multiplied by the likelihood ratio. Zaks focuses on how researchers specify and revise its inputs, rather than presenting a new estimator.

For iteration, Section 2 asks how to reuse evidence after changing a hypothesis, how to carry posterior information into subsequent updates, and how to compare more than two hypotheses. For timing, Section 3 argues that costly evidence search affects which hypotheses researchers initially consider. Additional evidence can reveal whether a refined hypothesis remains stable beyond the observations that inspired it. Mathematical order invariance does not establish that human probability judgments are free of order effects; reporting chronology can help readers assess these judgments.

Section 4 uses greed and grievance as potentially simultaneous motivations for rebellion. Zaks argues that treating them as exclusive explanations neglects their overlap and risks mistaking support for one cause for disconfirmation of another. These objections concern hypothesis specification and interpretation in the reviewed implementations; they should not be read as a general invalidation of Bayes' rule for overlapping events.

Section 5 questions whether instructions to use all relevant information actually prevent biased judgments. Penalizing complex hypotheses also requires a defensible complexity measure and penalty scale, while an explanation tailored to particular observations needs scrutiny with additional evidence.

## Experiments

No new empirical experiment or quantitative benchmark is reported. The evidence consists of analysis of published methodological recommendations and illustrative reasoning, including costly hypothesis search, repeated theory refinement, and overlapping rebellion motivations. The article proposes experimental checks of whether researchers with similar background information assign similar probabilities and evaluate others' assignments consistently; it does not report conducting those checks.

## Limitations

- The critique principally targets explicit numerical procedures and claims in the literature available at publication, rather than every possible Bayesian research design.
- The article diagnoses plausible failure mechanisms without estimating their prevalence or measuring comparative performance against other qualitative methods.
- Claims about cognitive bias, training costs, and superior alternative investments are arguments requiring further empirical assessment.
- Footnote markers and references survive in the supplied Markdown, but the corresponding footnote text and supplementary material are absent. Some equations contain extraction artifacts; the summary relies on the surrounding prose.
