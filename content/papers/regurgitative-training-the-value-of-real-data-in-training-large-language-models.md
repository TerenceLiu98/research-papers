## TL;DR

Across machine translation and question answering, models trained or fine-tuned on LLM-generated targets generally underperform counterparts trained on curated human data. Translation analyses associate the gap with both errors and reduced lexical diversity. Ranking synthetic examples by estimated quality, mixing outputs from multiple models, and prioritizing outputs that resemble human data each help in some settings, but none closes the real-data gap, even with much larger samples. Product ideation gives a more conditional result: human-preference fine-tuning improves the targeted quality or originality metric, while degrading the other metric.

## Research Question

How does training a language model on data generated by itself or other language models affect downstream performance, what properties of synthetic data help explain the effect, and how much can data-selection or human-feedback strategies mitigate it?

## Motivation

As LLM-generated text enters public and proprietary corpora, future models will increasingly train on synthetic content whether by design or through contamination. Synthetic data are inexpensive and scalable, but their errors and distributional regularities may be inherited by later models. The paper calls this setting [[concepts/regurgitative-training|Regurgitative Training]] and tests it in both fine-tuning and training-from-scratch regimes.

## Contributions

- Compares real-data training with self-generated and cross-model synthetic-data training across commercial LLM fine-tuning and small transformers trained from scratch.
- Extends the evaluation across objective tasks (German-English translation and question answering) and a subjective product-ideation task evaluated by people.
- Provides empirical evidence that translation errors and lower lexical diversity both plausibly contribute to the performance gap.
- Evaluates quality ranking, multi-model data mixtures, human-resemblance ranking through AI detectors, and a hybrid strategy.
- Separates response data from preference data, showing that human preferences can improve a selected subjective outcome even when the candidate content is model-generated.

## Method

### Objective Tasks

The main fine-tuning experiment uses 1,908,920 cleaned German-English pairs from Europarl. GPT-3.5 is progressively fine-tuned in five batches of 1,000 examples using human reference translations or translations from GPT-3.5, GPT-4, or LLAMA2, then evaluated on 10,000 held-out pairs. The authors repeat the design with GPT-4o-mini and newer generators, scale selected conditions to 100,000, 250,000, and 500,000 examples, and train smaller transformers from scratch for translation and question answering. Translation is evaluated primarily with BLEU, with BLEURT and COMET robustness checks.

To study mechanisms, the paper compares generated translations with references using embedding cosine similarity, nonsynonymous token deviations, unique-token counts, and Self-BLEU. It then tests three mitigation families: rank examples by predicted task quality; mix outputs from different generators; or rank generated examples by an AI detector's predicted probability of being human-written.

### Subjective Task

For product ideation, 200 U.S. participants each produce one retail-product idea for college students, and GPT-4o produces another 200. Independent workers rate every idea 20 times for purchase intention (quality) and novelty (originality). GPT-4o is trained by supervised fine-tuning on either human or model ideas, followed by Direct Preference Optimization using 500 pairs with the largest human-rated differences for either quality or originality.

## Experiments

In the 5,000-example translation study, training on human references clearly outperforms every synthetic source. GPT-4-generated targets yield only a small gain over the GPT-3.5 baseline, self-generated GPT-3.5 targets largely stall learning, and LLAMA2 targets reduce performance. The generators' target quality follows the same order: reported BLEU is 0.3454 for GPT-4, 0.3428 for GPT-3.5, and 0.2417 for LLAMA2. The gap persists with newer flagship generators, alternative translation metrics, training from scratch, question answering, and samples as large as 500,000.

Generated translations differ from human references in both accuracy and variety. Average embedding similarity to references is 0.8059 for GPT-4, 0.8047 for GPT-3.5, and 0.7506 for LLAMA2; corresponding nonsynonymous deviations total 21,619, 21,836, and 27,962. Human translations contain 14,604 unique tokens, versus 13,731, 13,690, and 13,081, and have lower Self-BLEU (0.1048 versus 0.1126-0.1154), consistent with greater lexical diversity.

All three mitigation strategies sometimes improve synthetic-data training but remain below human-data training. Quality ranking gives small gains in commercial fine-tuning and is the strongest strategy in the large-sample comparison. Mixing helps when generators have comparable quality and complementary diversity, but adding a weaker generator can offset that benefit. Human-resemblance ranking works best when the detector can reliably distinguish the generator's output. The quality-plus-mixture hybrid leads only in the first batch and then loses its advantage.

In product ideation, GPT-4o ideas receive higher mean quality ratings than human ideas (3.101 versus 2.901) but lower originality ratings (2.527 versus 2.933). Supervised effects are small and statistically nonsignificant. Preference tuning raises its focal metric: for example, GPT-DPO-Quality reaches quality 3.266 but originality 2.235, while GPT-DPO-Originality reaches originality 2.630 but quality 2.821. The reported cross-metric losses show that improvement is preference-specific rather than uniformly beneficial.

## Limitations

- The proposed mechanisms are supported by empirical associations; the paper does not identify a complete causal or theoretical account of how errors and lexical diversity affect trained models.
- Most detailed experiments concern translation, and the from-scratch models are much smaller than frontier systems.
- Synthetic data quality is task- and generator-dependent, so the results do not imply that model-generated data are uniformly inferior for every target or training design.
- BLEU prediction and AI-detection ranking depend on supervised proxies whose accuracy limits mitigation, especially as generated text becomes harder to distinguish from human text.
- The product-ideation study uses one prompt domain, 200 source ideas per condition, crowd ratings, and single-objective preference datasets. Its quality-originality tradeoff may not generalize to other subjective tasks or multi-objective alignment methods.
- Human-generated data are treated as real data but are not assumed to be error-free. The relevant comparison is with curated organic data in the tested settings.

## Related Concepts

- [[concepts/regurgitative-training|Regurgitative Training]]: training on content produced at least partly by the same or other generative models.
- [[Instruction-Tuning Data Diversity]]: a broader account of why task-relevant informational variety, not merely dataset size, can matter for fine-tuning.
- [[concepts/curse-of-ambiguity|Curse of Ambiguity]]: another mechanism through which synthetic generations may progressively lose distributional support and diversity.
- Direct Preference Optimization: the preference-based fine-tuning method used in the product-ideation study.
- Model collapse: the related recursive-training failure mode emphasized by prior work.
