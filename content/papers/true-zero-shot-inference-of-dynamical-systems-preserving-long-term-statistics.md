## TL;DR

DynaMix combines ten small recurrent experts with context-dependent gating to reconstruct unseen dynamical systems without target-system fine-tuning. Trained on 34 synthetic systems, it reports stronger preservation of attractor geometry and power spectra than the tested time-series foundation models across 54 held-out systems. Its approximately 10,000 parameters also support efficient inference. Short-term forecasting gains are mixed, and nonstationarity, sampling resolution, and observation embeddings remain important constraints.

## Research Question

Can a pretrained generative model infer an unseen system from a context trajectory while preserving its long-term dynamical statistics, without retraining on that system?

## Motivation

[[concepts/dynamical-systems-reconstruction|Dynamical Systems Reconstruction]] requires more than accurate near-term predictions. Chaotic trajectories diverge even when generated by the same equations, so long-horizon pointwise errors cannot alone judge reconstruction. Conversely, repeating the observed context can resemble an attractor while producing periodic rather than chaotic dynamics. The paper asks whether pretraining can transfer the dynamical fidelity of specialized reconstruction models to new systems.

## Contributions

- Introduces a multivariate mixture of Almost-Linear RNNs (AL-RNNs), conditioned on both context observations and the current latent state.
- Combines synthetic pretraining with sparse teacher forcing to support autonomous, zero-shot generation at test time.
- Evaluates spatial occupation distributions, temporal spectra, short-term errors, efficiency, and component ablations.
- Uses expert-weight distributions to compare dynamical regimes and demonstrates extensions to partial observations, empirical series, and higher observation dimensions.

## Method

**Recurrent experts and gating.** Each expert applies

$$
F_j(z_t)=A_jz_t+W_j\Phi^*(z_t)+h_j,
\qquad
z_{t+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}w_{j,t}^{\mathrm{exp}}F_j(z_t).
$$

The default uses $J=10$ experts, each with 30 latent coordinates and ReLU activation on only two coordinates. The first observation-sized block supplies the readout. A CNN extracts temporal context features; state-dependent attention weights those features; an MLP and softmax produce expert weights. This is a weighted combination of expert transitions, not the top-k token routing used in sparse transformer MoEs. Context length is flexible, while observation dimension is fixed by the chosen architecture.

**Training.** Approximately 600,000 length-550 sequences are simulated from 34 three-dimensional chaotic or cyclic systems, standardized by dimension, and augmented with Gaussian noise at 5% of the data standard deviation. Training uses a 500-step context with a 50-step overlap between context and rollout, observation MSE, and a regularizer encouraging attention exploration noise. Sparse teacher forcing replaces observed latent coordinates with data every ten steps, after loss evaluation, to control gradient growth while allowing free evolution between corrections. It is disabled at test time. Appendix A.1 specifies RAdam, 2,000 epochs, and CPU training.

**Observation embeddings.** Partially observed Lorenz-63 uses delay coordinates. Empirical univariate series use sinusoidal auxiliary coordinates with a period estimated from autocorrelation. Appendix A.6 presents two distinct higher-dimensional protocols: forecast two observed Lorenz-96 coordinates with the original model and subsequently delay-embed the output, or enlarge the gating/readout architecture and retrain on nonlinear embeddings of the original 34 training systems before forecasting all six Lorenz-96 coordinates. The latter is still zero-shot with respect to Lorenz-96, but uses a separately trained model.

**Dynamical similarity.** The authors compare standardized expert-weight distributions with squared 2-Wasserstein distance and transform it to $S=1/(1+W_2^2)$. Clusters suggest shared dynamical regimes; this is an empirical representation-based similarity, not proof of dynamical equivalence.

The source provides [Julia code](https://github.com/DurstewitzLab/DynaMix-julia) and [Python code](https://github.com/DurstewitzLab/DynaMix-python).

## Experiments

**Protocol.** The principal benchmark contains 54 unseen three-dimensional systems. Long rollouts of 10,000 steps are evaluated using state-space occupation KL divergence $D_{\mathrm{stsp}}$ and average Hellinger distance between normalized, smoothed power spectra $D_H$. Lower is better. Ten-step MASE assesses short-term accuracy. Main comparisons use 2,000 context steps, with a 512-step sensitivity analysis. Comparators include Chronos variants, Chronos-2, Panda, Mamba4Cast, Tiny Time Mixers, and TimesFM. The authors note that Panda's pretraining includes variations of test systems.

**Long-term reconstruction.** Figures 4 and 23 report aggregate advantages over the tested foundation models on both dynamical metrics, assessed with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and competitive short-term forecasts. The supplied large per-system tables have malformed headers and incomplete rows, so exact aggregate baseline scores are not reconstructed from them. Figure 6's Lorenz-63 example estimates maximum Lyapunov exponents of approximately 0.96 for DynaMix, 0.87 for ground truth, and 0.02 for Chronos, illustrating how context repetition can conceal loss of chaos.

**Custom-trained comparison.** Table 6 reports the following medians and median absolute deviations across 54 systems. These baselines train directly on each target context, unlike DynaMix.

| Model | $D_{\mathrm{stsp}}$ | $D_H$ | MASE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DynaMix | 3.80 ± 1.40 | 0.16 ± 0.06 | 0.35 ± 0.17 |
| AL-RNN | 3.86 ± 2.48 | 0.08 ± 0.06 | 0.47 ± 0.23 |
| Neural ODE | 3.43 ± 2.05 | 0.09 ± 0.04 | 0.18 ± 0.13 |
| Reservoir computer | 1.33 ± 0.99 | 0.08 ± 0.07 | 0.86 ± 0.37 |

DynaMix is competitive without target training, but does not dominate specialized models: all three have lower median spectral distance, and the reservoir has substantially lower spatial divergence. For six-dimensional Lorenz-96, Table 2 reports DynaMix scores of 1.93 ± 0.08, 0.07 ± 0.00, and 1.02 ± 0.06 across five trajectories with 4,000-step contexts; all three metrics improve on the listed foundation-model baselines under that protocol.

**Empirical transfer.** Table 1 evaluates ETT, traffic, cloud requests, weather, fMRI, and EEG despite using no empirical pretraining data. DynaMix's fMRI scores are $D_{\mathrm{stsp}}=0.17$, $D_H=0.09$, and MAE 0.45, compared with Chronos-t5-base's 5.78, 0.12, and 0.85. Traffic reverses this ordering: DynaMix gives 0.81, 0.21, and 6.93 versus Chronos's 0.40, 0.10, and 3.16. ETTh1 also favors TimesFM for MAE, 3.04 versus 4.92, despite DynaMix's stronger dynamical metrics. Empirical forecast horizons vary by dataset from 40 to 200 steps, so their raw MAEs are not comparable across datasets.

**Ablations and efficiency.** Removing attention, CNN processing, sparse teacher forcing, training-system diversity, or sufficient experts degrades reconstruction. Replacing the gating MLP with a linear operation has less effect. Other piecewise-linear recurrent experts remain competitive; LSTM, vanilla RNN, and reservoir experts perform worse in the tested setup. Figure 5 reports much lower parameter counts and faster 10,000-step generation under matched hardware. Exact timing ratios are not recoverable from its text caption.

## Limitations

- Evidence centers on low-dimensional, mostly stationary systems. Sharp peaks, mixed time scales, unsuitable sampling intervals, and incorrect embeddings cause failures. The Air Passengers preprocessing example is only a proof of concept for nonstationarity.
- Arbitrary context length does not imply unrestricted input dimensionality. Full six-channel inference uses the architectural expansion and retraining described above; irregular sampling remains future work.
- Matching occupation distributions and spectra supplies complementary evidence of reconstruction, but does not uniquely identify governing equations. The Lorenz-63 Lyapunov comparison is an illustrative case.
- Baseline rankings depend on the target and metric. Empirical forecasting and custom-trained reconstruction results do not support universal superiority.
- The supplied Markdown gives no explicit publication year, venue, DOI, or arXiv identifier. Its final comparison tables are incomplete, and Figure 7's reference to 84 systems differs from the stated 34 training plus 54 test systems. These details are left unresolved.
