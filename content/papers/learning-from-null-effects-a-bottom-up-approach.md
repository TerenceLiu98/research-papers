## TL;DR

The authors propose brief public null-results reports, supported by lab and departmental reporting norms, to make unpublished findings available for cumulative research. A diagnostic template and two experimental illustrations show what these reports can contain; the paper does not experimentally evaluate whether the proposed norm changes reporting behavior.

## Research Question

How can research communities make null findings visible when publication incentives discourage writing them up and preregistration alone does not ensure results disclosure?

## Motivation

Selective visibility can inflate apparent effects, impair evidence synthesis, and waste resources on studies that could have learned from unpublished work. In an October 2020 snapshot of 75 AEA-registered nudge studies, 40 had no public working paper or publication, 16 had publications, and 19 had working papers. None of the published papers and only one working paper reported primarily null findings. Missing results are not themselves proof of null effects: some studies may have been unfinished.

## Contributions

- Proposes local research-community standards as a route toward discipline-wide reporting norms.
- Supplies a structured approach to [[concepts/null-results-reporting|Null Results Reporting]], including explanations for inconclusive findings.
- Illustrates the approach with naturalization and refugee-attitude experiments.
- Distinguishes design transparency from disclosure of completed study results.

## Method

Labs, departments, and methodological communities would encourage or require short reports on studies that researchers cannot or do not wish to publish through peer review. The Immigration Policy Lab describes adopting a requirement for its preregistered studies. Public preprints and repositories make reports accessible and citable; registries and funders could reinforce the norm through reporting requirements.

The template summarizes design and findings, then examines five possible explanations: statistical power, measurement strategy, implementation problems, spillover or contamination, and flaws in theoretical priors. It concludes with implications for future designs and theory. Reusing preregistration and analysis materials is intended to reduce writing costs (Section 4).

## Experiments

These are applications of the reporting template, not tests of the proposed institutional reform (Section 5).

| Application | Intervention and comparison | Reported result and diagnostic lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naturalization in New York | Low-income lawful permanent residents received enhanced information, or enhanced information plus citizenship-workshop scheduling, compared with a simple information nudge. | Neither enhancement discernibly increased citizenship applications. Small meaningful effects might have escaped detection; workshop appointments offered only one location and date. Substantial paperwork and language barriers could limit the intervention. |
| Attitudes toward Syrian refugees in Jordan | Text and approximately two-minute videos emphasized refugee needs, gratitude toward hosts, or Islamic hospitality; controls proceeded directly to outcome questions. | No statistically significant effects across outcomes. The gratitude treatment may have had a small effect requiring a larger sample. Enumerator reports of boredom suggest delivery problems; familiar frames and relatively positive baseline attitudes may also limit change. |

Across the broader AEA registry, only 20 of 3,989 studies (0.5%) included preliminary results reports other than papers, and 394 (10%) linked working papers or publications as of October 2020. This describes reporting visibility, not the prevalence of completed null studies.

## Limitations

The proposal's effectiveness and uptake are not causally established. Time and reputational costs may persist, while dispersed reports may be hard to discover. Nonsignificance does not establish a zero effect: power, delivery, measurement, and context remain competing explanations. The supplied article summarizes the two experiments without their full sample sizes, estimates, or uncertainty intervals; it refers readers to supplementary reports not included in the supplied Markdown.
