## TL;DR

A joint distribution of node degree and metadata extends network percolation to feature-dependent removal. Generating-function predictions agree well with configuration-model simulations and several dynamics-derived applications, while spatial correlations produce systematic errors. Ignoring degree–feature dependence can either overestimate or underestimate connectivity after intervention.

## Research Question

How can the surviving giant component and percolation threshold be predicted when node removal depends jointly on topology and non-topological attributes?

## Motivation

Age, collaborative editing, misinformation activity, spatial proximity, and dynamical states can motivate interventions that degree-only attacks miss. The paper illustrates heterogeneous degree–feature relationships using board directors, Wikipedia articles, and Twitter users; these examples motivate the model rather than establish intervention effects.

## Contributions

- Generalizes configuration-model site percolation through a joint degree–feature distribution and occupation rule.
- Demonstrates nonmonotonic robustness responses and threshold-dependent effects of degree–feature correlations.
- Applies the framework to spatial features and to features generated by network dynamics, estimating unavailable joint distributions with a Bayesian machine scientist.

## Method

Let $P(k,\mathbf F)$ describe degree and features, and let $\phi_{k,\mathbf F}$ be the probability that a node remains. Define

$$
g_0(z)=\int d\mathbf F\sum_k P(k,\mathbf F)\phi_{k,\mathbf F}z^k,
\qquad
g_1(z)=\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle}\int d\mathbf F\sum_{k\geq1}kP(k,\mathbf F)\phi_{k,\mathbf F}z^{k-1}.
$$

The edge nonconnection probability satisfies $u=1-g_1(1)+g_1(u)$, and the giant component occupies fraction $S=g_0(1)-g_0(u)$ of the original nodes. The continuous onset satisfies $g_1'(1)=1$ (Eqs. 1–5). Constant occupation recovers random failures; degree or feature thresholds encode targeted removal. Feature dimensions do not introduce additional connectivity types.

For dynamics-derived features, the authors approximate the joint distribution with degree-dependent Gaussian profiles. Bayesian symbolic fitting supplies their means, widths, and peak heights; normalization and numerical integration then produce the percolation curve.

## Experiments

- **Independent features:** geometric degree and power-law feature distributions yield close agreement at $N=3000$, averaged over 100 realizations (Fig. 2). The studied example retains mean-field order-parameter exponents of one and finite-size scaling consistent with $\bar\nu=3$.
- **Correlated features:** positive and negative degree–feature models are compared with shuffled counterparts at $N=2^{16}$ over 100 realizations (Fig. 3). Positive correlation lowers robustness under removal of high-feature nodes. For negative correlation, the direction of the discrepancy depends on the removal threshold.
- **Spatial features:** random geometric graphs use nearest-neighbor distance as the feature and synchronously remove nodes whose nearest neighbor is too close. Theory overestimates giant-component size at low density; discrepancies decline as node count increases at fixed connection radius (Fig. 4).
- **Dynamical features:** stationary SIS infection probabilities on a reshuffled Barabási–Albert network ($N=2000$, $m=3$) give a reasonably accurate fitted prediction with deviations near criticality (Fig. 5). Mutualistic and birth–death dynamics on plant–pollinator projections and biochemical dynamics on the C. elegans interactome extend the comparison to empirical topologies (Fig. 6). Birth–death predictions slightly underestimate both threshold and giant-component size.

## Limitations

The ensemble approximation does not capture clustering or general topological correlations through $P(k,\mathbf F)$ alone. Spatial discrepancies therefore are not simply finite-size effects. Gaussian feature profiles and fitted functional forms add approximation error. The demonstrations mostly use scalar features and threshold rules, despite the general vector formulation. Connectivity after removal is the evaluated outcome; it does not establish recovery of the underlying dynamics. Discontinuous transitions, multilayer extensions, bond interventions, and optimal dismantling are proposed directions rather than demonstrated results.
