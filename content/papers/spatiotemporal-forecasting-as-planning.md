## TL;DR

SFP reframes physical spatiotemporal forecasting as planning: a forecasting backbone proposes a latent action, a conditional vector-quantized [[concepts/world-models|World Model]] generates candidate futures, and beam search selects candidates using a non-differentiable scientific metric. The selected future becomes a pseudo-label for differentiable policy training. Across weather, fluid, and combustion benchmarks, the paper reports improvements in both pixel-level error and domain metrics, with the main trade-off being substantially higher beam-search inference latency.

## Research Question

Can a forecasting model optimize non-differentiable domain metrics, such as the Critical Success Index or turbulent kinetic energy spectrum error, by planning over futures generated by a learned probabilistic dynamics model?

## Motivation

Standard forecasting systems usually train with differentiable proxy losses such as mean squared error, although scientific evaluation may instead emphasize thresholded extreme-event skill, physical spectra, or structural similarity. This mismatch can reward average-looking predictions while missing rare events or physically important fine-scale structure. The proposed [[concepts/spatiotemporal-forecasting-as-planning|Spatiotemporal Forecasting as Planning]] formulation turns the evaluation metric into a reward over generated futures and then distills the preferred futures back into the forecasting policy.

## Contributions

- Formalizes physical forecasting as a model-based reinforcement-learning problem in which the forecast model is a policy and its output conditions a generative world model.
- Builds a conditional VQ-VAE world model whose codebook supports multiple plausible future states conditioned on both history and the policy's latent action.
- Uses beam search to evaluate imagined futures with a non-differentiable domain metric, then uses the highest-reward candidate as a pseudo-label for ordinary gradient-based policy updates.
- Evaluates the framework with ten forecasting backbones and five benchmark families, including extreme weather, marine heatwaves, PDE dynamics, and combustion simulation.

## Method

SFP has two separately trained stages. First, a conditional VQ-VAE learns a distribution over future physical states. Its encoder quantizes a ground-truth future into a learned codebook, while its decoder reconstructs that future from the quantized representation and a condition extracted from the observed history. Training combines reconstruction, codebook, and commitment losses. The trained world model is then frozen.

In the second stage, an existing forecasting backbone acts as a policy. Given the current state, it emits a high-dimensional latent action that conditions the frozen world model. Beam search expands candidate latent sequences and retains promising generated futures. A domain metric scores the completed candidates; the maximum-reward future is treated as a pseudo-label. A lightweight projector maps the policy action into physical state space, where an MSE loss against that pseudo-label updates the policy. The paper calls this use of computable metric feedback **reinforcement learning from metric feedback**, although the implemented policy update is supervised self-training on planner-selected targets rather than a conventional policy-gradient update.

The reported default uses a 1,024-entry codebook and beam width 10. Candidate trajectories can also form a probabilistic forecast ensemble evaluated with CRPS.

## Experiments

The evaluation covers SEVIR and a marine-heatwave dataset, Navier-Stokes equations (NSE), shallow-water equations (SWE), Rayleigh-Benard convection (RBC), and the Prometheus combustion simulator. Backbones include ResNet, ConvLSTM, Earthformer, SimVP-v2, TAU, Earthfarseer, FNO, NMO, CNO, and FourCastNet.

On the three tasks summarized in Table 1, SFP reports average changes of **-34.9% MSE and +29.7% CSI on SEVIR**, **-56.2% MSE and -57.3% TKE-spectrum error on NSE**, and **-33.6% MSE and +11.5% SSIM on Prometheus**. Improvements vary substantially by backbone. Experiments on 10%-100% training subsets report a widening advantage under lower data availability, especially for CSI. In 50-step SWE forecasts, SFP reduces relative L2 error for all three reported backbones and all three variables.

Ablations report degradation when planning, self-training, or the domain-specific reward is removed; replacing TKE reward with MSE produces the largest physical-consistency decline. Beam width 10 is presented as a performance-latency compromise. SFP remains stable where the paper's direct DPO comparator collapses and reports lower CRPS than the deterministic baseline and PreDiff on the two plotted probabilistic tasks. Five-run MSE results are supplied for three backbones on NSE and SEVIR rather than for the complete benchmark matrix.

The additional computation is material. Table 2 reports training times around 1.4 times the baseline in the highlighted comparisons, while beam-search inference latency rises from 150-1,100 ms to 1,250-9,200 ms depending on task and backbone. Model size and memory also increase.

## Limitations

- The domain reward must be computable for candidate futures during optimization. CSI, TKE error, and SSIM comparisons as described rely on reference outcomes, so deployment-time planning without known future ground truth is not clearly specified in the supplied text.
- Planning quality is bounded by the frozen world model: search cannot recover futures outside its learned support and may exploit model error.
- Beam search imposes a roughly eightfold inference-latency increase in the configurations reported in Table 2, despite more moderate training-time and memory overhead.
- Statistical repeats cover only three backbones on NSE and SEVIR; the broader ten-backbone, multi-benchmark table does not report uncertainty.
- The data-scarcity, DPO-stability, CRPS, and sensitivity claims are primarily presented in figures whose exact numeric values are not available in the supplied Markdown.
- Publication year, venue, DOI, and the paper's own arXiv identifier are not stated in the supplied text, so they are left unspecified here.
