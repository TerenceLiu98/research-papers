## TL;DR

This paper models a heterogeneous graph dataset as a mixture of [[concepts/graphons|Graphons]]. It clusters graphs by vectors of empirical motif densities, estimates one graphon per cluster, and uses the inferred components in graphon-mixture-aware mixup (GMAM) and model-aware graph contrastive learning (MGCL). On the reported benchmarks, GMAM has the highest accuracy on six of seven supervised datasets, while MGCL ranks first on four and second on three unsupervised datasets, giving the best average rank.

## Research Question

Can empirical motif densities recover distinct graph-generating distributions within a heterogeneous dataset, and can those inferred graphon components improve graph mixup and graph contrastive learning?

## Motivation

Graphon estimators and graphon-based mixup commonly fit one latent graph model to an entire dataset or class. That assumption can blur distinct subpopulations and produce semantically inconsistent interpolations. Standard graph contrastive learning has a related problem: treating every other graph in a batch as a negative can repel graphs generated by the same latent model. The paper seeks a common model-based treatment of both problems.

## Contributions

- Introduces motif-based clustering for partitioning observed graphs into candidate graphon components before estimating a representative graphon for each cluster.
- Proves a concentration bound connecting empirical motif-density differences to the cut distance between the generating graphons, with a sampling term whose dominant motif-size dependence improves from linear to square-root scaling relative to the classical bound used for comparison.
- Proposes GMAM, which estimates a graphon mixture within each class and mixes the components assigned to the selected source graphs.
- Proposes MGCL, which uses component-specific graphons to generate augmented views and excludes graphs assigned to the anchor's component from the negative set.
- Evaluates mixture recovery, supervised augmentation, unsupervised representation learning, runtime, graph-size sensitivity, component count, motif count, graphon estimation, and the separate MGCL contributions of graphon-informed augmentation and cluster-aware loss.

## Method

### Graphon mixture estimation

For each graph, the method computes a vector of normalized counts for a fixed motif family. The main experiments use all nine connected motifs with at most four vertices. It applies k-means to these vectors, using $K=\log T$ as the default number of components for $T$ graphs. Within each cluster, it selects the $L$ graphs nearest the centroid and estimates a graphon from them with Scalable Implicit Graphon Learning (SIGL), which also estimates latent node positions.

The central bound decomposes the difference between the empirical density of a motif $F$ in two sampled graphs into a graphon-distance term and sampling error. If both graphs have $n$ vertices and their generating graphons are within cut distance $\epsilon$, then, with probability at least $1-2\eta$,

$$
|t(F,G_1)-t(F,G_2)| \leq e(F)\epsilon + 2\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\log\frac{4}{\eta}} + \frac{e(F)}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{4}{\eta}}\right),
$$

where $k$ and $e(F)$ are the motif's numbers of vertices and edges. This supports motif vectors as signatures of the latent graphon when graph sampling noise is sufficiently small.

### Graphon-mixture-aware mixup

GMAM runs mixture estimation separately within every class. After selecting graphs from two different classes, it identifies their assigned graphons, forms their convex combination, samples a new graph from the mixed graphon, and applies the same coefficient to the one-hot labels. This replaces the single-graphon-per-class assumption of G-Mixup with component-level interpolation.

### Model-aware graph contrastive learning

MGCL resamples a fraction of each anchor graph's potential edges according to its assigned graphon, leaving the remaining adjacency entries unchanged. It encodes the original and augmented graphs as a positive pair. Its modified InfoNCE denominator includes only graphs assigned to different graphon components, aiming to avoid false negatives from the same inferred generative model. A lower bound relates the loss to positive-pair alignment, separation from the centroid of other-component negatives, and the number of eligible negatives.

## Experiments

On synthetic mixtures of seven graphons, motif-based clustering reaches 80.0% accuracy for varying-size graphs and 79.3% for fixed-size graphs, versus theory-based nearest-moment scores of 81.4% and 82.9%. It outperforms the tested GCN, GIN, Graph2Vec, DeepWalk, spectral, and DisenGCN embeddings. The paper also shows that empirical moment vectors become more concentrated and separable as graph size increases.

For supervised classification, GMAM records the highest mean accuracy in six of seven reported datasets and is second on AIDS. Compared with the single-graphon SIGL mixup variant, GMAM improves every reported dataset. The datasets cover IMDB-B, IMDB-M, three Reddit binary/multiclass settings, COLLAB, and AIDS.

For unsupervised representation learning under linear evaluation, MGCL ranks first on NCI1, PROTEINS, DD, and MUTAG and second on COLLAB, RDT-B, and IMDB-B. Its average rank is 1.42, compared with 3.57 for the next-best reported average. Component ablations on PROTEINS, MUTAG, and IMDB-B show gains from graphon-informed augmentation alone and further gains after adding the cluster-aware loss.

## Limitations

- Motif estimates have high variance for small graphs, so component separation depends on graph size, the number of available graphs, and the stability of cluster-level statistics.
- A finite low-order motif family cannot distinguish graphons that agree on those motif densities; higher-order motifs may be necessary and increase computation.
- The theory uses the classical dense-graphon regime. Some benchmarks are sparse, where the fitted graphons are better interpreted as cluster-level motif approximations than exact generative models.
- The number of components is a tunable hyperparameter. The default $K=\log T$ is not uniformly optimal, and too many clusters can fragment shared structure.
- Cluster assignments are treated as underlying-model assignments in downstream procedures, so estimation errors can affect augmentation and negative selection.
- The evaluations use a particular estimator, SIGL, and standard benchmark datasets; the reported empirical gains do not establish that every heterogeneous graph dataset follows a graphon mixture.
