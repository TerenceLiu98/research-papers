## TL;DR

VlogQA is an extractive Vietnamese machine reading comprehension dataset with 10,076 human-written question-answer pairs over 1,230 food and travel vlog transcripts. Its contexts average 2,751.7 segmented words and preserve naturally occurring spoken-language and automatic speech recognition artifacts. XLM-R Large obtains the strongest reported test result, at 53.97% exact match and 75.34% F1, while models trained on written-domain UIT-ViQuAD transfer poorly to VlogQA.

## Research Question

Can a manually annotated dataset derived from real Vietnamese vlog transcripts support the development and evaluation of extractive question-answering systems for long, informal, and transcription-noisy spoken-language contexts?

## Motivation

Existing Vietnamese reading-comprehension datasets primarily use written Wikipedia, textbook, news, or FAQ content. Such sources underrepresent informal grammar, dialect variation, filler words, missing punctuation, and recognition errors found in everyday speech. VlogQA targets this gap with [[concepts/spoken-machine-reading-comprehension|Spoken Machine Reading Comprehension]] based on real YouTube transcripts rather than synthesized speech or read Wikipedia passages.

## Contributions

- Introduces 10,076 extractive question-answer pairs over 1,230 Vietnamese vlog transcripts covering food and travel.
- Documents a four-stage construction process: transcript collection, question-answer creation, corpus modification, and quality assurance.
- Compares the corpus with UIT-ViQuAD in context length, vocabulary, answer length, and question-type distribution.
- Benchmarks six multilingual and Vietnamese pretrained models and tests transfer from written-domain UIT-ViQuAD training data.
- Analyzes annotation agreement and model errors, including answer-boundary disagreements and errors across question types.

## Method

The authors collected transcripts from Vietnamese YouTube channels with at least 200,000 subscribers. Only 64 of the 1,230 transcripts were manually produced by video creators; the rest were automatically generated by YouTube. Full transcripts were retained rather than divided into passages.

For each transcript, an annotator wrote at least five questions and marked the shortest continuous answer span that remained a complete meaningful word or phrase. Ambiguous transcripts or those with excessive recognition errors could be discarded. Annotation was followed by additional training, self-validation, and review by a second annotator. The corpus was split approximately 8:1:1 by question, with each transcript confined to one split: 8,047 training, 1,017 development, and 1,012 test questions.

Quality assurance used a 100-question sample answered independently by six annotators. Agreement was measured with pairwise-averaged Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa, Krippendorff's alpha, ROUGE, and BERTScore. A separate team supplied additional test answers for estimating human exact match and F1.

## Experiments

The evaluated models are mBERT, XLM-R Base, XLM-R Large, PhoBERT, BARTPho, and ViT5. They were fine-tuned for ten epochs with batch size 8 and tokenizer maximum length 512, except PhoBERT at 256 because of hardware constraints. Evaluation uses exact match (EM) and token-overlap F1.

| Model | Test EM (%) | Test F1 (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| mBERT | 45.17 | 64.89 |
| XLM-R Base | 47.73 | 68.71 |
| XLM-R Large | **53.97** | **75.34** |
| PhoBERT | 23.06 | 34.37 |
| BARTPho | 20.43 | 32.58 |
| ViT5 | 29.37 | 45.53 |
| Human performance | 48.49 | 76.25 |

XLM-R Large has the best model result and is close to the reported human F1, though the human comparison uses independently supplied alternative answers rather than establishing an absolute ceiling. When the models are instead fine-tuned on UIT-ViQuAD and evaluated on VlogQA, XLM-R Large falls to 35.42% EM and 62.43% F1. This cross-domain decline supports the need for spoken-domain training data.

The three annotation-agreement coefficients are all about 0.44, while ROUGE-L is 0.7672 and BERTScore is 0.8867. The paper interprets this gap as evidence that annotators often locate similar content but disagree about exact answer boundaries. On development-set exact match, XLM-R Large has its highest error rate for Why questions (74.70%) and lowest for How-quantity (34.93%) and Where questions (33.96%).

## Limitations

- The dataset covers only food and travel vlogs from channels above a subscriber threshold, limiting topical and creator diversity.
- Automatically generated transcripts inherit accent, dialect, audio-quality, punctuation, capitalization, and speaker-identification errors.
- Context lengths vary sharply, and full transcripts contain substantial irrelevant material; token limits also mean the model setup cannot directly consume many complete transcripts.
- Annotation agreement is moderate at roughly 0.44, and the error analysis shows frequent disagreement over optional or missing boundary terms.
- Models trained on written Vietnamese reading comprehension transfer poorly to this spoken domain.
- The authors did not train a spoken-language pretrained model because of GPU and memory constraints.
- The source does not report repeated runs, uncertainty intervals, or significance tests for model comparisons.
