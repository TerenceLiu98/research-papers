## TL;DR

The paper replaces uniformly random interior collocation points in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) with deterministic good lattice points. Under regularity and optimization assumptions, the resulting residual quadrature has error $O((\log N_r)^d/N_r)$ instead of Monte Carlo's $O(N_r^{-1/2})$, leading to a tighter conditional solution-error bound for sufficiently large sample sets. Experiments on rough two-dimensional Poisson problems, an inverse Helmholtz problem, and five- and eight-dimensional PDEs generally favor [[concepts/good-lattice-training|Good Lattice Training]], although the dimension-dependent logarithmic factor and several empirical comparisons qualify the advantage.

## Research Question

Can a deterministic, non-adaptive number-theoretic design reduce the sampling component of PINN error on low-regularity and high-dimensional PDEs without requiring a preliminary solution or residual-dependent resampling?

## Motivation

A PINN's integrated residual loss is usually approximated with uniformly random collocation points. This Monte Carlo discretization contributes estimation error, and random designs can leave sparse coverage near sharp solution features. Residual-adaptive methods target these regions but require pretraining and introduce problem-dependent sampling hyperparameters. The paper instead studies a fixed, uniform point set with low discrepancy.

## Contributions

- Constructs interior PINN collocation sets from good lattice points generated by a number-theoretic vector, while retaining uniformly random boundary samples.
- Derives optimization and solution-error bounds that explicitly expose the sampling error. Under the stated smoothness, local optimization, stochastic-gradient, and operator-stability assumptions, the GLP term scales as $O((\log N_r)^d/N_r)$ rather than $O(N_r^{-1/2})$.
- Evaluates the design against uniform random, Latin hypercube, Halton, Hammersley, and Sobol sampling on forward and inverse PDE problems, including nonrectangular and high-dimensional domains.

## Method

For a prime-sized design, the method searches for a primitive root and constructs a generating vector whose components are successive powers modulo the design size. The interior points are

$$
x_{ij}=\frac{2q_{ij}-1}{2N_r}, \qquad q_{ij}\equiv i h_j \pmod{N_r}.
$$

The resulting set minimizes discrepancy among the considered lattice generators. These points replace the random interior samples in the empirical residual loss, while boundary points remain uniformly random. Training otherwise uses an ordinary fully connected PINN and Adam followed, in most experiments, by L-BFGS. For a circular domain, the paper maps a two-dimensional lattice from the unit square through a polar coordinate transformation.

The analysis first relates lattice discrepancy to quadrature error and then propagates that error through stochastic-gradient convergence and a stability inequality for the PDE operators. Fixed and diminishing learning-rate results are given. The final comparison is conditional: it applies when the residual has continuous mixed derivatives, the empirical loss satisfies local smoothness and Polyak-Lojasiewicz-type conditions along the optimization path, gradient moments obey the assumed bounds, and the PDE operators satisfy the stated stability bound.

## Experiments

- **Rough 2D Poisson equations:** With 10,946 interior points, GLP obtains $e_\infty=4.475\times10^{-3}$ and $e_2=4.045\times10^{-2}$ on the one-peak problem, lower than all five reported alternatives. On the two-peak disk problem, it gives the lowest $e_2$ ($2.171\times10^{-2}$), while Hammersley gives a slightly lower $e_\infty$ ($8.220\times10^{-3}$ versus $8.943\times10^{-3}$). Sampling-budget curves generally retain the GLP advantage.
- **Inverse 2D Helmholtz equation:** Using 6,765 residual points and 40 observations, GLP improves solution and parameter errors over random sampling both without noise and with 1%, 5%, and 10% observation noise. At 10% noise, its $e_2$ is $3.9407\times10^{-3}$ versus $2.6511\times10^{-2}$ for random sampling.
- **High-dimensional linear PDEs:** In five dimensions, 10,007 GLP points outperform random designs with up to 51,097 points on both reported metrics. In eight dimensions, 11,215 GLP points improve strongly over random sampling at the same size, but a 33,139-point random design reports slightly better errors than GLP ($e_2=1.172\times10^{-3}$ versus $2.287\times10^{-3}$; $e_\infty=5.272\times10^{-3}$ versus $6.369\times10^{-3}$).
- **High-dimensional nonlinear PDEs:** The five-dimensional GLP design with 3,001 points outperforms random designs with as many as 15,019 points. In eight dimensions, 11,215 GLP points also outperform the reported random designs, including one with 46,213 points.

All experiments use a single fixed random seed of 100. The principal comparisons use relative $L_2$ and maximum errors on large random test sets, with training conducted on NVIDIA A100 or A800 GPUs.

## Limitations

- The theoretical advantage is asymptotic and conditional on differentiability, local loss geometry, stochastic-gradient moment bounds, and PDE stability assumptions; the experiments do not directly verify all of them.
- The $O((\log N_r)^d/N_r)$ term can become unfavorable when dimension is large and the point budget is limited. The paper explicitly notes this restriction, and the larger random design in the eight-dimensional linear experiment beats GLP.
- GLP is applied only to the interior residual. Boundary points remain random, so their $O(N_b^{-1/2})$ term persists in the bound.
- Most random comparisons report one fixed seed. Ten-seed error bands are used only for selected two-dimensional comparisons, limiting evidence about run-to-run variation elsewhere.
- The study focuses on PINNs and a small set of manufactured PDE solutions. It does not evaluate other neural PDE solvers, non-uniform number-theoretic sampling, or broad irregular-domain families.
- Hammersley sampling performs poorly in one one-peak training trace but well on the two-peak problem, indicating that optimizer interactions can obscure a point set's intrinsic quadrature quality.
