## Overview

Legislative cosponsorship networks represent lawmakers' shared support for bills as relational data. They capture patterns of collaboration and signaling that can inform studies of legislative organization and outcomes.

## Key Ideas

- Network construction determines interpretation: sponsor–cosponsor ties can preserve direction, while joint participation networks treat collaborators symmetrically.
- Participation frequency, collaboration success, and recency are distinct weighting choices. Success-weighted ties encode outcomes as well as relationships.
- Individual network scores can be aggregated to bills by means or maxima, representing different hypotheses about collective support.
- Retrospective outcome-weighted measures require care in prediction: eventual outcomes and focal-bill contributions must not be mistaken for information available before a decision.
- Shared participation may reflect ideological similarity, party organization, strategic signaling, or reciprocal support; it does not alone identify interpersonal influence.
