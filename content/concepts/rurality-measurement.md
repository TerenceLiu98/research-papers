## Overview

Rurality measurement links a theoretical account of rural places or people to observable indicators. Settlement density, metropolitan access, commuting, economic structure, and self-identification describe different constructs; a single rural binary cannot represent them all.

## Key Ideas

- **Choose concept and unit separately.** A county may be appropriate for county government research but too heterogeneous to characterize a respondent's neighborhood.
- **Connectivity matters.** RUCA classifications distinguish settlement cores and commuting relationships; equally sparse places can differ in access to jobs and services.
- **Thresholds define populations.** Combining small towns with remote rural places changes the group under study and potentially its estimated characteristics.
- **Residence and identification differ.** A geographic classifier cannot substitute for a survey measure of rural identification. Neighborhood self-description also does not fully measure rural consciousness.
- **Ordinal categories need care.** Population-weighted averages of RUCA codes assume distances between categories; plurality and probabilistic assignments retain categories but lose other aspects of local diversity.
- **Controls matter.** Reclassifying rurality can affect coefficients on other covariates. Robustness should examine theoretically defensible alternatives, not select the classifier producing preferred significance.
