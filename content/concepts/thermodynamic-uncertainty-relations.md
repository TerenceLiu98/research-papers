## Overview

Thermodynamic uncertainty relations bound the precision of stochastic currents in nonequilibrium steady states. The thermodynamic uncertainty relation (TUR) ties relative current fluctuations to entropy production, while kinetic uncertainty relations (KURs) use dynamical activity as a complementary bound.

## Key Ideas

- The TUR states that the squared relative dispersion of a steady current cannot be arbitrarily small at fixed entropy production. Precise directional currents require an irreversibility cost.
- The KUR constrains current precision using the average number of jumps per unit time. It becomes especially useful when time-symmetric activity matters more than entropy production in a far-from-equilibrium regime.
- In social imitation, the current can count reaction-resolved opinion changes. The reaction-potential difference controls the TUR bound, while total opinion volatility controls the KUR bound.
- The bounds are about fluctuations over long observation times and are evaluated through current cumulants. Full Counting Statistics and large-deviation methods provide spectral routes to those cumulants.
- Saturation depends on the regime. The social imitation model studied by Irisarri et al. finds the TUR tight near equilibrium and the KUR more informative in strong anti-conformity, while symmetry breaking changes current precision near criticality.
