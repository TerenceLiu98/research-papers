## Overview

Information-search measurement supplements observed game choices with records of what participants inspect before deciding. MouseLab-style interfaces conceal information behind selectable boxes or windows and record access sequences and durations. These traces help distinguish learning rules that predict similar actions but require different information.

## Key Ideas

- Best-response learning requires information about rivals' actions and a way to evaluate responses. Imitating a successful rival requires an observable link between that rival's action and payoff. Own-payoff reinforcement can operate from memory.
- In Bigoni and Fort's Cournot experiment, deliberate button presses open one window at a time, while a 30-second decision limit makes attention selective. Calculator inputs additionally provide a proxy for expectations about aggregate rival output.
- Search patterns can classify participants and condition [[concepts/experience-weighted-attraction-learning|Experience-Weighted Attraction Learning]] updates on inspected payoffs. Classification reflects compatibility with a rule, not proof of an exclusive cognitive mechanism.
- A look-up indicates access rather than comprehension or use. Absence of a look-up is also ambiguous when information can be remembered or inferred.
- Endogenous search differs from randomized information provision. Joint search-choice analysis can support a mechanism interpretation, but by itself does not establish the causal effect of disclosure.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/information-and-learning-in-oligopoly-an-experiment|Information and learning in oligopoly: An experiment]]: connects information acquisition, output adjustment, and a generalized EWA model in repeated Cournot markets.
- Bigoni (2010), "What do you want to know? Information acquisition and learning in experimental Cournot games": related use with computerized opponents, as summarized in the 2013 paper.
- Johnson, Schkade, and Bettman (1988), "Monitoring information processing and decision: The Mouselab system": methodological antecedent cited by Bigoni and Fort.
