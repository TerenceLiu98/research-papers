## Overview

Cross-stage dependence concerns whether scientific entry, resource allocation, production, and retention contribute distinct grounds for selecting research trajectories. Later decisions may inherit the consequences of earlier selections or share evaluative criteria, information sources, and technological infrastructure.

## Key Ideas

- A grant can generate output and visibility that later evaluators reuse as a signal of promise. Several favorable decisions therefore need not represent independent judgments.
- Agreement alone does not establish dependence: selectors can independently respond to reliable scientific evidence. Identification must distinguish inherited signals from that evidence.
- Cumulative advantage and path dependence can generate this relationship but are not equivalent to it. Instrumental lock-in, for example, need not involve shared grounds for evaluation across stages.
- Under uncertainty, dependence is hypothesized to remove plausible alternatives prematurely and slow redirection. This is a conditional prediction, not an established system-wide finding.
- Funding discontinuities, randomized disclosure of status signals, and comparisons of shared evaluation infrastructure offer possible empirical tests.
- Shared AI systems could couple research generation and assessment; heterogeneous tools could instead broaden search. Independent verification and recoverable expertise may help preserve correction routes.
