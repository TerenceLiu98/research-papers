## Overview

Public personality cues are observable verbal, nonverbal, or paraverbal signals used to infer a public figure’s personality. The inferred public persona need not reveal intrinsic dispositions: strategic self-presentation also shapes the evidence available to observers.

## Key Ideas

- **Cue validity and cue utilization are separate.** A signal must be relevant to the trait, and an observer or model must interpret it appropriately.
- **Signals are ambiguous.** One behavior can reflect several traits, while one trait can generate many behaviors. Political and cultural perspectives can change interpretation.
- **Explicitness changes the measurement target.** Restricting analysis to direct self-disclosure provides a narrower target than including indirect expressions. Neither should be silently substituted for the other.
- **Absence is not opposition.** A sentence without a positive empathy cue does not establish a cold-hearted speaker. Opposite-pole statements can serve as functional tests against this confusion.
- **Aggregation needs a denominator.** The share of all sentences expressing a trait and its share among detected personality cues answer different questions.
