## Overview

A policy that improves a measured prediction target can worsen overall outcomes if it changes other consequences that decision makers value. Evaluating decision quality therefore requires specifying the relevant payoff function as well as the prediction target.

## Key Ideas

- An omitted characteristic matters for this bias when it affects payoffs directly, beyond its usefulness for predicting the measured outcome.
- For payoff consisting of a measured component plus an omitted term, the true policy contrast also includes the difference in expected omitted payoffs under the two rules.
- Compare multiple outcomes or impose explicit constraints on valued consequences. Excluding a protected attribute from model inputs does not establish equitable policy outcomes.
- Unknown preferences limit claims of dominance even when predictive accuracy is strong.
