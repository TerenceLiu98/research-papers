## Overview

Geographical imagination is the process through which people perceive, represent, and remake knowledge of places and spatial relations. These representations draw on experience, memory, emotion, identity, and discourse. They are socially constructed and implicated in power because describing a place also frames who belongs there, how it differs from elsewhere, and at what scale it should be understood.

## Key Ideas

- Geographical imaginations concern familiar as well as unfamiliar places; neither direct experience nor distance guarantees an uncontested representation.
- They are selective transformations of spatial knowledge rather than neutral mirrors of territory.
- Scale shapes meaning. A local encounter may be represented as evidence about a region or nation, while regional or bodily knowledge may challenge that generalization.
- Competing imaginations can reveal different [[concepts/territorial-subjectivity|Territorial Subjectivities]] and produce political conflict.
- Digital platforms accelerate the circulation and contestation of spatial representations, making geographical imagination central to [[concepts/digital-geopolitics|Digital Geopolitics]].

## Important Papers

- Gregory (1994), *Geographical Imaginations*: establishes geographical imagination as a central framework for understanding spatial discourse and power.
- Daniels (2011), "Geographical Imagination": reviews the concept and its role in representing places and worlds.
- [[papers/transnational-bloggers-in-china-geographical-imagination-territorial-subjectivity-and-digital-geopolitics|Transnational Bloggers in China: Geographical Imagination, Territorial Subjectivity and Digital Geopolitics]]: traces global, regional, and bodily geographical imaginations across Bilibili videos and comments.
