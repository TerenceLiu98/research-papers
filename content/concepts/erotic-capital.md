## Overview

Erotic capital treats physical, visual, and interpersonal appeal as a socially valued resource. In Ge, Zheng, and Tian's account of Hakim's framework, it encompasses beauty, sexual attractiveness, social skills, liveliness, self-presentation, and sexuality. Its value depends on social settings and can intersect with economic and cultural advantages.

## Key Ideas

- Attractiveness extends beyond bodily appearance to presentation, sociability, and perceived personality.
- Economic and cultural resources can reinforce desirability: credentials, occupational prestige, and refinement shape interpretations of a person's appearance.
- The concept can be applied to representations of men as well as women. Ge and colleagues analyze five publicly represented dimensions of appeal rather than claiming access to private sexual competence.
- Being positioned as an active evaluator of attractiveness can express agency while preserving restrictive beauty standards and class distinctions. Empowerment is an interpretive question, not an automatic consequence of reversing the gaze.
