## Overview

Candidacy eligibility criteria are formal party rules defining who may seek nomination. They concern access to the candidate pool, alongside the separate questions of who selects candidates and at what organizational level selection occurs.

## Key Ideas

- **Screening before selection:** demanding requirements may deter office-seeking opportunists and allow committed aspirants to signal their attachment to a party.
- **Socialization:** minimum membership periods and requirements for copartisan signatures can encourage sustained participation and internal networks. Internalizing unity as a norm need not imply identical policy preferences.
- **Type matters:** monetary deposits and loyalty pledges need not foster the same interactions as membership duration or signatures. Union and religious requirements may introduce additional sources of loyalty.
- **Formal rules are incomplete:** informal vetting, enforcement, and waivers affect practical access. An absence of written restrictions is not evidence of unrestricted recruitment.
- **Identification matters:** associations between rules and unity can reflect endogenous organizational choices. Comparing rules at joining and nomination probes different mechanisms without conclusively separating sorting from socialization.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/candidacy-eligibility-criteria-and-party-unity|Candidacy Eligibility Criteria and Party Unity]] (Rehmert, 2020): combines statutes, roll calls, and candidate surveys to examine eligibility rules, finding the clearest support for requirements involving time and internal networks.
