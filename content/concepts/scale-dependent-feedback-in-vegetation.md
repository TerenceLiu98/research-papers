## Overview

Scale-dependent feedback in vegetation describes how the sign and strength of plant interactions vary with distance. Local facilitation promotes aggregation, while competition extending beyond the facilitated region limits patch growth and separates neighboring patches. Their combination can produce regular vegetation patterns even on relatively homogeneous terrain.

## Key Ideas

- **Interaction range matters.** Similar local processes can produce different patch sizes when their spatial footprints differ.
- **Kernels encode spatial effects.** Convolving plant distributions with facilitation and competition kernels produces a net interaction field that can regulate establishment or mortality.
- **Plant strategies change pattern scale.** Extensive resource foraging can support larger, more widely separated patches than spatially concentrated resource exploitation.
- **Pattern and transition are distinct evidence.** Reproducing geometry does not by itself demonstrate bistability, hysteresis, or a particular transition order; those require additional dynamical analysis.
- **Modified interactions can reorganize patches.** In the shrub–grass study, contracted competition kernels generate hollow shrub rings, while expanded facilitation and local recruitment generate larger boundary patches.
