## Overview

Harmonized studies use the same measurement strategy and treatment/control contrast, understood by their substantive role across settings. Harmonization concerns comparability of empirical targets and does not require identical samples or settings.

## Key Ideas

- Target discrepancies compare settings at fixed design; artifactual discrepancies compare designs at fixed setting.
- Changing treatment dosage or outcome measurement can change an effect without demonstrating a failure of external validity.
- Artifactual discrepancies may reveal substantively useful intervention properties; they are not merely random nuisance terms.
- A sequential agenda varies settings under common designs, varies designs within settings, then studies their interaction.
- Adding nonharmonized studies can enlarge the region of artifactual sign disagreement even as additional observations reduce statistical uncertainty.
- Practical equivalence requires substantive justification; approximate harmonization is not automatically guaranteed by similar intervention labels.
