## Overview

AI-assisted public procurement auditing uses models to flag suspicious contracts, bidding patterns, supplier relationships, or document descriptions for investigation. An anomaly or risk score is a reason for scrutiny, not sufficient evidence that fraud occurred. Effective oversight therefore depends on data provenance, understandable alerts, and institutional procedures for reviewing and contesting decisions.

## Key Ideas

- Procurement analysis can combine prediction from structured records, [[concepts/social-network-analysis|Social Network Analysis]] of supplier relationships, and language analysis of contract or tender descriptions. These methods expose different types of potential irregularity.
- Labels require scrutiny: confirmed wrongdoing, complaints, heuristic red flags, and anomalous transactions are different learning targets. Unclear labeling limits the interpretation of model performance.
- Explanations must support the audience's task. A feature-attribution output may describe a prediction without giving an auditor sufficient grounds for an administrative action.
- [[concepts/human-oversight-of-ai|Human Oversight of AI]] connects alerts to domain judgment, correction, and supplier contestation. Monitoring and recalibration address changing data, procurement practices, and fraud tactics.
- PRO-Trust proposes integrating these safeguards across development and use, with responsibilities divided among public managers, auditors, and technical teams. Its evidence supports the relevance of the design concerns, while its operational effectiveness remains to be evaluated.
