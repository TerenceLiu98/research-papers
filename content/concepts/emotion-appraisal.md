## Overview

Emotion appraisal is the process of evaluating a situation along dimensions that determine which emotion, if any, arises. Relevant dimensions include whether an event is desirable, whether it was caused by oneself or another agent, and whether it is manageable. Appraisal is context dependent: similar situations can yield different emotions across people or occasions because they are evaluated differently.

## Key Ideas

- Emotions cannot be inferred from situation labels alone. The same event may produce fear, excitement, anger, or no emotion depending on its appraisal.
- Appraisal profiles connect interpretation with response. An emotion does more than identify emotional significance; it can reorganize attention, decision speed, motivation, and action tendencies.
- Variability is a central property rather than measurement noise. Human emotion representations are distributed and vary across individuals and contexts, so a stable representation associated with one emotion category is not by itself evidence of an emotional state.
- For artificial systems, emotion-related outputs and internal features should be distinguished from functional appraisal. A stronger attribution of emotion would require situation- and agent-dependent evaluations that cause sustained, interpretable changes across multiple aspects of processing.
- The functional criterion does not require a biological substrate. It asks how a state changes processing and action, while leaving open whether a non-biological or distributed system could implement those changes.
