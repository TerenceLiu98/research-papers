## Overview

Bridging-based moderation aggregates judgments to favor agreement among contributors whose historical ratings differ. Its purpose is to identify contributions with support across disagreement patterns, reducing reliance on a numerical majority from one cohesive group.

## Key Ideas

- Matrix factorization can separate contributor leniency and note helpfulness from latent contributor–note agreement.
- Latent positions are behavioral estimates, not direct observations of political identity.
- Requiring broader agreement can leave more notes unresolved, creating a trade-off between coverage and cross-group support.
- Interaction negativity and organization into opposing camps are distinct outcomes: antagonism can rise while structural alignment falls.
- Changes in scoring, changes in participant behavior, and independently measured note quality require separate evaluations.

## Important Papers

[[papers/algorithmic-intervention-decreases-polarization-in-content-moderation|Algorithmic Intervention Decreases Polarization in Content Moderation]] reports less bimodal latent positions and lower network alignment after Community Notes adopted bridging, alongside more negative interactions. Its observational comparison does not identify causality, and independent helpfulness gains were not statistically significant.
