## Overview

Directed technological change studies how economic incentives determine not only the rate of innovation but also which kinds of technologies researchers develop. Expected profits, factor prices, market size, and research productivity steer inventive effort toward technologies that augment particular factors or alter which inputs firms use.

## Key Ideas

- **Innovation responds to relative scarcity and cost.** High wages can make labor-saving technologies more valuable, while cheap effective labor can redirect effort toward technologies that use labor more intensively.
- **Research productivity shapes direction.** Even with identical market incentives, differences in how effectively scientists create competing technologies alter the long-run composition of innovation.
- **Feedback can stabilize or destabilize growth.** In a [[concepts/task-based-models-of-automation|task-based model]], automation can reduce the effective labor cost of the next task and thereby discourage further automation. Patent structure and creative destruction can weaken or reverse this feedback.
- **Market direction need not be socially efficient.** Innovators respond to private profits rather than all social benefits and costs. Labor-market wedges can make firms automate according to wages even when labor's social opportunity cost is lower.
- **Balanced growth imposes restrictions.** When several technology frontiers evolve, their rates must be compatible with stable task shares and factor prices; endogenous research allocation can generate this balance only in particular parameter regions.

## Important Papers

- Acemoglu (1998), "Why Do New Technologies Complement Skills? Directed Technical Change and Wage Inequality": connects relative skill supplies to the endogenous direction of technical change.
- Acemoglu (2002), "Directed Technical Change": provides a general framework for price and market-size effects on innovation direction.
- [[papers/the-race-between-man-and-machine-implications-of-technology-for-growth-factor-shares-and-employment|The Race between Man and Machine: Implications of Technology for Growth, Factor Shares, and Employment]]: directs research between automation and the creation of labor-intensive tasks and studies the resulting balanced-growth paths.
- Acemoglu (2003), "Labor- and Capital-Augmenting Technical Change": studies endogenous factor-augmenting innovation and balanced growth.
