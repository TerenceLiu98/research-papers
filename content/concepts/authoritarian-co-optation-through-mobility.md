## Overview

An authoritarian regime can cultivate political acquiescence by maintaining accessible routes into privileged positions. The mechanism operates through beliefs about future advancement: relatively few successful candidates receive rewards, while a larger population sees opportunities within the existing system.

## Key Ideas

- **Access matters beyond selection outcomes.** Opening recruitment can affect people who never win a position, if the opportunity changes their perceived prospects.
- **Credibility is conditional.** Transparent procedures, enforcement, and repeated recruitment can strengthen confidence; patronage and fiscal deterioration can undermine it.
- **Meritocratic access has scope conditions.** It broadens opportunity relative to patronage when qualifying capabilities are more widely distributed than political connections. It need not equalize resources or realized outcomes.
- **Political outcomes are distinct.** Lower redistribution demand does not necessarily imply greater government trust, less protest, or observed regime survival.
- **Perception and realization require separate evidence.** Subjective mobility changes cannot establish actual socioeconomic gains.
