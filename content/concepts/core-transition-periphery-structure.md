## Overview

The Core-Transition-Periphery structure is a process-based model of intra-urban differentiation. It distinguishes a persistently leading core, an intermediate transition zone whose trajectories and boundaries reorganize most readily, and a delayed periphery. The zones are defined by long-term evolutionary paths and cross-scale stability rather than by fixed morphology or distance alone.

## Key Ideas

- **Core:** Repeated or early growth impulses accumulate in a structurally persistent high-vitality region.
- **Transition:** Intermediate trajectories receive and overlap impulses from different periods. High boundary volatility across regionalization scales makes this the most dynamic part of the structure rather than a residual buffer.
- **Periphery:** Growth commonly begins later and observed trajectories are simpler; aggregate outer boundaries can remain relatively stable even when the zone contains local heterogeneity.
- **Pattern-process coupling:** The spatial ordering provides a carrier for the [[concepts/spatiotemporal-ripple-effect|Spatiotemporal Ripple Effect]], while repeated temporally ordered diffusion reproduces distinctions among the three zones.
- **Different from a morphological template:** The structure can coexist with polycentric form. It describes the dominant organization of trajectories over a study period and should not be assumed universal across cities or periods.
- **Scale dependence:** Macro partitions emphasize an integrated core-to-periphery pattern, meso partitions expose transition-zone differentiation, and micro partitions prioritize local specificity.
