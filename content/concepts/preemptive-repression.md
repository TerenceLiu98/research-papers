## Overview

Preemptive repression is coercion intended to prevent anticipated dissent before it becomes an observable challenge. It can reduce opposition groups' ability to mobilize by removing people and resources, or reduce their willingness to mobilize through fear. A predictable occasion for protest or international attention can give governments an incentive to act in advance.

## Key Ideas

- **Incapacitation and deterrence are distinct mechanisms.** Removing organizers can disrupt networks, while threats and detentions can discourage participants who remain free. The same operation may serve both purposes.
- **Observed quiescence can be an outcome of coercion.** Low protest during an event does not establish a lack of grievances or a lack of repression. Actions taken earlier may already have reduced mobilization.
- **Anticipated constraints can move repression earlier.** In the [[concepts/scrutiny-publicity-dilemma|Scrutiny-Publicity Dilemma]], governments expect foreign journalists to make visible violence costly during a megaevent. This can encourage repression before their arrival, particularly where they are expected to stay.
- **Tactics differ in visibility.** The Argentine World Cup study reports a shift from direct killings toward disappearances as the tournament approached. Lower visibility does not imply lower severity.
- **Deterrence is not guaranteed.** Repression can deepen grievances or trigger resistance. Establishing deterrence requires evidence beyond the timing of violence and subsequent absence of protest.
- **Observation windows matter.** Evaluating only tournament days misses earlier coercion and possible later rebounds. Identifying net harm also requires a credible counterfactual, not just a longer window.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/international-sports-events-and-repression-in-autocracies-evidence-from-the-1978-fifa-world-cup|International Sports Events and Repression in Autocracies: Evidence from the 1978 FIFA World Cup]]: documents an increase in recorded violence before the tournament near anticipated journalist locations, followed by restraint during the event.
- Ritter and Conrad (2016), "Preventing and Responding to Dissent: The Observational Challenges of Explaining Strategic Repression": cited by the World Cup study for anticipatory coercion and its observational challenges.
- Truex (2019), "Focal Points, Dissident Calendars, and Preemptive Repression": cited for repression around predictable mobilization opportunities.
