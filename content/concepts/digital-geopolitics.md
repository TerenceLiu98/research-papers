## Overview

Digital geopolitics studies how geopolitical claims, spatial narratives, identities, and power relations are produced, circulated, and contested through digital technologies and culture. It includes state strategy and infrastructure, but also everyday interactions in which creators and audiences negotiate representations of places and populations.

## Key Ideas

- Digital mediation does not merely transmit geopolitical narratives; platform formats, recommendation systems, comments, and creator practices help constitute them.
- Geopolitical friction can arise when actors with different [[concepts/territorial-subjectivity|Territorial Subjectivities]] encounter incompatible [[concepts/geographical-imagination|Geographical Imaginations]].
- Scale is an active rhetorical resource. A local or bodily experience can be enlarged into a national comparison, while audiences can relocate a general claim within regional differences or embodied experience.
- Audiences participate by affirming, adapting, criticizing, or scrambling creator narratives. Digital geopolitics is therefore negotiated rather than exclusively imposed from above.
- Under platform capitalism, emotionally resonant geopolitical contrasts can generate visibility, engagement, and commercial value.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/transnational-bloggers-in-china-geographical-imagination-territorial-subjectivity-and-digital-geopolitics|Transnational Bloggers in China: Geographical Imagination, Territorial Subjectivity and Digital Geopolitics]]: analyzes how foreign bloggers and Chinese Bilibili audiences co-produce geopolitical friction across global, regional, and bodily scales.
- Woods (2021), "Clashing Cyphers, Contagious Content: The Digital Geopolitics of Grime": develops digital mediation and friction through the circulation of grime music.
- Tekir (2020), "Huawei, 5G Network and Digital Geopolitics": examines digital geopolitics through technology infrastructure and international power competition.
