## Overview

Concept-based literature networks organize a corpus around the relationships its studies investigate. They preserve connections among ideas across papers and support explicit descriptions of research themes, theoretical gaps, and empirical coverage.

## Key Ideas

- Define the corpus with reproducible selection rules and retain study provenance for each coded relationship.
- Distinguish concept networks from citation networks: the former represent substantive relationships, while the latter represent links between publications.
- Encode direction, empirical status, and measurement-focused self-ties explicitly. Centrality summarizes connectedness in the coded corpus, not scientific validity.
- Inspect communities and missing connections to propose research questions. A missing edge can also reflect selection or coding choices.
- Use local neighborhoods to identify candidate causal pathways and confounders, while separately assessing causal assumptions and evidence.
- Resolve synonymous terminology through source reading; concept aggregation is an interpretive decision that should remain inspectable.
