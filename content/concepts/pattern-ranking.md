## Overview

Pattern ranking occurs when the geometry of a ranking interface guides responses independently of preferences over candidates or survey items. Donkey voting, which ranks candidates in their displayed top-to-bottom order, is a special case; zigzag and dogleg responses extend the idea beyond a straight diagonal.

## Key Ideas

- Displayed-row rankings and candidate-identity rankings differ. The same visual pattern can imply different candidate preferences after order randomization.
- Randomization need not remove geometric responding. Under the randomized-order, no-pattern-ranking null in Atsusaka's framework, complete row-relative permutations are uniformly distributed; deviations provide a diagnostic.
- A frequent geometric response can also coincide with genuine preferences. Neither its raw frequency nor rejection of uniformity directly identifies the proportion of pattern rankers.
- Rotation-based comparisons require comparable underlying preferences across groups exposed to different orders.
- Complete-ranking diagnostics do not automatically apply to truncated rankings, and factorial growth limits frequency-based testing for many items.
