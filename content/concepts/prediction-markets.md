## Overview

Prediction markets trade contracts whose payoffs depend on future events. A binary election contract pays a fixed amount if a candidate wins and zero otherwise. Its price relative to that payoff is commonly interpreted as an anticipated winning probability, subject to market conditions and participant incentives.

## Key Ideas

- Trading combines participants' beliefs and information into a changing price. Participants can revise or exit positions before the event is resolved.
- Prices concern the probability of a specified outcome; approval polls measure support, and search volumes measure attention. These quantities are related but not interchangeable.
- Information aggregation depends on participation and liquidity. Thin trading can weaken the interpretation of prices as collective forecasts.
- Efficiency and forecasting accuracy are empirical questions. Dependence in transformed price changes can motivate investigation, but does not alone demonstrate a profitable strategy after costs or explain the behavior of individual traders.
- [[Multifractal Detrended Fluctuation Analysis]] provides one descriptive approach to the scaling of price changes. Surrogate comparisons distinguish residual nonlinear structure from apparent multifractality associated with finite size and marginal distributions.
