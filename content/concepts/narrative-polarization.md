## Overview

Narrative polarization is divergence between information environments in how central actors are positioned within narratives. It concerns structural portrayals of interests, agency, control, and opposition. Two environments can discuss the same issue yet assign incompatible roles to its participants, or discuss different topics without opposing portrayals of the same actors.

## Key Ideas

- **Compare relationships, not only topics.** In [[concepts/greimas-actantial-model|Greimas' Actantial Model]], a Subject-Object pair identifies whose pursuit of which outcome is foregrounded. This offers an actor-centered basis for comparing narratives.
- **Separate distribution overlap from opposing portrayals.** Shared probability mass, $\sum_x\min\{p(x),q(x)\}$, measures overlap of narrative distributions. Low overlap can arise from different issue agendas, so it does not by itself establish narrative polarization.
- **Condition on a shared object.** In a two-actor-group comparison, subject divergence is $D(i)=p(i,A)-q(i,A)$, where the shares refer to group $A$ among retained subjects for object $i$ in two environments. Its sign depends on environment and actor ordering. A negative difference does not show which actor receives the majority of attributions in either environment.
- **Distinguish agreement from balance.** Both environments may disproportionately associate an object with the same actor and still have near-zero divergence. Measure absolute attribution separately when that distinction matters.
- **Inspect multiple structural levels.** Similar actor-goal distributions can coexist with different configurations of control, opposition, and recipients. Motifs may expose internal actor complexity, dependency, or justifications for violence that core narratives miss.
- **Keep interpretation at the measured level.** Narrative convergence in a corpus does not establish ideological convergence among its authors, individual exposure, or causal depolarization. Environment definitions, actor aggregation, role reliability, and missing roles shape the measurement.
