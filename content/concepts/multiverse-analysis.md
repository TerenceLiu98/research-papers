## Overview

Multiverse analysis makes analytical flexibility explicit by defining alternative decisions and evaluating their combinations. Its object is the distribution of findings across a specified analytical universe, rather than the uncertainty interval from a single selected model.

## Key Ideas

- The universe can vary outcome measurement, sample inclusion, controls, fixed effects, and standard-error estimation jointly.
- Defining defensible alternatives is essential: enumerating more specifications does not make every specification appropriate.
- Summaries can distinguish coefficient direction, significance, and the decisions associated with changes. A share of significant specifications is not a posterior probability that an effect exists.
- One-choice perturbations describe local switches; predictive models can capture interactions among decisions. Their importance measures answer different questions.
- Exhaustive enumeration may be expensive. Randomly sampled specifications can approximate universe-level summaries, with approximation quality requiring assessment.

## Important Papers

- Steegen et al. (2016), “Increasing transparency through a multiverse analysis,” is the foundational multiverse reference cited by the application below.
- [[papers/estimating-the-extent-and-sources-of-model-uncertainty-in-political-science|Estimating the extent and sources of model uncertainty in political science]] combines systematic enumeration with multiple specification dimensions and finds sampling and outcome measurement more consequential than covariate selection in four applications.
