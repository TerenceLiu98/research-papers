## Overview

A basin is riddled when every neighborhood of every point in it intersects its complement in positive measure. Competing destinations remain accessible under arbitrarily small perturbations, including near the attracting set.

## Key Ideas

- A classical route combines a chaotic attractor in an invariant subspace, transverse instability of embedded periodic points, and competing attractors.
- Trajectory chaos and destination uncertainty are different properties; chaos alone does not imply riddling.
- Finite-resolution destination maps and small uncertainty exponents provide evidence but cannot by themselves prove the all-scales geometric property.
