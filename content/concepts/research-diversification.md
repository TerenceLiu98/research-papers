## Overview

Research diversification concerns expansion or change in a researcher's topics and collaborators. Breadth at a point in time, departure from a prior research trajectory, and diversity of partners capture different dimensions. They can respond differently to restrictions on resources, and their effects on publication volume need not match their effects on research impact.

## Key Ideas

- **Horizontal diversification:** Breadth within a period, measured through topic counts or semantic dissimilarity among keywords. Counts depend on the topic model and the amount of published output.
- **Vertical diversification:** Departure from earlier research, measured through topic distances or abstract similarity across consecutive years. A brief exploratory episode is different from a sustained disciplinary transition.
- **Collaboration diversification:** Variation in institutional partners. Unique coauthor institutions divided by coauthor count measures institutional spread, not the persistence of particular ties or the balance of expertise.
- **Proposed productivity tradeoff:** Learning and coordination costs can reduce output, while new knowledge combinations may increase impact. This is a conditional mechanism, not a universal benefit of disruption.
- **Measurement limits:** Topic stability, embedding choices, and publication coverage affect observed diversification. Bibliometric impact gains do not independently establish novelty or improved scientific validity.
- **Causal limits:** A shock changing both topics and productivity does not establish that topic changes mediate its productivity effect. Funding, partner access, and selection into continued publication can also matter.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/science-under-sanctions-the-impact-of-the-entity-list-on-chinese-academic-research|Science under sanctions: The impact of the entity list on Chinese academic research]]: reports increased breadth and partner diversity after institutional sanctions, but annual topic deviation attenuates within three years while measured impact gains persist.
- Furman and Teodoridis (2020), "Automation, research technology, and researchers' trajectories: Evidence from computer science and electrical engineering": cited in the sanctions paper as evidence on disruption and exploratory research.
- Arts and Fleming (2018), "Paradise of novelty-or loss of human capital? Exploring new fields and inventive output": cited in the sanctions paper as a comparison on unfamiliar domains and knowledge recombination.
