## Overview

Interactive agenda setting is the exercise of influence over which issues or frames receive attention while people are talking. A participant sets an interaction's agenda by shifting discussion toward a preferred topic and inducing others to continue attending to it. The concept applies to structured settings such as debates and hearings as well as informal deliberations and online discussions.

## Key Ideas

- The agenda emerges turn by turn as participants negotiate both who speaks and what is discussed. This differs from macro-level agenda setting, which typically tracks issue attention across institutions, media, or time.
- A topic shift is evidence of agenda-setting behavior, but it is not sufficient by itself. Power also appears when other participants maintain attention on the shifted-to topic rather than immediately redirecting the discussion.
- Agenda-setting ability is relational and context dependent. It is most meaningful when comparing people competing over the same limited discussion time and topical space.
- Issue changes and reframing can both constitute agenda setting. Which form matters depends on the breadth, purpose, and norms of the interaction.
- Talk time, comment counts, and interruptions measure participation quantity, not control over topical attention. They need not identify the same participants as an agenda-setting measure.
- Agenda control can shape later decisions by placing issues on the table, excluding alternatives, or affecting which proposals receive collective consideration.
