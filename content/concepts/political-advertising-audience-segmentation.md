## Overview

Campaigns can deliver different issue emphases, partisan cues, or ideological presentations to different audiences. Segmentation concerns the relationship between message and audience, rather than simply the amount of advertising purchased. Narrow geographic reach can also reduce spending on viewers ineligible to vote in a contest.

## Key Ideas

- Compare messages within the same campaign to distinguish segmentation from differences between candidates and constituencies.
- Delivered audience demographics do not reveal the advertiser's intended targeting criteria: platform optimization can shape who sees each creative.
- More heterogeneous messaging can coexist with less total policy discussion. Issue diversity and issue prevalence measure different properties.
- Selective delivery may support mobilization of existing supporters, but message patterns alone do not establish effects on turnout, persuasion, or polarization.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/political-advertising-online-and-offline|Political Advertising Online and Offline]]: finds within-candidate differences between Facebook and television messaging and demographic variation in Facebook ad exposure in the 2018 US elections.

## Related Concepts

Segmentation links campaign message design to audience selection. It should be distinguished from advertising effectiveness, which requires evidence about how exposure changes recipients' attitudes or behavior.
