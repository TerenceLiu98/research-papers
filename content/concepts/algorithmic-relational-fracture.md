## Overview

Algorithmic relational fracture (ARF) is the acute sensemaking process initiated when a communication breakdown makes the tension between an AI's perceived social role and its non-sentient technical nature salient and difficult to reconcile. It describes the user's interpretive struggle, not merely the system error that triggered it.

## Key Ideas

- ARF arises in [[Relational AI]] interactions where interpersonal or identity goals make a failure consequential beyond task completion.
- Users interpret breakdowns through a flawed-partner frame (for example, amnesia, neglect, or rigidity) and a faulty-machine frame (for example, memory limits, context drift, model updates, or moderation constraints).
- These explanations can be nested. A personified shortcoming supplies the relational meaning of the failure while a technical constraint supplies its ultimate cause.
- Emotional neglect marks a boundary of technical reframing: in the originating study it did not show a discernible co-occurrence with technological explanations.
- The resulting attribution shapes [[Pseudo-Relational Accommodation]], including attempts to restore, detach from, or reaffirm the relationship.
