## Overview

Graphical preferences concern how the appearance of information affects its evaluation. A rising trajectory can attract approval even when the observer verbally prefers an outcome schedule that delivers benefits earlier. Such preferences must be distinguished from substantive preferences for the quantities represented.

## Key Ideas

- Holding totals constant can reveal a preference for sequence shape; calibrating its magnitude requires varying the compensation offered by an alternative.
- An adaptive plot staircase raises or lowers the benefit of a flat reference alternative in response to choices against a rising series. Halving adjustments progressively bounds the indifference threshold.
- The flat average equivalent measures the compensating flat-series level. Standardization by visible area depends on the vertical-axis baseline and need not represent a ratio of total benefits.
- A visual choice alone does not distinguish appearance from underlying outcome preferences. Comparisons with nongraphical elicitation help assess that distinction.
- Thresholds depend on fixed stimulus characteristics and tested bounds. They are not context-free personality traits or demonstrated behavioral effects.

## Related Concepts

Adaptive threshold elicitation supplies the measurement logic; convergent validity concerns agreement with other measures. Neither is equivalent to graphical preference itself.
