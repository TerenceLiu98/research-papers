## Overview

Voter issue salience is the importance a voter assigns to an issue when evaluating politics and parties. It concerns the priority of an issue, which is distinct from the voter's preferred policy position and from the emphasis a party gives that issue.

## Key Ideas

- **Priority can condition party evaluation.** Voters who prioritize an issue may put greater weight on proximity, competence, emphasis, and credible policy commitments on that issue.
- **Ambiguity can have heterogeneous consequences.** Han finds that expert-measured ambiguity on the economy or immigration is associated with lower party support among voters prioritizing that issue. The study does not find a significant benefit among other voters.
- **Measurement defines the interpretation.** Naming the country's most important problem identifies a top national priority. A binary indicator derived from that answer does not measure the full ranking or intensity of personal concerns; an unselected issue need not be unimportant.
- **Party emphasis and voter salience are different variables.** Their interaction tests whether parties emphasizing an issue receive stronger support from voters who prioritize it. Neither variable is equivalent to agreement on the desired policy.
- **Interaction coefficients require conditional interpretation.** In a model with ambiguity, salience, and their interaction, the ambiguity coefficient describes the reference salience group. Predicted probabilities clarify substantive magnitude in nonlinear models.
