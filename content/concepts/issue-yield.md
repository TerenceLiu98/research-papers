## Overview

Issue yield is the capacity of a policy to reconcile a party's protection of its existing support with expansion toward new voters. It explains selective issue emphasis through the joint distribution of policy preferences and party support.

## Key Ideas

- A bridge policy enjoys greater support than the party itself and is positively associated with the party's supporters. Broad popularity alone can instead create a venture policy that risks alienating the base.
- Pamper policies are positively associated with the party but have a smaller support base; dead-end policies combine smaller support with negative association.
- With policy support share \(i\), party support share \(p\), and joint support share \(f\), De Sio and Weber define scaled yield as \(Y=(f-ip)/[p(1-p)]+(i-p)/(1-p)\), for \(0<p<1\).
- Yield is party- and context-specific. The model assumes that changing issue emphasis is easier than changing policy positions and that campaign attention can influence voters' criteria of choice.
- A rival's ownership of an issue may inhibit exploitation of an otherwise favorable policy. High yield is an opportunity measure, not proof of credibility, causal persuasion, or future vote gains.
