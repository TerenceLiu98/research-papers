## Overview

Party fit concerns whether a prospective officeholder’s policy preferences align with the reputation of their prospective legislative party. It is relational and historically variable: moderation or extremism alone does not determine fit across all parties and periods.

## Key Ideas

- Candidates may use party reputation to assess both nomination prospects and their expected policy influence and career rewards after election.
- Poor fit can discourage candidacy through self-selection; recruitment and gatekeeping can reinforce this process. These mechanisms are difficult to separate empirically.
- A more ideologically heterogeneous delegation can provide potential allies for candidates outside its center.
- Selective entry can reinforce existing polarization without explaining why parties initially polarized.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/ideological-moderates-wont-run-how-party-fit-matters-for-partisan-polarization-in-congress|Ideological Moderates Won’t Run: How Party Fit Matters for Partisan Polarization in Congress]]: introduces the framework and examines state legislators’ perceptions and congressional entry. The 1998 survey supports the predicted relationship for Republicans but not Democrats; 2000–2010 entry models show lower entry among moderates in both parties.
