## Overview

Candidate attractiveness is a viewer's assessment of a politician's physical appeal. It is distinct from perceived competence, general favorability, and vote choice. Although often studied through static photographs, it can also respond to the selection and editing of video footage, music, filters, and presentation.

## Key Ideas

- **Appearance is mediated.** Viewers encounter selected representations of candidates. Archival footage and audiovisual editing can change the impression conveyed without changing the politician's physical features.
- **Attractiveness and support are separate outcomes.** In Munger and Li's TikTok experiment, both selected Biden edits increased attractiveness ratings but neither significantly increased favorability. An appearance effect therefore does not by itself demonstrate political persuasion or electoral advantage.
- **Ratings reflect the viewer as well as the image.** Partisanship and demographic characteristics correlate with attractiveness judgments in the study's control group. Those associations do not identify causal effects of viewer characteristics.
- **Genre effects require appropriate comparisons.** A contrast between two selected edits does not isolate music, filtering, footage, or production quality. Generalizing to a genre requires broader stimulus sampling or designs that manipulate specific features.
- **Context matters for measurement.** Attractive celebrity control videos can supply a comparison standard. Absolute ratings and treatment contrasts answer different questions, and immediate ratings do not establish durable attitudes.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/thirst-traps-and-quick-cuts-the-effects-of-tiktok-edits-on-evaluations-of-politicians|Thirst Traps and Quick Cuts: The Effects of TikTok "Edits" on Evaluations of Politicians]]: randomized exposure to selected political edits increases attractiveness ratings, with different patterns for favorability.
- Giacomin and Rule (2020), "How static facial cues relate to real-world leaders' success: A review and meta-analysis": cited by Munger and Li as background on appearance and leadership evaluations.
- Laustsen (2014), "Decomposing the relationship between candidates' facial appearance and electoral success": cited by Munger and Li for distinguishing facial competence and attractiveness.
