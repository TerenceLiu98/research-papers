## Overview

Publication bias makes accessible research differ systematically from the research conducted. Selection can occur during manuscript production, submission, review, and editorial decisions. Evidence about one stage does not automatically measure selection across the entire process.

## Key Ideas

- **File drawer bias:** significant findings receive more favorable publication treatment than nonsignificant findings of otherwise comparable quality.
- **Repeat study bias:** an original study receives more favorable treatment than an otherwise equivalent replication.
- **Gotcha bias:** a replication receives more favorable treatment when it contradicts an earlier result than when it confirms one, holding the replication result fixed.
- **Joint selection matters:** preferences for contradiction and significance can reinforce or counteract one another depending on the original result.
- **Specify the denominator:** significance among published studies under a true null is distinct from the fraction of significant published findings whose null hypotheses are true.
