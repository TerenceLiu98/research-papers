## Overview

Hostile sexism is one dimension of the Ambivalent Sexism Inventory. It captures antagonistic beliefs about women, distinct from benevolent sexism's protective and idealizing justifications of traditional gender relations. The distinction matters because the dimensions can predict different outcomes.

## Key Ideas

- Measurement should match the intended outcome: evidence for predicting gender-discriminatory choices does not exhaust the meaning or consequences of sexism.
- Strong common-factor loadings, prediction of relevant behavior, and limited overlap with political outcomes provide complementary item-selection criteria.
- Short batteries reduce survey burden but can compress variation at the extremes. Item information from [[concepts/item-response-theory|Item Response Theory]] helps characterize this tradeoff.
- Lower scores need not mean simple indifference: in the linked study they are associated with preference for female profiles, motivating careful interpretation of the scale's full range.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/optimizing-the-measurement-of-sexism-in-political-surveys|Optimizing the Measurement of Sexism in Political Surveys]]: evaluates hostile, benevolent, and modern sexism measures and recommends two- to five-item hostile batteries for efficient measurement of prejudice toward women.
