## Overview

AI-mediated communication (AI-MC) is interpersonal communication between people in which a computational agent acts on behalf of a communicator by modifying, augmenting, or generating messages to accomplish communication or interpersonal goals. Unlike conventional computer-mediated communication, the system is not only a channel: it participates in producing the message.

## Key Ideas

- AI involvement varies in magnitude from minor corrections or suggestions to complete message generation, and can operate across text, audio, images, and video.
- An AI-MC system can optimize for goals such as efficiency, clarity, attractiveness, trustworthiness, humor, or status. The selected objective can shape language and social norms at scale.
- Autonomy ranges from suggestions that a sender accepts or edits to agents that communicate with little supervision. Role orientation distinguishes systems acting for senders from those acting for receivers.
- AI intervention complicates perceived agency and responsibility. Receivers may attribute a message to the human sender, the computational agent, or some combination of the two.
- Disclosure is context-dependent: revealing routine autocorrection may add little value, while undisclosed synthetic speech or strategically generated self-presentation can create deception and manipulation risks.
- AI-MC is narrower than all algorithmically mediated communication. It excludes ranking and recommendation systems that only indirectly support communication, as well as human-agent interaction where the AI itself is the partner, such as [[Relational AI]].
