## Overview

Public value failure describes institutional arrangements in which markets or public organizations fail to provide goods, services, or processes sufficient to realize public values. In the public values tradition associated with Bozeman (2007), performance is judged against commitments such as accountability, equity, transparency, and responsiveness, rather than efficiency alone.

## Key Ideas

- **Specify the missing value.** A diagnosis should identify the obligation that is inadequately supplied and the institutional mechanism responsible. A faster service can still fail to provide meaningful reasons or a route to contest its decisions.
- **Formal equality and substantive equity differ.** Uniform rules can improve consistency while overlooking morally relevant differences among cases. Aggregate predictive performance does not settle whether individuals receive equitable treatment.
- **Automation redistributes discretion and responsibility.** Moving decisions from officials into systems can disperse accountability among designers, vendors, and deploying agencies. Anastasopoulos applies the framework to accountability, fairness and equity, individual consideration, and reason-giving.
- **Human discretion can also fail.** Bias, arbitrariness, and inconsistent judgment can undersupply public values. The framework evaluates institutional arrangements, including human, automated, and hybrid processes.
- **Safeguards must address the diagnosed mechanism.** Review authority, understandable reasons, appeal procedures, and explicit responsibility address different failures. Their presence on paper does not establish effectiveness in practice.

- [[papers/artificial-intelligence-bureaucratic-discretion-and-democratic-administration|Artificial Intelligence, Bureaucratic Discretion, and Democratic Administration]]: develops a normative account of four value clusters threatened by replacing human discretion and proposes institutionally protected human review.
- Schiff, Schiff, and Pierson (2022), "Assessing Public Value Failure in Government Adoption of Artificial Intelligence": an earlier application cited by Anastasopoulos; its results are not independently assessed here.
