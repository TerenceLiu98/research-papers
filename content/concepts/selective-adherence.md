## Overview

Selective adherence describes biased use of advice: decision-makers give recommendations more weight when they fit stereotypes about the decision subject. It can occur with human or algorithmic advice and can preserve discrimination even when people frequently override recommendations overall.

## Key Ideas

- The mechanism concerns how advice is used, distinct from bias in how an algorithm generates its outputs.
- Holding advice constant while varying a subject’s group cue tests differential reliance. Separately varying the advice source tests whether an algorithm amplifies that pattern.
- General reluctance to follow algorithms does not rule out stereotype-consistent reliance on particular recommendations.
- Evidence is context-dependent. Alon-Barkat and Busuioc find a citizen-sample ethnicity effect under a directional test but a reversed effect among civil servants surveyed after a major discrimination scandal. The latter does not establish that professional status or scandal exposure caused the reversal.
