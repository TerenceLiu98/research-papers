## Overview

Partisan residential segregation describes how members of political parties are separated across residential space. It concerns opportunities for contact with opposing partisans, and can occur both between cities and among neighbours within the same city or census tract.

## Key Ideas

- Distance-weighted exposure averages nearby residents' group membership, giving closer neighbours greater influence. Isolation measures exposure to one's own group.
- Exposure and isolation need not be complements when some residents belong to neither focal party.
- Comparing one party's exposure with the other party's isolation within the same area reveals local clustering concealed by aggregate party shares.
- Spatial proximity measures potential contact rather than actual interaction. Residential patterns alone do not identify partisan housing preferences or effects on polarization.
- Racial segregation and residential partisan segregation can overlap without being equivalent.
