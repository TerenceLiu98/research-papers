## Overview

Anchoring vignettes provide shared hypothetical cases that help separate scale interpretation from substantive differences in reported judgments. Their value depends on respondents perceiving the described cases comparably and applying compatible response rules to vignettes and substantive targets.

## Key Ideas

- Identical questions alone do not ensure comparable answers: respondents can map the same judgment onto different response categories.
- Described policy profiles can anchor ideological scales while leaving perceptions of real politicians free to vary across respondents.
- In a shift-and-stretch model, vignette ratings estimate individual intercepts and slopes; applying the inverse transformation to target ratings produces adjusted perceptions.
- Common perception is relocated to the vignettes, not eliminated. Profiles should span the substantive dimension and support the intended comparisons.

## Important Papers

- King et al. (2004), “Enhancing the validity and cross-cultural comparability of measurement in survey research”: foundational application cited by Jessee.
