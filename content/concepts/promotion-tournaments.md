## Overview

Promotion tournaments allocate scarce higher offices through competition among officials whose career prospects depend partly on relative performance. They connect individual career incentives to policy choices: an official may seek to improve their own jurisdiction's outcomes while avoiding actions that improve a competitor's position. In the Chinese setting studied by Fang, Li, and Wu, provincial personnel control makes prefectural mayors within a province a relevant comparison group.

## Key Ideas

- **Relative standing matters.** A policy that benefits two jurisdictions can be privately unattractive to an official if the resulting gain to the rival reduces the official's promotion probability. The efficiency of a tournament therefore depends on interactions between jurisdictions as well as incentives to improve local performance.
- **Spillovers can encourage exclusion.** In Fang, Li, and Wu's model, selecting a rival city's projects improves that rival's short-run performance. Promotion incentives and positive spillovers jointly produce [[concepts/local-protectionism|Local Protectionism]], even though selecting higher-quality outside projects would improve long-run outcomes.
- **Close contests intensify distortion under stated assumptions.** With identical project-quality distributions and symmetric unimodal performance noise, cross-city allocation is lowest when officials have equal prior strengths. The empirical proxy is the absolute difference in predicted promotion probabilities; a smaller difference means stronger competition.
- **Affinity and career horizons alter incentives.** Valuing a connected rival's promotion reduces exclusion in the model. Increasing the weight on one's own promotion increases it. The model predicts opposite signs for their interactions with the strength gap without fixing the individual signs in advance.
- **Political objectives can affect firms.** Firms that place weight on their home official's utility inherit part of the promotion incentive. Stronger investment associations for local SOEs than for central SOEs are consistent with this channel, but do not directly measure the firms' preferences.
- **Empirical proxies require care.** Predicted promotion probabilities combine personal, political, and economic covariates. Fixed-effects associations with subsequent allocations need not isolate a causal effect of competition if appointments and economic conditions change together.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/tournament-style-political-competition-and-local-protectionism-theory-and-evidence-from-china|Tournament-style political competition and local protectionism: Theory and evidence from China]]: develops a project-selection model and tests its predictions with Chinese procurement and equity-investment records.
- Lazear and Rosen (1981), "Rank-order tournaments as optimum labor contracts": the general tournament foundation cited by Fang, Li, and Wu.
- Li and Zhou (2005), "Political turnover and economic performance: the incentive role of personnel control in China": a cited foundation for the political-career application.
