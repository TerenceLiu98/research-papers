## Overview

An ergodic scale-free random environment is a random cell configuration whose law is compatible with translations and rescaling, and whose translation- and scale-invariant observables are deterministic. In planar-map applications, cells cover the plane and their adjacency graph carries a random walk or conductance structure. These assumptions support large-scale averaging despite highly variable local geometry.

## Key Ideas

- Translation invariance modulo scaling describes how a uniformly sampled location can be recentered and rescaled without changing the law of the environment.
- Ergodicity modulo scaling turns scale-invariant random quantities into deterministic constants, enabling spatial and multiscale averages.
- Moment conditions control cell diameter, area, degree, and conductance strongly enough for invariance principles and geometric estimates.
- Connectivity along macroscopic horizontal and vertical line segments prevents large-scale bottlenecks in the cell adjacency graph.
- Under suitable assumptions, random walks on the cell configuration converge in the quenched sense to planar Brownian motion, possibly after time parametrization.
- The paper [[Circle Packing and Riemann Uniformization of Random Planar Maps in an Ergodic Scale-Free Environment]] uses this framework to compare abstract cells with circle-packing and Riemann-uniformization embeddings.
