## Overview

Riemann uniformization of a planar map replaces each face by a regular polygon and glues matching edges isometrically. The resulting surface carries a conformal structure. When the surface is parabolic, a conformal map sends it to the complex plane and provides a geometric embedding of the map's vertices and edges.

## Key Ideas

- A face of degree $p$ is represented by a regular $p$-gon, so the combinatorial map becomes a piecewise-Euclidean Riemann surface.
- Parabolicity means that the surface is conformally equivalent to the complex plane; hyperbolicity means it is conformally equivalent to the unit disk.
- Semi-flowers around vertices provide local regions whose conformal images can be controlled using Koebe distortion estimates.
- Length-area arguments transfer cell diameter and degree moment bounds into macroscopic continuity estimates for the conformal map.
- In the paper [[Circle Packing and Riemann Uniformization of Random Planar Maps in an Ergodic Scale-Free Environment]], a harmonic corrector and ergodic Dirichlet-energy arguments show that the conformal embedding agrees with the original cell configuration up to a deterministic area-preserving linear map and sublinear error.
