## Overview

Crowd turbulence is uncontrolled collective motion produced when dense body contacts transmit and amplify physical forces. Individuals may be displaced in several directions even when they are trying to remain still. The mechanism explains how dangerous crowd dynamics can emerge without intentional aggression or a breakdown of social cooperation.

## Key Ideas

- **Contact forces dominate.** At extreme densities, intended walking is less important than pressure transmitted between bodies and boundaries. Models of cognitive route choice alone do not represent this physical regime.
- **Stress release produces irregular motion.** Force chains build and release unevenly, moving groups of people in ways that individuals cannot readily anticipate or counteract.
- **Falls can cascade.** A fallen person creates space and disrupts the balance of forces around them, causing further loss of balance and potentially trapping people on the ground.
- **Geometry conditions the dynamics.** Bottlenecks, merging streams, crossing flows, and temporary obstacles can concentrate density and pressure. Average venue density can conceal dangerous local conditions.
- **Monitoring has scale limits.** Large-scale smartphone flow estimates and fine-scale measurements of crowd pressure answer different questions. Density ranges reported in particular studies should not be treated as universal safety cutoffs.
