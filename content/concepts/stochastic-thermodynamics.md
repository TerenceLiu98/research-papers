## Overview

Stochastic thermodynamics extends thermodynamic reasoning to small or fluctuating systems modeled as stochastic processes, especially continuous-time Markov jump processes. It defines entropy production and related quantities along individual trajectories and at the ensemble level, making irreversibility measurable even when a system is far from equilibrium.

## Key Ideas

- A trajectory-level entropy production compares the probability of a forward trajectory with that of an appropriately time-reversed trajectory. Its average is a Kullback-Leibler divergence and is therefore non-negative.
- Local detailed balance relates the ratio of forward and reverse transition rates to entropy changes and reaction-specific affinities. Microscopic reversibility is the structural condition that makes the comparison well-defined.
- Fluctuation theorems connect the probabilities of positive and negative entropy-producing trajectories. The integral form implies second-law inequalities after averaging.
- Ensemble descriptions use probability currents and dynamical activity. Currents are time-antisymmetric, whereas activity measures the total rate of jumps and is time-symmetric.
- The framework does not require every application to use a literal energy interpretation. In social imitation, combinatorial internal entropy and reaction biases can play the corresponding mathematical roles while opinion changes replace physical fluxes.
- Coarse-graining introduces an internal-entropy contribution based on the number of microscopic configurations represented by a mesostate. On complex networks, this contribution depends on the chosen mesoscopic variables.
