## Overview

The Theory of Competing Networks studies how interconnected networks compete for eigenvector centrality, interpreted as influence or a process-dependent resource. It combines spectral properties of network structure with strategic choices about connections. Its application to geopolitics requires a justified mapping from countries, blocs, and relationships to the modeled networks; centrality is not automatically an empirical measure of national power.

## Key Ideas

- **Network strength and importance differ.** The largest eigenvalue of a network's adjacency matrix measures its strength in this framework. The associated eigenvector assigns node centralities; their sum within a constituent network measures its importance in the combined system.
- **Connector location matters.** Links between highly central nodes can accelerate dynamics, whereas peripheral connectors can act as bottlenecks. Counting cross-network links alone does not capture these effects.
- **Cooperation has a strength condition.** In the network-level connection game summarized by Aguirre and colleagues (2026), weaker networks can sustain a cooperative equilibrium when their coalition becomes stronger than the strongest competitor. The reported resistance to unilateral reversal concerns the modeled configuration, not all forms of cooperation.
- **Relative and absolute gains differ.** A dominant network can lose relative share while benefiting from greater system-wide growth. Applying this argument to economic welfare requires evidence beyond centrality rankings.
- **The decision-making level changes the outcome.** When individual nodes of two networks form links for their own benefit, peripheral nodes can initiate integration before the original hubs connect and dominate the merged core. Cooperation between networks and hierarchy inside an integrated network are distinct outcomes.
- **Representation bounds interpretation.** Financial, military, trade, and cultural ties may yield different or overlapping networks. The 2026 geopolitical application treats these choices as a reason to use the framework as a conceptual lens rather than a calibrated forecast.
