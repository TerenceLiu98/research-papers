## Overview

Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies combines quantitative analysis of text collections with contextual interpretation of discourse. Frequency patterns guide close reading, while concordance evidence helps explain how recurring language constructs identities, priorities, and social meanings.

## Key Ideas

- Keywords are salient relative to a reference corpus; reference selection and temporal alignment shape the contrast.
- Collocations identify recurrent associations, while concordance lines recover their local meanings. Association thresholds should be paired with frequency cutoffs to reduce rare-event artifacts.
- Diachronic analysis distinguishes stable vocabulary from changing semantic contexts. A recurring word can acquire different policy meanings over time.
- Coding reliability supports consistency of categorization, but does not by itself establish an interpretation's validity or audience effects.
- Corpus construction, translation, and selection rules bound what the analysis can reveal.
