## Overview

Legislative effectiveness concerns a lawmaker's demonstrated ability to advance their agenda through the legislative process into law. It is narrower than general political influence or quality of representation: oversight, constituency service, voting, and obstruction are not directly captured by a sponsorship-based measure.

## Key Ideas

- **Measure progress and significance.** State Legislative Effectiveness Scores combine five stages, from introduction to enactment, with commemorative, substantive, and substantive-and-significant categories weighted 1, 5, and 10.
- **Interpret normalization carefully.** SLES averages legislators' shares of weighted activity at each stage and rescales to a chamber-term mean of 1. It captures relative effectiveness within an institutional setting, not an absolute scale of lawmaking skill across settings.
- **Separate individual scores from group gaps.** Majority-minority, chair-rank-and-file, and senior-freshman differences can reveal how influence is distributed. Equal chamber averages by construction cannot establish equal total productivity across legislatures.
- **Use multiple validation checks.** Hand-coded bill histories, expert rankings, stability across terms, and expected institutional associations address different measurement questions. No single check establishes that a score isolates personal ability.
- **Keep causal interpretation separate.** Institutional positions, agenda choices, and resources jointly shape legislative success. Associations between rules and score gaps do not themselves identify effects of changing those rules.
