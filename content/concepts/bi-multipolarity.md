## Overview

Bi-multipolarity is Rosecrance's proposed international system combining two dominant powers with an autonomous multipolar sphere. The superpowers partly cooperate to restrain external conflict, while other states buffer their rivalry and resist incorporation into either camp. It describes an arrangement of mixed interests and reciprocal constraints, not simply an intermediate number of poles.

## Key Ideas

- **Mutual regulation:** dominant powers constrain conflicts among other states; those states provide mediation, buffers, and alternative outlets for superpower competition.
- **Partial cooperation:** superpowers share interests in preventing disruptive external conflict but continue to oppose each other's predominance. Smaller powers likewise combine local rivalry with resistance to great-power encroachment.
- **Durable autonomy:** if external states were entirely absorbed into two camps, the arrangement would revert to the strict bipolar confrontation it seeks to moderate.
- **Limited detente:** too little accommodation risks renewed central confrontation; complete superpower unity could generate a new antagonism between the dominant pair and the remaining states.
- **Conditional stability:** Rosecrance predicts fewer conflicts than under multipolarity and less destructive outcomes than under bipolarity. These are theoretical claims requiring both restraint and continuing influence, not measured effects.
- **Nuclear diffusion:** proliferation can increase the severity of existing conflicts. The proposed advantage depends partly on the superpowers' capacity to limit diffusion or its disruptive consequences.
