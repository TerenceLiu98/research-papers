## Overview

Punctuated equilibrium theory explains policy dynamics through a contrast between extended stability and occasional major shifts. Distributional assessments commonly look for many changes near zero alongside a few large changes.

## Key Ideas

- Policy budgets and other stages of the policy process provide change observations for assessing punctuation.
- Kurtosis and L-kurtosis summarize distributional shape, but ordinary kurtosis is strongly influenced by extreme observations and should not simply be interpreted as peakedness.
- Inequality in absolute changes offers a complementary interpretation: how much of total change occurs in how few time units.
- A distributional summary does not preserve event order or independently identify the mechanism responsible for large changes.

## Important Papers
