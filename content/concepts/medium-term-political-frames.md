## Overview

Medium-term political frames connect historical experience to strategic choice by shaping actors’ understandings of the choices available. They can persist as circumstances change, yet be revised when shocks undermine established interpretations. Their explanatory role concerns the construction of perceived options as well as preferences among options.

## Key Ideas

- **Persistence:** An established interpretation can constrain responses even after the conditions that produced it have changed.
- **Formation:** Political opportunities, organizational constituencies, historical precedents, and competition among influential advocates can jointly shape frames.
- **Interaction:** Actors’ demands also influence their opponents’ expectations, so frames can help constitute the opportunity environment.
- **Distinct reform paths:** State pluralism emphasizes representation in state institutions; societal corporatism emphasizes independent social organizations and state recognition. These are alternative orientations within Renwick’s account, not interchangeable labels for democratization.
- **Evidence:** Historical claims require evidence of earlier discourse, continuity into later choices, and consideration of structural and immediate-context explanations.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/why-hungary-and-poland-differed-in-1989|Renwick (2006)]]: compares Hungary’s institution-centered opposition framework with Solidarity’s emphasis on social and trade-union pluralism to explain divergent transition settlements.
