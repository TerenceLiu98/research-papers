## Overview

Media ecology studies communication as an interdependent environment rather than as isolated media effects. It examines how media forms, platforms, organizations, technologies, audiences, and political contexts interact, and how different components can perform complementary functions within the same information environment.

## Key Ideas

- A platform is part of a wider ecology that includes producers, outlets, formats, audiences, algorithms, institutions, and competing media.
- Platform affordances are not equivalent to platform effects. A mode such as video or text can interact with outlet goals and editorial practices, so cross-platform differences should not be attributed to one feature without comparison or qualification.
- Media organizations can distribute different functions across platforms. Informational formats may reach broad audiences, while partisan or emotionally intense formats may deepen identification and mobilization.
- Media ecologies change over time as organizations adapt content to platform conventions. Digital versions of older formats may be edited, shortened, or reframed for a new platform.
- Multimethod research is useful because content analysis can study production while surveys can examine audience exposure and outcomes; neither source alone captures the full ecology.
