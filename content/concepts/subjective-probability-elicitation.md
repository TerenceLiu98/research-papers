## Overview

Subjective probability elicitation measures uncertainty alongside a central expectation. An elicited distribution reflects both the respondent’s judgments and the measurement procedure, including wording, response interfaces, and distributional fitting assumptions.

## Key Ideas

- Probability-based methods hold thresholds fixed and ask for probabilities; value-based methods hold probabilities fixed and ask for quantiles. Hybrid formats elicit both.
- Elicitation protocols distinguish setup, elicitation, fitting, and adequacy assessment. A fitted distribution need not be directly visible to the respondent.
- Parametric fitting reconstructs a full distribution from sparse answers, but restricts the shapes that can be recovered.
- Known experimental benchmarks allow accuracy comparisons. Naturally held beliefs generally lack an observable distributional ground truth.
- Individual uncertainty and differences in expectations across people are distinct; an aggregate fitted distribution should not automatically be interpreted as either one alone.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/eliciting-beliefs-as-distributions-in-online-surveys|Eliciting Beliefs as Distributions in Online Surveys]] compares five online formats and finds the Manski hybrid best overall across four beta benchmarks, with quantile elicitation strongest in symmetric conditions.

## Related Concepts

Direct elicitation measures stated probabilities; market-based aggregation instead combines beliefs through trades and incentives. These procedures need not recover the same object.
