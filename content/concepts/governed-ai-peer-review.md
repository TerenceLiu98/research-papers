## Overview

Governed AI peer review is an institutionally controlled workflow in which a journal or conference supplies an AI assessment as transparent, contestable input to human review. The institution defines the AI's scope, protects manuscript confidentiality, records how the system is used, and preserves human authority over publication decisions. This differs from shadow AI, where reviewers privately use general-purpose tools with unobserved prompts, data practices, and reliance.

## Key Ideas

- **AI output is diagnostic, not dispositive.** The AI can conduct scoped checks such as reporting completeness, clarity, or methodological consistency, but should not issue accept-or-reject recommendations. Humans retain responsibility for novelty, contribution, impact, and final judgment.
- **Authors need a right of response.** Giving authors the AI report and collecting a short clarification before human review makes errors contestable and can help reviewers resolve flagged issues efficiently without requiring a full revision.
- **Shared assistance creates shared error.** When every reviewer sees the same AI report, their judgments may become correlated through anchoring or complacency. Additional reviewers cannot diversify away this common component, so useful assistance requires [[concepts/calibrated-reliance|Calibrated Reliance]].
- **Governance makes effects measurable.** Structured logs and randomized holdouts can estimate AI reliability, reviewer dependence, productivity effects, decision shifts, and effects on novel or unconventional submissions. The same quantities are difficult to observe under private AI use.
- **Task allocation is an emphasis, not a rigid partition.** AI can emphasize auditable checks while humans emphasize value-laden judgments, but overlap is unavoidable and the allocation should change with evidence about model capabilities.
- **Operational gains are conditional.** AI may reduce reviewer time or permit smaller panels when its precision and productivity benefits exceed induced correlation and workflow costs. These benefits cannot be inferred from standalone model quality alone.
