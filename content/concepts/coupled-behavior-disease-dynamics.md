## Overview

Protective behavior responds to infection risk, costs, and others' choices. Transmission then changes because people alter their exposure or infectious contacts. Modeling both processes together can explain outcomes that fixed-compliance epidemic models cannot represent.

## Key Ideas

- **Endogenous protection:** compliance or intervention uptake evolves alongside health compartments rather than remaining a fixed input.
- **Feedback through risk:** successful protection can weaken incentives to maintain it, creating renewed susceptibility to transmission.
- **Distinct time scales:** behavioral adaptation and infection dynamics can proceed at different rates, changing transient peaks and long-run outcomes.
- **Contact heterogeneity:** degree affects exposure and therefore the relative importance of protection costs and benefits.
- **Private and collective incentives:** preventing transmission benefits others, so individually preferred compliance can fall below the socially preferred level.
- **Model-dependent waves:** repeated infection peaks generated by behavioral feedback establish a possible mechanism; real outbreak attribution requires additional evidence.
