## Overview

Party issue salience measures relative emphasis rather than a party’s preferred policy direction. Its interpretation depends on the communication arena: manifesto emphasis records a party-controlled agenda, while media coverage incorporates journalists’ selection, competing actors, and events.

## Key Ideas

- Positions and priorities require separate validation. Sources can agree about a party’s stance while disagreeing about how prominently it features an issue.
- Pooled agreement across domains can coexist with weak within-domain agreement. Generally prominent issues can dominate both sources without preserving the relative priorities of individual parties.
- Absolute log ratios compare proportional discrepancies symmetrically, but exclude zero values unless an additional treatment is specified.
- Party salience differs from [[concepts/voter-issue-salience|Voter Issue Salience]], which concerns voters’ priorities rather than party messages or media associations.

Cardinal relative salience weights sum to a fixed budget over a specified issue set. Adding or removing an issue changes the normalization of remaining weights, so measurements are not separable across issues. Ordinal rankings avoid requiring cardinal distances. Manifesto emphasis and expert judgments of underlying priorities remain distinct operationalizations.

Internal unity can constrain issue emphasis. Lehmann finds that both candidate preference heterogeneity and expert-perceived public divisions are associated with lower party issue salience. Avoidance is less pronounced when the issue is highly salient across the party system, suggesting limits to parties’ control over their agendas. Expert assessments may register public infighting that party-controlled manifestos deemphasize. These observational associations do not identify deliberate avoidance independently of other consequences of divisions.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/keeping-the-team-together-how-intra-party-divisions-shape-party-behavior-across-issues|Keeping the team together]]: compares issue avoidance, position change, and blurring across eight issues; avoidance receives the most consistent support with both expert and candidate measures of divisions.

- [[papers/measuring-party-positions-and-issue-salience-with-mass-media-and-manifesto-data|Measuring party positions and issue salience with mass media and manifesto data]]: pooled salience correlation is 0.60, compared with 0.18–0.38 within the four issue domains; niche parties show greater relative divergence in the full regression.

- [[papers/measuring-issue-salience-for-political-parties-using-llms|Measuring issue salience for political parties using LLMs]]: budgeted holistic scoring improves agreement with expert judgments, while disagreement between manifesto-based and expert measures highlights the importance of source choice.
