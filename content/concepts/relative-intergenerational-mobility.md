## Overview
Relative intergenerational mobility describes the openness of status attainment across generations, distinguished from absolute mobility generated by changes in the distribution of occupations or other social positions.

## Key Ideas
- Weaker association between parental and offspring status indicates greater relative mobility.
- A regional rank–rank slope measures persistence; one minus that slope provides an openness measure in the study below. Estimates depend on the status measure and sampled population.
- Regional openness is conceptually distinct from an individual's upward or downward transition. It can shape expectations even among people who remain in their origin class.
