## Overview

Survey professionalism describes sustained, intensive survey participation. It concerns participation behavior rather than an intrinsic classification of respondents as inattentive or dishonest. Multiple-panel membership, survey counts, and browsing-based activity capture related but different aspects of that behavior.

## Key Ideas

- Survey-site visits can include finding questionnaires, registering, and collecting rewards. A visit count is not a completed-survey count.
- Absolute activity and activity relative to total browsing produce different classifications; thresholds should be reported explicitly.
- Professionalism can coexist with stable responses. Speeding, straightlining, and repeated participation require separate measurement.
- Cross-platform browsing can reveal activity hidden from individual panel providers, but donation selection and incomplete device coverage constrain inference.
- Repeated questionnaire visits indicate potential attempts, not necessarily successful duplicate completions or deliberate fraud.
