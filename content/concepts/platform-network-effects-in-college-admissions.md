## Overview

Platform network effects in college admissions arise when a shared application becomes more useful as more institutions participate. Once a student has completed the platform's common components, adding another member college may require less effort than applying outside the platform. Membership can therefore affect not only a college's own accessibility but also how the surrounding network channels applications and enrollment.

## Key Ideas

- **Membership changes marginal application cost.** The platform reuses information across institutions, so the value of joining can rise with the number and variety of other member colleges.
- **Network size is an empirical moderator, not proof by itself.** A larger adoption effect when total membership is high is consistent with a network mechanism, but platform design, digitization, and secular changes can also vary over time.
- **Local penetration can reveal pathways.** If a joining college disproportionately gains students from states where many colleges already use the platform, that pattern is more specific than an aggregate association with network size.
- **Effects need not move together.** A larger network may increase applications and geographic reach without producing the same moderation in yield, admits, racial composition, or other outcomes.
- **Distribution matters.** Networks can integrate markets geographically while increasing sorting on income or academic preparation. Market integration and equal access are distinct claims.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/reducing-frictions-in-college-admissions-evidence-from-the-common-application|Reducing Frictions in College Admissions: Evidence from the Common Application]]: Relates Common Application effects to total membership and source-state penetration, finding stronger application and out-of-state enrollment effects in larger or more penetrated networks.
- Rysman (2009), "The Economics of Two-Sided Markets": Reviews platform feedback mechanisms and the empirical challenges of identifying network effects.
- Hoxby (2000), "The Effects of Geographic Integration and Increasing Competition in the Market for College Education": Analyzes the broader integration of the US higher-education market.
