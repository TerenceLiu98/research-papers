## Overview
Child-centered familism describes family practices organized around children's well-being, development, and future status, with substantial emotional and material resources directed toward younger generations.

## Key Ideas
- Fertility and parenting decisions are connected: anticipated educational investment and status competition can influence desired family size.
- Intensive investment in child quality can coexist with low fertility.
- Greater opportunity may increase optimism about children's prospects and reduce perceived competitive parenting burdens. This is a proposed mechanism, not a universal prediction across family cultures.
