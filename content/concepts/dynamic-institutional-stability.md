## Overview

Dynamic institutional stability describes persistence when an institutional state determines both current payoffs and the political power used to choose future states. A proposed change is evaluated by the stable destination it triggers, not only by its immediate benefits. An apparently attractive reform can therefore be blocked because it transfers authority to a coalition that will enact a later, less attractive change.

## Key Ideas

- **States combine outcomes and rules.** Each state specifies individual payoffs and the coalitions empowered to replace it. Moving between states can alter both welfare and control over subsequent decisions.
- **Stability is forward-looking.** A state can persist even when a winning coalition prefers an alternative in a one-step comparison. What matters is whether that alternative leads to a stable destination the coalition prefers.
- **Outcome mappings depend on initial conditions.** A mapping $\phi(s)$ assigns each initial state its eventual stable state. Unlike a single global equilibrium selection, different starting institutions may converge to different fixed points.
- **Stable alternatives discipline selection.** If the selected outcome differs from the status quo, it must be supported by a currently winning coalition and must itself be stable. No other reachable stable alternative may defeat both the status quo and the selected destination from the current state's perspective.
- **Instability can stabilize the status quo.** A reform that everyone prefers can be rejected when it is only an unstable intermediate step toward a state opposed by those currently in power. Dynamic stability and Pareto efficiency therefore need not coincide.
- **Equilibrium equivalence is conditional.** In the finite-state framework of Acemoglu, Egorov, and Sonin, the axiomatic mapping coincides with pure-strategy Markov perfect equilibrium outcomes for sufficiently patient agents, small transition costs, suitable protocols, and acyclic collective preferences.
- **Ordered preferences make acyclicity tractable.** Single-crossing preferences with monotonic quasi-median voters, or single-peaked preferences with sufficiently intersecting winning coalitions, provide useful sufficient conditions rather than universal guarantees.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/dynamics-and-stability-of-constitutions-coalitions-and-clubs|Dynamics and Stability of Constitutions, Coalitions, and Clubs]]: develops the axiomatic recursion, proves its noncooperative equilibrium equivalence, and applies it to political participation.
- Chwe (1994), "Farsighted Coalitional Stability": an antecedent based on state transitions and cooperative stability concepts.
- Gomes and Jehiel (2005), "Dynamic Processes of Social and Economic Interactions: On the Persistence of Inefficiencies": a related dynamic account of bargaining position and inefficiency.
