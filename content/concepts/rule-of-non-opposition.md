## Overview

The rule of non-opposition permits a proposal to pass whenever nobody maintains a blocking objection. A participant may actively support the proposal or stand aside. Consequently, passage alone does not identify unanimous endorsement or shared preferences.

## Key Ideas

- Standing aside may reflect mild support with costly public endorsement, reluctant acceptance, or opposition restrained by the value of future cooperation.
- Proposal accommodation changes the demand before the response stage. It can lead to unanimous support for a weaker proposal, so accommodation and standing aside are distinct mechanisms.
- When every participant can block passage, the most restrictive acceptance threshold can constrain the proposal even if no veto is observed.
- Frequent passage does not establish that prescribed punishments remain optimal after disagreement. Credibility requires checking incentives at those decision points as well as during ordinary play.
- Process records of amendments, stand-asides, active support, and maintained objections narrow interpretations that a binary adoption record leaves open. Adoption does not establish implementation.
