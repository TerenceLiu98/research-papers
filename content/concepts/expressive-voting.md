## Overview

Expressive voting treats casting a ballot as a source of value through expressing identity or affiliation, beyond its instrumental chance of changing an election result. In the account considered by Fraga, Moskowitz, and Schneer, supporting a likely winning party can provide greater expressive benefits than supporting a likely loser, even when neither vote is likely to be pivotal.

## Key Ideas

- **Participation without pivotality:** Voters may value expressing membership in a political group even in an uncompetitive election. This can make [[concepts/partisan-alignment|Partisan Alignment]] relevant to turnout independently of electoral closeness.
- **Group context:** The Schuessler framework discussed in the ingested paper allows expressive utility to increase with one's group size until the opposing group becomes too small for the contrast to remain meaningful. A winner preference is one expressive account, not a necessary implication of every identity-based voting theory.
- **Awareness:** An explanation based on the expressive value of supporting a likely winner requires voters to perceive their party's electoral prospects. Correct perceptions of district composition support that prerequisite but do not establish the motivational mechanism.
- **Alternative explanations:** Elite mobilization can also produce higher aligned turnout and greater candidate awareness. Contact measures help assess this alternative, but absence of a statistically detectable contact increase does not demonstrate absence of campaign influence.
- **Evidence boundary:** Turnout changes, candidate awareness, and reported contact jointly inform the theory. None directly measures the utility of identity expression, and their association is not itself causal mediation evidence.

## Important Papers

- Schuessler (2000), "Expressive voting," as discussed in the ingested article: Develops an identity-expression account in which group context affects the payoff from participation.
- [[papers/partisan-alignment-increases-voter-turnout-evidence-from-redistricting|Partisan Alignment Increases Voter Turnout: Evidence from Redistricting]]: Finds higher turnout under alignment and survey patterns more consistent with expressive voting than with broad increases in campaign contact, while leaving the mechanism incompletely identified.
- Ashworth, Geys, and Heyndels (2006), "Everyone likes a winner: An empirical test of the effect of electoral closeness on turnout in a context of expressive voting," as cited and discussed in the ingested article.
