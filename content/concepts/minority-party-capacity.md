## Overview

Minority party capacity is the ability of a legislative minority to influence legislative outcomes. In Ballard and Curry's framework, capacity is greatest when constraints on the majority create opportunities, the minority can hold its members together, and it is motivated to legislate. Formal control of the chamber does not alone determine substantive bargaining power.

## Key Ideas

- **Opportunity depends on majority constraints.** Internal divisions, compulsory legislation, and pressure to act can make the majority seek minority votes despite procedural advantages.
- **Cohesion makes votes bargaining resources.** A coordinated minority can seek concessions; a divided minority may let the majority recruit enough individual defectors at lower policy cost.
- **Motivation is distinct from leverage.** A minority may prefer opposition and electoral positioning even when it could negotiate. Presidential leadership, policy commitments, emergencies, and public pressure can encourage legislative engagement.
- **Conditions vary by issue.** Aggregate party polarization can conceal areas where a majority is divided and a minority unified. Issue-specific voting distributions offer one operationalization, although votes need not reveal sincere preferences.
- **Separate capacity from observed influence.** A bill reaching the floor or becoming law does not by itself show how much the minority shaped its content. Policy histories complement statistical outcome models.
- **Distinguish legislative stages.** The originating study finds clearer support for the combined conditions in floor access than for enactment conditional on House passage. Minority cohesion has little apparent additional role in the latter analysis.
