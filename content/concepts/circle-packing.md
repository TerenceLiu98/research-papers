## Overview

Circle packing represents the vertices of a planar map by disks with disjoint interiors, with tangency encoding adjacency. Connecting tangent disk centers gives a geometric embedding of the map. For infinite triangulations, the packing is canonical up to Mobius transformations and is classified as parabolic or hyperbolic according to whether its carrier is the plane or the disk.

## Key Ideas

- The Koebe-Andreev-Thurston theorem gives circle packings for finite simple planar maps, while infinite triangulations require existence and uniqueness results adapted to the infinite setting.
- The Ring Lemma controls the ratio between the radius of a disk and the radii of neighboring disks in terms of vertex degree.
- Recurrence of simple random walk implies circle-packing parabolicity for simple infinite triangulations; this links probabilistic behavior to the geometry of the embedding.
- Dubejko weights turn a circle packing into a conductance network whose random walk can be compared with Brownian motion.
- Vertex extremal length can prevent macroscopic disks and is useful for proving that rescaled circle-packed walks have a Brownian limit.
- In the setting of the paper [[Circle Packing and Riemann Uniformization of Random Planar Maps in an Ergodic Scale-Free Environment]], cell centers and circle centers agree up to sublinear error under suitable assumptions.
