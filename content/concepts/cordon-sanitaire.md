## Overview

A principled refusal by established political actors to collaborate with a party, commonly used to exclude far-right parties from governing power.

## Key Ideas

Its scope varies: coalition exclusion, legislative non-cooperation, electoral coordination, and media exclusion impose different boundaries. France’s Republican Front and Germany’s Brandmauer exemplify context-specific forms. Formal exclusion alone does not establish opposition to the excluded party’s discourse or policies.

## Important Papers

[[papers/the-pretence-of-the-cordon-sanitaire-non-collaboration-as-a-distraction-from-discursive-congruence|Shuttleworth, Brown, and Mondon (2025)]] compares France, Germany, and the UK to show how formal non-collaboration can coexist with discursive congruence. The paper cautions against assuming an inherently oppositional relationship between mainstream and far-right politics.
