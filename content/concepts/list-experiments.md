## Overview

Respondents report how many listed statements apply, masking which individual items they affirm. Under random assignment, no design effects on control responses, and truthful counting, the treatment–control mean difference estimates sensitive-item prevalence.

## Key Ideas

- Masking can reduce sensitivity concerns, but counting multiple items can introduce mistakes and satisficing.
- Floor and ceiling responses can compromise privacy. Control-item selection matters.
- Higher estimated sensitive behavior does not establish greater accuracy: false positives can mimic reduced concealment.
- Nonstrategic errors can bias prevalence upward or downward; classification accuracy and prevalence RMSE answer different questions.
