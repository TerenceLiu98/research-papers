## Overview

Legislative protest voting separates expressive opposition from sincere preference between a proposal and the status quo. It can make ideological extremists look moderate when conventional scaling interprets every defection as spatial disagreement. The concept concerns legislative behavior, distinct from citizen protest voting in elections.

## Key Ideas

- A party defection alone does not establish protest: legislators can sincerely prefer the status quo.
- A behavioral mixture can estimate separate ideal points and protest propensities, allowing equally extreme members to protest at different rates.
- Fowler and Lewis operationalize eligibility as majority-party membership on votes with at least 90% caucus yea support. This restriction defines their estimator, not every possible form of legislative protest.
- Rare protests can distort individual ideological rankings even when they almost never change legislative outcomes.
- Estimated protest components do not independently establish motives, and nonmonotonic response patterns can also arise from sincere preferences.
