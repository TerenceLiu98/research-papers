## Overview

Populism and ultranationalism can overlap when appeals to the sovereignty of the people combine with exclusionary national boundaries and criticism of elites accused of protecting outsiders. They remain analytically distinct: populism contrasts the people with corrupt elites, while ultranationalism excludes national others and need not include anti-elitism. Populism can also target economic elites without hostility toward foreigners.

## Key Ideas

- **Two boundaries:** Analyze who belongs to the people and which elites are accused of betraying them. National exclusion and anti-elitism should each be established rather than inferred from generic patriotic language.
- **Internal and external enemies:** Outsiders can include foreigners and domestic actors accused of serving foreign interests. In Zhang and Schroeder's Chinese online study, pro-Western intellectuals are prominent internal targets.
- **Accountability without regime rejection:** Appeals to the people can demand punishment of government officials while affirming party authority. Anti-elite criticism does not necessarily imply opposition to the political system as a whole.
- **Constructive and antagonistic claims:** Demands for representation or accountability can coexist with exclusion and hostility. Zhang and Schroeder interpret these emphases through exploratory semantic axes, not a universally validated typology.
- **Bottom-up expression:** Ordinary users can originate and circulate populist rhetoric without electoral competition or a singular populist leader. Posting and reposting document expression and circulation, not necessarily persuasion or offline mobilisation.
- **Contextual measurement:** Keyword selection, annotation rules, censorship, and platform audiences shape observed discourse. A classifier trained on a particular national and temporal setting does not automatically provide a general measure of populism.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/its-all-about-us-vs-them-comparing-chinese-populist-discourses-on-weibo-and-twitter|It's All About US vs THEM!: Comparing Chinese Populist Discourses on Weibo and Twitter]]: Compares the overlap of populism and ultranationalism in Chinese-language posts from May 2022, distinguishing accountability-oriented and representation-oriented rhetoric.
- Mudde (2004), "The Populist Zeitgeist": Conceptual source cited by Zhang and Schroeder for the people-versus-elite distinction.
- Zhang (2020), "Right-Wing Populism with Chinese Characteristics? Identity, Otherness and Global Imaginaries in Debating World Politics Online": Cited context for national identity and anti-Western othering online.
