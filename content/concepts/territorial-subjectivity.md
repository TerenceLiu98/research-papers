## Overview

Territorial subjectivity describes the reciprocal formation of subjects and territories. Territories stabilize and destabilize identities through territorialization and deterritorialization, while people and collectives reproduce, negotiate, and transform territories through everyday practice and representation. Subjectivity is therefore an ongoing, relational process rather than a fixed identity attached to a bounded place.

## Key Ideas

- Territory is a contested spatial arrangement produced through political negotiation and unequal power, not merely a fixed geographic container.
- Subject formation and territorial formation are mutually constitutive: actors are shaped by places and spatial orders while also reshaping them.
- Territorial subjectivities can be layered across real, digital, and representational territories.
- They can also operate at multiple scales, including global, national, regional, local, and bodily experience.
- Differences among territorial subjectivities help explain why the same representation can evoke belonging for one audience and conflict for another.
- [[concepts/geographical-imagination|Geographical Imagination]] is one representational expression of territorial subjectivity.

## Important Papers

- Schwarz and Streule (2024), "Territorial Subjectivities: The Missing Link between Political Subjectivity and Territorialization": develops the reciprocal account of territorialization and subject formation.
- [[papers/transnational-bloggers-in-china-geographical-imagination-territorial-subjectivity-and-digital-geopolitics|Transnational Bloggers in China: Geographical Imagination, Territorial Subjectivity and Digital Geopolitics]]: extends the framework across real, digital, and representational layers and global, regional, and bodily scales.
- Halvorsen (2019), "Decolonising Territory": connects territorial analysis to Latin American knowledge and grassroots strategies.
