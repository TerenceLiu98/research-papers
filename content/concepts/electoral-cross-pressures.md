## Overview

Electoral cross-pressures arise when politically relevant considerations favor different candidates or parties. They can produce ambivalence, indecision, or changing support, including among voters with partisan attachments. A voter may agree with one party on economic issues and another on a personally salient social issue.

## Key Ideas

- Cross-pressures concern conflict among considerations; they are not equivalent to ideological moderation or lack of political knowledge.
- Issue salience affects which conflicts matter for a choice. Partisan attachment and political awareness can condition their consequences.
- [[concepts/ideological-dimensionality|Ideological Dimensionality]] describes the structure of political positions; cross-pressures describe competing pulls experienced by voters within that structure.
- Flexible predictive models can capture combinations that additive terms overlook. Feature importance establishes predictive contribution, while causal mechanisms require further evidence.
