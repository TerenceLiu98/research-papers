## Overview

The affect misattribution procedure presents a prime followed by an ambiguous target, such as an unfamiliar character. Participants judge the target while being instructed to disregard the prime. Differences in target evaluations across prime categories provide an indirect measure of the associations activated by those categories.

## Key Ideas

- Misattribution is the proposed spillover of prime-induced affect into judgment of the target. It does not require participants to explicitly endorse attitudes toward the prime.
- Relative pleasant-response frequencies define the measured contrast. They should not be treated as a universal score of all forms of prejudice.
- Difficulty controlling the prime’s influence motivates the method, but does not guarantee complete immunity to intentional responding.
- Cue comprehension, literacy, timing, and practice affect applicability. Positive-versus-negative word primes can check whether the task functions locally without validating every substantive interpretation.

## Important Papers

- Payne et al. (2005), “An Inkblot for Attitudes: Affect Misattribution as Implicit Measurement”: original procedure, cited by the application below.
- [[papers/measuring-ethnic-bias|Measuring Ethnic Bias]]: adapts geographic primes to ethnic comparisons in Nairobi and finds 7.8–13 percentage-point differences in pleasant judgments despite null average DG/PGG effects.
