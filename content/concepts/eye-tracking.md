## Overview

Eye tracking records where and when people look, allowing researchers to study information acquisition during reading, evaluation, and choice. Fixations, transitions, and pupil measurements provide distinct signals whose psychological interpretation depends on task design.

## Key Ideas

- **Define areas of interest.** AOIs connect gaze coordinates to meaningful stimulus elements. Their size, spacing, and margins must accommodate measurement error without overlap.
- **Separate measurement from inference.** Fixation proportions, dwell time, returns, and first-fixated options describe attention allocation. Longer viewing can reflect importance, difficulty, or other processes; it is not a unique measure of preference.
- **Pupil signals require controls.** Dilation can reflect arousal or cognitive load and is sensitive to illumination. It cannot by itself identify emotional valence.
- **Match equipment to the task.** Spatial accuracy constrains stimulus density, while sampling rate constrains temporal measures. Webcam estimates can support simple, widely spaced displays but require quality checks under variable home conditions.
- **Report processing choices.** Calibration, drift correction, fixation classification, exclusions, and aggregation affect interpretation and reproducibility.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/using-eye-tracking-to-understand-decision-making-in-conjoint-experiments|Using Eye-Tracking to Understand Decision-Making in Conjoint Experiments]]: validates choice-based attribute rankings against fixations and shows that larger tables induce selective coverage and changes in search strategy despite fairly stable choice effects.

- [[papers/odd-profiles-in-conjoint-experimental-designs|Odd Profiles in Conjoint Experimental Designs]]: combines fixation counts and search transitions with choices, finding small and inconsistent responses to unusual attribute combinations in two conjoint scenarios.

- [[papers/attention-and-political-choice-a-foundation-for-eye-tracking-in-political-science|Attention and Political Choice: A Foundation for Eye Tracking in Political Science]]: connects gaze measures to political research and explains design, analysis, and inference constraints.
