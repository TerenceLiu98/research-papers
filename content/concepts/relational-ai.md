## Overview

Relational AI refers to algorithmic systems with which users form socioemotional bonds and enact social roles such as friend, confidant, or romantic partner. The category includes purpose-built companion systems and general-purpose conversational agents when interaction centers on companionship, validation, intimacy, or relationship maintenance.

## Key Ideas

- Users can orient to the same system as both a social partner and a programmed artifact. Smooth interaction lets these orientations coexist; communication breakdowns make their tension difficult to ignore.
- Social cues, persona continuity, and responsive language invite interpersonal expectations even though the system lacks consciousness, intent, and independent moral agency.
- Relational failures threaten attachment and partner identity, not only task completion. Repair is therefore asymmetric: the user interprets the failure and performs the adjustment while the system cannot intentionally reciprocate.
- [[Algorithmic Relational Fracture]] describes the acute sensemaking that follows a breakdown, while [[Pseudo-Relational Accommodation]] describes the user's one-sided response.
