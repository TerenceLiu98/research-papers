## Overview

Connected social media consists of separately operated outlets linked to a focal firm through investment, personnel, or shared ownership relationships. The analytical distinction is between formal corporate disclosure and reporting by an outlet that appears to be a third-party intermediary while having ties to the covered firm. A connection is an observable relationship, not by itself proof of editorial control or false reporting.

## Key Ideas

- Connections can arise through direct equity stakes, shared controllers or managers, or investments in a common third-party entity. Their timing and persistence are measurement choices that affect which articles count as connected.
- An outlet's relationship to a particular firm matters: a connected outlet can also cover firms to which it has no identified tie.
- Favorable reporting can involve a change in sentiment, a change in topic selection, or both. [[concepts/optimism-shifting|Optimism Shifting]] describes the redirection toward favorable future prospects following adverse news.
- Comparing connected and unconnected outlets around the same event helps hold the focal news environment constant. It does not automatically remove selection into connections or selective publication.
- Content production, audience exposure, investor beliefs, and prices are separate outcomes. Their causal links require evidence beyond a favorable article or a correlated return.
