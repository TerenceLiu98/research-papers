## Overview

Protest event analysis systematically codes observable collective political actions from documentary sources to study their timing, location, participants, and claims. Its central measurement problem is that a source report and an underlying event are different units: several articles may describe one protest, while other protests receive no coverage.

## Key Ideas

- **Define the event and sampling frame.** Rules must specify which actions, places, and periods qualify. A dataset restricted to geographically located street activities cannot measure online mobilisation, and a major-city sample cannot describe rural protest comprehensively.
- **Separate reports from events.** Removing duplicate articles is different from merging distinct reports of the same event. Retaining reports before aggregation preserves disagreements about attendance or other attributes.
- **Distinguish mobilisation measures.** Event counts measure protest frequency; cumulative attendance measures participation across events. Neither identifies the number of unique participants without additional information.
- **Treat coverage as a measurement issue.** Local and regional outlets can improve detection of local events, but the recorded distribution still depends on reporting. Agreement with independent sources supports selected aspects of validity without proving completeness.
- **Preserve the level of inference.** Aggregating events to city-years enables contextual count models. Associations with city characteristics do not establish the characteristics or motivations of individual protesters.
