## Overview

Perpetuated sensegiving is an ongoing process of shaping others' interpretations by introducing new cues and reopening questions so that they continue trying to make sense of an event without settling on a final account. Chase, Maitlis, and Shepherd develop the concept as a central mechanism of [[Conspiracy Entrepreneurship]]. It combines interpretive guidance with recurrent obstruction of closure.

## Key Ideas

- **Reopening interpretation:** Purported evidence and new inconsistencies redirect attention, while provisional explanations or expressions of fatigue can be reframed as reasons for further investigation.
- **Connecting to wider narratives:** References to preexisting accounts of hidden actors or institutional deception give emerging claims a familiar frame without resolving the specific event.
- **Cautious questioning:** Repeated questions can preserve suspicion while allowing a speaker to appear responsible or avoid making a definite assertion. In the originating case, this is a recurrent pattern of communication, not a claim that questioning itself is manipulative.
- **Direction without convergence:** Interpretations are steered away from the mainstream account while remaining unsettled among alternative explanations. The audience can converge on an investigative identity without agreeing on a final counternarrative.
- **Participation and agency:** Invitations to contribute observations can sustain perceived agency even when the platform's host selectively shapes the course of inquiry.

The originating paper distinguishes the mechanism from strategic ambiguity as follows. These are theoretical contrasts drawn by the authors, rather than an exhaustive classification of all ambiguous communication.

| Dimension | Strategic ambiguity in the paper's comparison | Perpetuated sensegiving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time horizon | Often bounded by an organizational purpose | Repeatedly renews inquiry without an intended endpoint of closure |
| Interpretation | Accommodates several meanings simultaneously | Directs interpretation toward counternarratives while reopening particular explanations |
| Commercial role | Can protect exploitation of an opportunity | Helps sustain the engagement through which an opportunity is commercialized |

The evidence comes from one qualitative case. Although the model describes a process that functions to prevent closure, the authors explicitly do not establish whether the actor's underlying intentions were strategic or emergently adaptive. They also leave the proposed feedback from monetization to further sensegiving for future research.

## Important Papers

- [[Ambiguity as Opportunity: A Theoretical Model of Conspiracy Entrepreneurship]]: Introduces perpetuated sensegiving and identifies its practices through temporally ordered broadcasts and archival records.
- Gioia and Chittipeddi (1991), "Sensemaking and sensegiving in strategic change initiation": Foundational sensegiving research discussed in the originating manuscript.
- Eisenberg (1984), "Ambiguity as strategy in organizational communication": A source for the strategic-ambiguity comparison.
