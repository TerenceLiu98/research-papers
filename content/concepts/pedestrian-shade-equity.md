## Overview

Pedestrian shade equity evaluates access to shade where people can walk, rather than treating total vegetation or citywide shade coverage as sufficient measures of benefit. Buildings and trees both cast shadows, but their location, geometry, and timing determine which public surfaces benefit.

## Key Ideas

- Mask simulated shade to public pedestrian space before aggregating. Private vegetation coverage alone does not establish accessible shade.
- Separate intensity from resource quantity: average shaded fraction describes local coverage, while multiplying by sidewalk area measures available shaded area.
- Assess absolute adequacy and relative inequality together. Abundant shade can coexist with unequal distribution, while uniformly low shade can remain inadequate.
- Distinguish building shade from the additional contribution of trees to avoid counting overlapping shadows twice.
- Interpret neighbourhood comparisons as area-level evidence. Generated sidewalks, varying census units, and unobserved pedestrian movement limit conclusions about personal exposure.
