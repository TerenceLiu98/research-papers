## Overview

Involution games model costly effort escalation in competition for resources that effort does not expand. An individual can gain a larger resource share by working harder than competitors, yet widespread escalation raises costs without creating additional collective value. The dilemma concerns incentives to spend effort on allocation, rather than depletion of the underlying resource stock.

## Key Ideas

- **Private share versus collective surplus.** A proportional-allocation contest can assign agent $i$ net payoff $M e_i/\sum_j e_j-e_i$. The resource pool $M$ is fixed within the contest, while total effort costs increase with escalation. Whether high effort is individually favored depends on competitors and parameters.
- **Operationalizing involution.** Two-strategy models often measure the fraction choosing high effort. This is a model-specific indicator, not a direct measurement of social welfare or real-world excessive competition.
- **Resource abundance.** A larger prize can increase the payoff advantage of extra effort. More resources therefore need not reduce costly competition in a fixed-prize allocation model.
- **Spatial context.** Local resource disparities, interaction neighborhoods, and the arrangement of rich and poor areas affect incentives even at a fixed global resource total. Greater regional inequality and finer spatial mixing are different changes and need not have the same effect.
- **Endogenous location.** Migration toward successful competitors changes local competition as well as individual opportunities. Its effect on high-effort adoption must be assessed separately from its effect on spatial concentration.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/involution-game-with-migration-and-spatial-heterogeneity-of-social-resources|Involution Game with Migration and Spatial Heterogeneity of Social Resources]]: couples local contests with migration and regional inequality. The source's contradictory migration claims and payoff specifications limit stronger conclusions.

- Wang and Szolnoki (2022), "Involution game with spatio-temporal heterogeneity of social resources": resource-heterogeneity extension described in that review.
