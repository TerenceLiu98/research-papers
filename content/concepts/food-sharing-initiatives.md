## Overview

Food Sharing Initiatives (FSIs) are community-based activities that share food, food-related resources, or opportunities to grow and prepare food. Examples include surplus-food redistribution, community gardens, seed swaps, shared meals, and other digitally mediated practices. The concept covers both the activity itself and the local organizational presence that can be mapped, categorized, and studied.

## Key Ideas

- FSIs are diverse in both activity and sharing method. A database can distinguish activities such as growing, cooking and eating, and distribution from methods such as gifting, collecting, bartering, and selling.
- Their information is often local, multilingual, and distributed across changing websites and social platforms, making systematic discovery difficult.
- Comparable FSI data supports analysis of where initiatives operate, how food is shared, and how urban food-sharing landscapes change over time.
- Digital visibility is not equivalent to real-world presence. Automated discovery can miss initiatives without a web presence or misclassify nearby services with similar terminology, so verification and recurrent checking matter.
- The SHARECITY and CULTIVATE work treats FSI mapping as both an information-retrieval problem and a way to make grassroots sustainability activity visible to researchers and stakeholders.
