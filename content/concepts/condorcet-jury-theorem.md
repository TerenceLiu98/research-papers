## Overview

[[Condorcet's Jury Theorem]] states that, when voters independently choose between two alternatives and each voter identifies the correct alternative with probability greater than .5, majority rule is more accurate than an individual vote. As the group grows, the probability that the majority chooses correctly approaches one.

## Key Ideas

- The theorem requires a defined correct or superior alternative, a common decision objective in its simplest form, independent votes, and individual accuracy above .5.
- Majority voting aggregates decentralized information: each voter can be imperfect while the group becomes highly accurate under the theorem's assumptions.
- The meaning of the correct alternative can be objective, such as the true state of the world, or tied to a common-goal benchmark such as a Pareto-superior option.
- Heterogeneous individual accuracies can be accommodated in extensions, but the average accuracy alone does not rescue majority rule when dependence is uncontrolled.
- Correlation is a central failure mode. [[The Condorcet Jury Theorem, Free Speech, and Correlated Votes]] shows that pairwise uncorrelated votes can still be jointly dependent and can make the majority less accurate than an individual.
