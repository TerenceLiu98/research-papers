## Overview

Photographic cultural marking connects technical image production to shared conventions of interpretation. Light, color, framing, and material presentation acquire significance through learned practices rather than carrying a single intrinsic meaning.

## Key Ideas

- Capture preserves possibilities for later expression; post-production and display select and emphasize particular meanings.
- Expertise includes anticipating the finished image and understanding when automatic settings conflict with the intended expression.
- Temporal and spatial contrasts matter: similar twilight colors can support different associations when interpreted as morning or evening.
- Markedness is relational and historically variable. A production medium can become conspicuous when another becomes the assumed default.
- Interpretation requires contextual support. A plausible symbolic reading is not evidence that every audience shares it.
