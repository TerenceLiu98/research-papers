## Overview

Vagueness concerns whether a political statement commits its author to a policy action or outcome that can be evaluated without value judgments. It can occur within an individual statement, even when its ideological direction is clear. Describing vagueness as strategic posits a motive; observing noncommittal language alone does not demonstrate deliberate intent.

## Key Ideas

- Specificity and ideological consistency are separate dimensions. A collection of concrete promises can mix left- and right-wing signals; an ideologically consistent program can avoid testable promises.
- Praprotnik and Ennser-Jedenastik measure aggregate vagueness as one minus the share of objectively testable pledges. Subjective promises and statements without pledge character both enter the numerator of the resulting noncommitment share.
- Testability must be available in advance. A general aspiration to improve educational conditions leaves the criterion open, whereas abolishing tuition fees specifies a verifiable action. Maintaining the status quo can also be testable.
- Pledge specificity differs from pledge fulfillment: making a measurable promise does not imply delivering it.
- In Austrian manifesto data, vagueness rises with perceived issue competence while positional ambiguity falls. This is a contextual association rather than a general causal law.
