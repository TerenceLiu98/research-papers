## Overview

Polycentric governance distributes decision-making authority across multiple semi-autonomous units whose choices can affect one another. A village, municipality, organization, or jurisdiction may set its own rules, but movement, trade, shared resources, or other cross-boundary relationships make those rules strategically interdependent. System-level outcomes therefore need not match what any one unit intended.

## Key Ideas

- **Multiple centers of authority.** Local units retain meaningful discretion rather than merely implementing a uniform decision from a single center.
- **Interdependence.** Mobility and shared environments transmit one unit's policy effects to others. The relevant unit of analysis is therefore both the local decision maker and the network in which it operates.
- **Endogenous external shocks.** A change can be endogenous to the overall system yet confront a neighboring unit as an external pressure.
- **Domino effects.** One unit's restrictive policy can redirect people or costs toward other units and change their incentives to adopt similar policies.
- **Lock-in.** Once many units adopt a rule, unilateral reversal can become costly even when coordinated reversal would be preferable.
- **Centralization trade-off.** A monocentric intervention can coordinate a system-wide transition that decentralized units cannot sustain individually, but it also removes local institutional autonomy.

## Important Papers

- Ostrom (2010), "Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems": develops the broader framework for systems with multiple governing authorities.
- [[papers/from-open-to-closed-societies-inequality-migration-and-womens-rights|From Open to Closed Societies: Inequality, Migration, and Women's Rights]]: studies how autonomous village membership rules generated spatial diffusion and lock-in, followed by centralized egalitarian reform.
