## Overview

Visual conjoint experiments retain randomized multidimensional profiles while changing how respondents encounter their attributes. Examples include simulated social-media profiles in which photographs, biographies, and engagement counts occupy different visual positions and receive unequal prominence.

## Key Ideas

- Specify the target encounter before choosing a stimulus format. Realism is relative to that setting.
- Holding explicit attribute information constant does not hold psychological treatment constant: images can change salience and bundle additional cues.
- Randomizing presentation modality permits direct tests of whether attribute effects differ across formats.
- Interface familiarity may moderate responses, but any particular moderator requires evidence. Age heterogeneity does not establish digital-literacy heterogeneity.
- Visual realism does not by itself establish realistic profile frequencies, representative respondents, or predictive validity for consequential choices.

## Important Papers
