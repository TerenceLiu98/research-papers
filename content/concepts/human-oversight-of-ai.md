## Overview

Human oversight of AI comprises the responsibilities and procedures through which people evaluate, challenge, override, and monitor AI-supported decisions. Meaningful oversight requires understandable evidence and authority to act on it; the mere presence of a human reviewer does not establish that outputs receive independent scrutiny.

## Key Ideas

- Strategic oversight defines the system's objectives, permitted uses, acceptable risks, and accountability arrangements. Operational oversight reviews outputs and monitors performance during use.
- Explainability and oversight are complementary. Reviewers need accessible justifications, but an explanation alone does not provide a review procedure, authority to override an output, or a route for affected parties to contest a decision.
- In [[concepts/ai-assisted-public-procurement-auditing|AI-Assisted Public Procurement Auditing]], PRO-Trust proposes mandatory human review of high-risk alerts, audit committees, feedback on misleading alerts, monitoring dashboards, recalibration criteria, and supplier dispute mechanisms.
- Feedback can support model revision, while domain experts assess legal and procedural context that may be absent from the training data. Responsibility must be explicit across technical teams and institutional decision-makers.
- Evidence that experts consider oversight procedures relevant differs from evidence that those procedures improve decisions in practice. The PRO-Trust study provides the former through limited expert feedback and leaves deployment evaluation for future work.
