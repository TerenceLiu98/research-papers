## Overview

External validation tests whether event records recover independently documented political occurrences. Internal validation asks whether coding systems extract the same information from shared texts. Both are useful, but agreement on an incomplete news corpus cannot establish coverage of underlying events.

## Key Ideas

- Align event definitions, actors, locations, and time windows before comparing sources. Binary presence and event counts measure different targets.
- Report positive-event recovery alongside overall agreement: numerous correctly recorded absences can conceal substantial underreporting.
- Independent references require a substantive justification of coverage and reliability; independence alone does not guarantee completeness.
- Spatial models complement confusion matrices by estimating geographically correlated errors and covariate-associated underreporting. Similar predictive discrimination can coexist with different spatial error structures.
- Source coverage, event extraction, and geolocation jointly affect validity. A comparison of complete datasets does not isolate the causal effect of coding automation.
