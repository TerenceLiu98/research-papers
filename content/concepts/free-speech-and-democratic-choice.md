## Overview

Free speech and democratic choice are linked in this paper through the information structure of collective decisions. Open debate can expose citizens to multiple and opposing schools of thought, creating the possibility of lower or negative correlations among votes. When voters are correct on average more than half the time, that lower correlation can improve majority-rule accuracy.

## Key Ideas

- Free speech is treated as an institutional condition that permits competing interpretations and access to information beyond an individual's immediate experience.
- Autocratic control can force voters toward a common position, producing highly correlated votes that add little through aggregation.
- Opposing schools of thought may lower average correlation, but diversity does not by itself imply accuracy: a large school with poor ability to identify the superior alternative can reduce average voter accuracy below .5.
- The mechanism is probabilistic rather than deterministic. The paper argues that free speech creates the potential for more effective majority rule, not that it guarantees a correct majority.
- Democratic choice therefore depends on both plural information environments and citizens' ability to discriminate among credible and spurious claims.
