## Overview

Social-semantic coevolution is the reciprocal process through which relationships shape ideas, language, and tastes while semantic similarity shapes the formation of relationships. Contact can transmit and recombine cultural forms, but repeated interaction can also concentrate attention and reduce system-level diversity. Conversely, shared meanings lower communication costs and can make further social connection more likely.

## Key Ideas

- **Selection and socialization are coupled:** similar actors may choose one another, and connected actors may become more similar after interacting. Observational convergence usually cannot be assigned wholly to either mechanism.
- **Individual and system effects can differ:** a broker may gain diverse information by connecting groups even as widespread bridging exposes previously separate ideas to common competition and reduces diversity across the whole system.
- **Time scale matters:** connection can generate novel combinations immediately while weakening the distinct traditions needed for future recombination.
- **Common geometry enables comparison:** hyperbolic embeddings can represent hierarchy radially and similarity angularly in both social and semantic networks, making movement in the two domains directly comparable.
- **Diversity has multiple operationalizations:** angular spread can account for semantic relationships among items, while entropy over topics or words provides a simpler frequency-based check. Neither measure by itself establishes creativity, validity, or social value.
