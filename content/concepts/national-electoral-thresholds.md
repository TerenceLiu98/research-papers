## Overview

A national threshold of representation describes access to legislative seats. A legal cutoff may differ from the effective national barrier when district victories or upper-tier provisions offer alternative routes to seats. Geographic concentration can make small national parties viable.

## Key Ideas

- Eligibility and allocation are separate: a system can exclude small parties yet allocate seats proportionally among those eligible.
- A stochastic threshold represents variation in access at a given national vote share, including uncertainty in the geographic distribution of support.
- In the Kalandrakis–Rueda model, the smallest vote share among seat-winning parties bounds the realized threshold from above. Zero seats alone do not prove ineligibility because eligible parties may receive zero seats under stochastic allocation.
- Estimated thresholds summarize rules and competitive behavior jointly. They should not automatically be interpreted as statutory requirements or causal effects of electoral law.
