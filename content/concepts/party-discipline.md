## Overview

Party discipline is the influence party organizations exert to bring legislators' behavior into line with leadership preferences. Observed party unity combines this influence with members' underlying preference similarity; unity alone therefore does not identify discipline.

## Key Ideas

- **Distinguish preferences from inducements.** A roll-call ideal point can absorb organizational pressure when its behavioral model assumes sincere voting.
- **Separate counting from whipping.** A whip count gathers intended votes; whipping attempts to change votes. Prewhipping intentions can provide additional identifying information, subject to assumptions about truthful reporting and intervening shocks.
- **Influence can be targeted.** In Canen, Kendall, and Trebbi's model, whips persuade opposing members close enough to the voting threshold that the benefit exceeds the cost of influence.
- **The agenda responds.** Anticipated discipline changes passage probabilities and can change which proposals leadership finds worth pursuing. Holding the agenda fixed and allowing it to adjust answer different counterfactual questions.
- **Measurement requires restrictions.** Internal counts offer one identification strategy; assumptions that certain votes have no inducements offer another. Neither strategy makes party effects assumption-free.
