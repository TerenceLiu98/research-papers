## Overview

Interest-group scorecards evaluate legislators using selected roll-call votes and a group's preferred position on each vote. They combine policy advocacy with public judgments about individual lawmakers, potentially informing endorsements, campaign support, voter mobilization, and electoral challenges. A scoring decision is therefore both a measurement choice and a political action.

## Key Ideas

- **Selection and position are different decisions.** A group chooses whether to score a vote and which side to support. Analysis restricted to scored votes describes disagreement conditional on selection, not the full legislative agenda.
- **Party alignment does not imply agreement on every vote.** An aligned group can reject a compromise supported by party leaders, exposing legislators to competing incentives within their coalition.
- **Governing and position taking can diverge.** Leaders may value passing an imperfect bill while a group emphasizes its policy commitments. Both actors face multiple incentives; this is a potential source of disagreement, not a fixed distinction between their goals.
- **Scorecards are not neutral samples of ideology.** Selecting divisive or salient votes can change apparent ideological distances. Researchers should distinguish the information in a rating from the strategic process that produces it.
- **Observed conflict is not identified influence.** A negative association between group-leadership disagreement and party cohesion can reflect group pressure, shared preferences, bill content, or existing factions. Scoring announcements and prior voting behavior can inform research designs but do not automatically establish causality.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/interest-groups-and-intra-party-conflict-on-scored-votes|Interest Groups and Intra-Party Conflict on Scored Votes]]: compares fourteen aligned groups with congressional leaders during 2007-2018, finding lower cohesion on disagreement votes and a Republican-driven majority/minority asymmetry.
- Charnock (2018), "More Than a Score: Interest Group Ratings and Polarized Politics": historical background on ratings and ideological commitments, discussed in the linked paper.
- Fowler (1982), "How Interest Groups Select Issues for Rating Voting Records of Members of the US Congress": background on strategic issue selection, cited in the linked paper.
