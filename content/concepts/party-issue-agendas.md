## Overview

A party issue agenda is the distribution of attention that a political party gives to policy topics in its public communication. It captures which issues a party emphasizes and how that emphasis changes across parties, time, and political contexts; it is distinct from the positions a party takes within those issues.

## Key Ideas

- Issue attention can be operationalized as the share of a party's communications assigned to each topic during a defined period. The time unit and communication source therefore shape the measured agenda.
- Election manifestos provide standardized but infrequent snapshots. Press releases, speeches, parliamentary activity, news, and social media support more dynamic measurement but differ in who controls the agenda and which audiences they address.
- Text-as-data methods include hand coding, dictionaries, unsupervised topic models, and supervised classification. Comparative analysis requires categories that retain consistent meanings across countries, parties, languages, and time.
- Press releases reflect communication selected by party organizations and are not constrained by a parliamentary agenda, making them useful for studying short-term reactions. Incomplete archives and selective deletion can nevertheless bias the inferred distribution of attention.
- Dynamic agendas allow researchers to study whether parties lead, follow, or differentiate from competitors and whether attention changes with public priorities or external events. An observed temporal association alone does not establish why one party responded to another.
