## Overview

Hybrid human-AI societies are populations and institutions in which humans and artificial agents repeatedly interact, adapt to one another, and jointly shape collective outcomes. The concept emphasizes feedback rather than one-off tool use: AI systems learn from human-produced data and behavior, while their recommendations, decisions, communication, and delegated actions change later human behavior and the environments from which future systems learn.

## Key Ideas

- **Heterogeneous adaptation:** humans differ in norms, expectations, and responses to AI, while artificial agents differ in capabilities, interfaces, ownership, and update speed.
- **Population effects:** introducing even a small share of artificial agents can alter cooperation, coordination, fairness, trust, or risk sharing. [[concepts/replicator-dynamics|Replicator Dynamics]] and related stochastic models describe how strategies spread under these conditions.
- **Behavior-network feedback:** actions affect reputation and partner choice, so behavior and interaction structure may evolve together as a [[concepts/coevolutionary-network-games|Coevolutionary Network Game]].
- **Cultural mediation:** generators, recommenders, and assistants do more than transmit culture. They influence which artifacts are produced, which connections form, and which norms receive attention.
- **Language and delegation:** language models can frame strategic situations and act as delegates. This introduces feedback among communication, trust, commitment, exploitation, and responsibility.
- **Epistemic heterogeneity:** similar human and AI outputs may arise from different grounding, experience, causal reasoning, confidence, or verification processes. Long-run dynamics can therefore depend on internal decision pipelines as well as observed actions.
- **Institutional co-evolution:** laws, transparency rules, audits, market incentives, and ownership structures alter agent behavior while adoption and public response reshape regulation and development incentives.
- **Evidence requirements:** formal models and [[concepts/hybrid-llm-agent-based-simulation|Hybrid LLM Agent-Based Simulations]] need calibration and validation against human experiments or field data. See [[concepts/validity-boundaries-for-llm-social-simulations|Validity Boundaries for LLM Social Simulations]].
