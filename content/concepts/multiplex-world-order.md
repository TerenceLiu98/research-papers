## Overview

Multiplex world order is a framework associated with Amitav Acharya that emphasizes diverse actors, overlapping governance arrangements, and cooperation across multiple domains. As presented in Aryal's review, it extends analysis beyond counting major powers to ask how influence and coordination operate across global, regional, and subnational levels.

## Key Ideas

- The framework envisages an order without a single hegemon or dominant coalition controlling global governance.
- Interdependence includes security, environmental cooperation, development, and connectivity alongside economic exchange.
- Governance operates through overlapping formal institutions, informal networks, and hybrid arrangements at different scales.
- Political, cultural, and ideological diversity is treated as a potential resource for cooperation rather than a condition to eliminate.
- Interaction capacity, understood in the reviewed account as the relative ability to lead and foster collaboration, offers an alternative emphasis to military or economic capability alone.
- Multiplexity and multipolarity are distinct analytical lenses. Multiple power centers do not by themselves demonstrate layered governance or effective cooperation.

## Important Papers

- [[asian-multipolarity-and-the-future-of-the-liberal-international-order-lio|Asian Multipolarity and the Future of the Liberal International Order (LIO)]]: reviews the framework and relates it to regional agency in Asia; it does not independently measure interaction capacity.
- Acharya (2017), "After liberal hegemony: The advent of a multiplex world order." A foundational work cited in Aryal's discussion.
- Acharya, Estevadeordal, and Goodman (2023), "Multipolar or multiplex? Interaction capacity, global cooperation and world order." The source Aryal uses for the interaction-capacity perspective.
