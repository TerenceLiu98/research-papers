## Overview

Agreement is evidence about both the underlying question and the process that generated the group's choices. If unanimity is especially likely among highly conformist groups, modest disagreement can signal that private information has retained influence. The result is a nonmonotonic relationship between observed agreement and expected accuracy.

## Key Ideas

- **Selection across groups:** conditioning on agreement changes the inferred mixture of group types. An accuracy curve for one known conformity level need not describe a population with unknown, varying conformity.
- **Diagnostic versus causal value:** observing dissent can reveal a more reliable process; this does not establish that adding dissent mechanically improves accuracy.
- **Scope:** in the focal Bayesian count-only model, sufficient between-group heterogeneity supports the effect, particularly when it exceeds within-group variation. Unanimity remains informative in many other settings.
- **Sequence exception:** complete decision histories can produce highly accurate non-unanimous outcomes even at fixed conformity. Such outcomes need not predict a group's future reliability.
- **Practical relevance:** reversals at agreement levels that almost never occur should carry little weight when measuring the phenomenon.
