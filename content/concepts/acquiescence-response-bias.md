## Overview

An agreeable response can reflect a response tendency as well as a belief. When agreement always signals endorsement of the target proposition, acquiescence can inflate apparent prevalence. Variation in this tendency across respondents can also distort associations with ideology, education, or other characteristics.

## Key Ideas

- **Randomize the key within items.** Assign respondents either an assertion or its logical negation, then recode both toward the same substantive belief. Mixing unrelated true and false statements does not supply the same comparison.
- **Separate wording gaps from corrected beliefs.** Under symmetric additive bias, the midpoint of key-specific means estimates the underlying mean, and half their difference estimates average bias. Neither key alone is a gold standard.
- **Correct associations as well as means.** Key–covariate interactions model heterogeneous bias; balanced weighting across randomized keys offers an alternative for estimating associations.
- **Make identification assumptions visible.** Logical equivalence, random assignment, and symmetry matter. Report results by key alongside corrected estimates because symmetry is not directly established by the wording experiment.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/acquiescence-bias-inflates-estimates-of-conspiratorial-beliefs-and-political-misperceptions|Hill and Roberts (2023)]] — demonstrates key-sensitive political-belief estimates across six surveys and develops mean and regression corrections.

## Related Concepts

Survey measurement error is the broader problem of divergence between reported responses and the target construct. Acquiescence is a specific directional response tendency; it need not imply deliberate partisan expression or conscious misreporting.
