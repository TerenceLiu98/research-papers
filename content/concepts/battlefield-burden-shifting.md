## Overview

When military damage concentrates near the boundary between opposing blocs, alliance expansion can move exposure away from existing members and toward remaining outsiders. A country may therefore value accession because subsequent enlargement will move the prospective battlefield farther away.

## Key Ideas

- **Accession creates an externality.** A smaller remaining buffer zone concentrates expected losses among fewer countries, changing their incentives to join.
- **Future expansion shapes current concessions.** Candidates can pay for admission in anticipation of eventually becoming hinterland; a hegemon can extract value at successive steps.
- **Private incentives can conflict with collective security.** Expansion can continue even if the loss of neutral territory raises total expected war damage.
- **Exposure thresholds matter.** Full absorption requires the last buffer country to prefer frontline membership. In Konrad and Morath's baseline, this requires the buffer-zone war probability to exceed half the no-buffer probability.
- **The mechanism is conditional.** The formal result assumes a particular geography, bargaining protocol, and distribution of damage. It does not by itself identify the causes of observed alliance enlargement.
