## Overview

Data quality assessment asks whether an existing measure supports a particular theoretical question and set of cases. It combines conceptual scrutiny with evidence about systematic and random measurement error. Quality is conditional on intended use; an overall correlation or a reproducible coding procedure cannot settle every validity question.

## Key Ideas

- **Content validity:** map operational indicators to the theoretical construct, identifying omitted meanings and irrelevant inclusions. Factor analysis can examine coherence but cannot replace this conceptual judgment.
- **Production process:** audit institutional incentives, sources, respondent recruitment, coding, aggregation rules, and coverage. With multiple raters, examine both disagreement and systematic response patterns.
- **Convergent validity:** compare alternative measures and diagnose where they diverge. Independently written, blind case studies can supply evidence when comparable quantitative series do not exist.
- **Shared errors:** high agreement can coexist with systematic bias. Raters shared across indicators can induce correlated errors, including in apparent factor structure.
- **Consequences for research:** use the assessment to select cases and measures, propagate uncertainty, and qualify substantive conclusions rather than treating validation as a one-time approval.
