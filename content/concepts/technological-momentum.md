## Overview

Technological momentum describes how technological systems become increasingly embedded in institutions, practices, and expectations as they develop. In the Hughes (1987) framework summarized by Khurana, early design and adoption remain comparatively open to deliberate choices; accumulated organizational adaptation later makes reversal more difficult.

## Key Ideas

- Institutional rules, investments, incentives, and everyday practices can reinforce adoption.
- Influence is reciprocal: people shape technologies, while established technological systems reshape institutional choices.
- Momentum does not eliminate agency. Governance can still shape how a technology is used and whose objectives it serves.
- In education, changing assessments, usage policies, and platform deployment can be interpreted as institutional embedding. Adoption alone does not prove irreversible change or improved learning.
