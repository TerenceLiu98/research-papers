## Overview

Stability and constraint describe complementary properties of attitude systems. An attitude may persist because of durable individual characteristics, because shocks decay quickly, or because pressures to change are limited. Constraint concerns functional interdependence: changes in one attitude lead to adjustments in others.

## Key Ideas

- Rank stability can coexist with population-wide change. Stable ordering and stable absolute levels answer different questions.
- Permanent shared traits can associate attitudes without establishing dynamic spillovers between them.
- Constraint can be asymmetric. An attitude may influence others strongly while receiving little influence itself.
- A large share of spillover variance need not imply a large share of total variance. Both the denominator and the time horizon matter.
- Transient responses can reflect measurement error or changing considerations; rapid decay alone does not identify their source.
