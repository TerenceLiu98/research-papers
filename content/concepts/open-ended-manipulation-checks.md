## Overview

Open-ended manipulation checks elicit what respondents understood or remembered about experimental material. Comparing a recalled response with its prompt can yield a continuous measure of attention rather than a binary correct/incorrect judgment.

## Key Ideas

- Character n-gram overlap provides reproducible lexical scores and some tolerance for spelling variation; semantic similarity can better accommodate paraphrases.
- Similarity is a proxy for reception, requiring validation against meaningful comprehension or correctness criteria.
- Text-based scoring requires comparable prompts across experimental conditions, including the control condition.
- Attention weighting and threshold sensitivity analyses reveal which respondents drive results, but post-treatment attention and demographic selection complicate causal and population interpretations.
- A check placed after the outcome additionally requires recall at checking time to reflect reception when the outcome was recorded.
