## Overview

Marine protected areas (MPAs) are spatially delimited ocean areas established for the long-term conservation of marine biodiversity and resources. Their effectiveness depends on policy design, enforcement, ecological conditions, and local social and economic context. Because MPAs are not generally assigned randomly, estimating their effects requires attention to placement and sampling bias.

## Key Ideas

- MPA evaluation compares outcomes such as fish biomass between protected and non-protected locations while accounting for the fact that the locations and policies were selected through existing ecological and social processes.
- Design features associated with stronger conservation outcomes include fishing prohibitions, effective enforcement, older age, larger area, and favorable surrounding habitat, although these features do not by themselves identify a causal effect.
- Surveys may overrepresent accessible regions near shorelines, markets, or larger human populations. Such preferential sampling can differ between MPA and non-MPA sites.
- Spatial causal models can use measured environmental and socioeconomic covariates together with latent spatial processes to separate policy effects from geographic placement patterns.
- Even after excluding nearby non-MPA sites, ecological interference through fish movement and residual social, political, or ecological confounding can remain.
