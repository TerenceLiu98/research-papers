## Overview

Elisabeth Noelle-Neumann's theory links fear of social isolation to the public visibility of an opinion. People take cues from those who speak around them when judging whether their own view has support. Someone who anticipates isolation may stay silent, depriving others who share the view of a signal that they have an ally.

## Key Ideas

- The feedback runs through the audience. One person's silence changes what the next person hears. As a result, an apparent consensus can grow while the distribution of private beliefs stays fixed.
- A person's contacts shape which opinions seem locally prevalent. A numerical minority with frequent internal contact may hear enough agreement to remain vocal. If both camps have this support, both can remain publicly audible.
- Public statements reveal the mix of views among speakers. Estimating the size of a silent group requires evidence about people who had an opportunity to speak and chose to stay silent.

## Important Papers

- Elisabeth Noelle-Neumann (1974), “The Spiral of Silence: A Theory of Public Opinion,” develops the link between perceived support, fear of isolation, and willingness to speak.
- [[papers/dynamics-of-opinion-expression|Dynamics of opinion expression]] derives the amount of within-group contact needed to sustain expression when the opposing group is silent and when it also speaks.
