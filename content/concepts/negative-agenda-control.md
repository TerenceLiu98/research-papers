## Overview

Negative agenda control is the ability to prevent consideration or advancement of proposals that would produce outcomes opposed by the controlling coalition. A majority party can exercise this power through committee gatekeeping or through floor procedures.

## Key Ideas

- Committee control and numerical floor control are distinct institutional sources of power. Holding committee organization fixed helps distinguish their effects.
- Blocking revisions of favorable status quos can shape policy without requiring a majority to enact its ideal proposal.
- Roll-call cutpoints can reveal changes in the set of status quos considered for revision, but are not direct measures of enacted policy locations.
- Agenda selection also affects estimated legislator ideal points: revealed voting behavior can change when the choice set changes.
