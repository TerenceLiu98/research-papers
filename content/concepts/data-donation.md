## Overview

Data donation is a research method in which participants voluntarily provide personal digital trace data for scientific use. It can give independent researchers access to behaviors and exposures that platforms do not make observable through public APIs, surveys, or public user activity. Because the donated records can be sensitive, the method couples measurement design with informed consent, data minimization, security, pseudonymization, and participant withdrawal rights.

## Key Ideas

- Donation can capture exposure rather than only public expression. [[What News Do People Get on Social Media? Analyzing Exposure and Consumption of News through Data Donations]] observes posts shown in participants' feeds as well as public and private forms of engagement.
- Collection should be purpose-limited. The CheckMyNews design records public news content but restricts private news records and excludes private non-news posts.
- Automated background instrumentation reduces recall error and respondent burden, but software reliability and platform markup changes become part of measurement validity.
- Privacy protections do not remove selection effects. People willing to install monitoring software can differ systematically from the target population, and attrition after consent or installation can further narrow the analytic sample.
- Monitoring can alter the behavior being measured. Longer observation may help behavior stabilize, but it does not prove that reactivity has disappeared.
- Participant control and research transparency are central: consent, withdrawal and data-removal rights, auditable code, and limited identifiers determine whether donation is ethically credible.
