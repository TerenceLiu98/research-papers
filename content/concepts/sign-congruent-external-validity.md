## Overview

A mechanism has sign-congruent external validity between settings when, for almost every common measurement strategy and contrast, the empirical targets have the same sign. Exact external validity additionally requires equal magnitudes and therefore implies sign-congruent external validity, but not conversely.

## Key Ideas

- Directional theories can remain informative even when effect sizes vary across populations or settings.
- Target-congruence concerns agreement among study targets; sign-congruent external validity concerns a mechanism across settings under common designs.
- Observed estimates include statistical error. A positive significant estimate and a nonsignificant estimate do not automatically demonstrate opposite effects.
- Sign comparisons require harmonized designs and suitable estimator properties to support an external-validity interpretation.
- Agreement under one design does not establish the almost-every-design property in the formal definition.
