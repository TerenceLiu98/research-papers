## Overview

Moral economy examines the normative expectations embedded in economic relationships. Actors can disagree about appropriate conduct even when they participate in the same market or platform.

## Key Ideas

- Economic exchange is evaluated through obligations and legitimacy, not only price or profitability.
- In Lu’s account of creator work, audience commitments to sincerity and transparency conflict with brand promotion and platform priorities of optimisation, marketability and competitive self-reliance.
- **Contingent moral improvisation** describes situational, revisable disclosure practices and boundaries used to navigate these conflicts without standardised institutional support. It is a response to competing moral economies, not a synonym for moral economy itself.
- Individual compromises may maintain trust temporarily while leaving reputational risks and responsibility concentrated on creators.

## Important Papers

- Thompson (1971), “The moral economy of the English crowd in the eighteenth century”: the foundational formulation identified in Lu’s literature review.
- [[papers/between-audience-lords-and-rich-fathers|Between ‘audience lords’ and ‘rich fathers’]]: applies the lens to conflicting audience, advertiser and platform expectations in Chinese technology reviewing.
