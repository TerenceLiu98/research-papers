## Overview

Online political incivility is disrespectful or hostile political communication that attacks people or groups rather than engaging their arguments. Operational definitions vary, but common indicators include insults, profanity, name-calling, personal attacks, and racist, sexist, or xenophobic language. The concept covers both user-generated and media-produced content, so the producer, audience, platform, and communication mode should be specified.

## Key Ideas

- Incivility is a family of operational measures rather than a universal score. Automated toxicity systems, dictionaries, and human coding can capture overlapping but nonidentical forms such as insults, threats, profanity, and identity attacks.
- Platform comparisons can confound affordances with ownership, moderation, producers, audiences, and topics. Comparisons are more informative when these sources of heterogeneity are addressed explicitly.
- Communication mode may matter even when producers are identifiable and organizational ownership is held constant. Video formats can combine language with visual and auditory cues, emotional arousal, and performance, although language-only comparisons cannot estimate those nonverbal contributions directly.
- Incivility can affect both political engagement and trust. Exposure may discourage information consumption or lower social and political trust, while partisan audiences and producers may use inflammatory content to intensify identification.
- Moral rhetoric and toxic rhetoric can be co-produced in the same message. An association between them should not automatically be interpreted as a causal effect of moral language on toxicity.
