## Overview

Fear of exclusion in club formation arises when joining an immediately attractive group can expose an individual to a worse eventual membership outcome. A person may secure a suboptimal but sustainable club because waiting or reorganizing risks exclusion from that club. The mechanism can emerge from rational continuation incentives without an assumed psychological cost of exclusion.

## Key Ideas

- **Sustainable and optimal differ.** A club maximizing current utility need not survive future membership choices.
- **An inferior destination gives exclusion bite.** In Joshi's construction, some individuals must end up in a worse residual club. If everyone eventually obtained the same sustainable payoff, this particular deterrent would disappear.
- **Patience strengthens the mechanism.** A sufficiently important future membership risk can outweigh a temporary gain from optimal club size.
- **Commitment is endogenous and limited.** Members cannot promise to remain in a newly formed optimal club. Their later incentives to secure better sustainable membership undermine such promises.
- **Existence does not imply inevitability.** The construction supports a specific class of absorbing equilibria; it neither selects a unique outcome nor rules out other club structures.
