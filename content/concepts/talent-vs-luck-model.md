## Overview

The Talent vs Luck Model is a stylized agent-based model of career outcomes. Agents have fixed, normally distributed talent and equal initial capital, but encounter different random sequences of favorable and unfavorable events. Because those events change capital multiplicatively, modest differences in event histories can generate extreme inequality even when talent is much less dispersed.

## Key Ideas

- **Talent governs opportunity conversion:** A favorable encounter doubles capital only when a random draw falls below the agent's talent. Greater talent therefore raises the probability of exploiting an opportunity without guaranteeing that an opportunity arrives.
- **Setbacks do not depend on talent:** An unfavorable encounter halves capital for every agent. This asymmetry is an explicit modeling assumption, not an empirical law about careers.
- **Multiplicative histories create dispersion:** Repeated doubling and halving turn small differences in event sequences into large capital differences. The resulting power-law-like distribution is generated by the model's dynamics rather than inherited from the talent distribution.
- **Individual and aggregate advantages differ:** Conditional on the same opportunity, a more talented agent has an advantage. Across a population, the much larger number of moderately talented agents makes it likely that an extreme run of luck occurs among them.
- **Policy conclusions are model-conditional:** Broad or random allocations can reach talented agents whose prior outcomes conceal their talent, whereas winner-based allocations reinforce realized luck. This ranking depends on the event process, allocation schedule, and chosen success metric.
