## Overview

Social desirability bias arises when perceived social expectations shift reported answers away from honest responses. Susceptibility can depend on the topic and respondent: awareness of a norm alone does not imply bias if honest preferences already align with it.

## Key Ideas

- Indirect elicitation can obscure the sensitive purpose or provide alternative reasons for a response. In conjoint tasks, these mechanisms are termed imperceptibility and rationalization.
- A smaller sensitive-attribute effect is not sufficient evidence of reduced bias. Survey design can also change attention and genuine evaluation.
- Controlled comparisons should isolate these alternative design effects. A low-bias control is useful only insofar as it captures the relevant nuisance effect.
- Population averages can conceal susceptible subgroups. Prespecified subgroup criteria and independent prediction help distinguish heterogeneity from opportunistic selection.
- Relative mitigation does not establish truthful measurement or quantify the share of all bias removed.
