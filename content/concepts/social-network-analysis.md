## Overview

Social Network Analysis (SNA) studies relationships among entities by representing them as nodes and edges. It can describe structural position, connectivity, community organization, and paths through which information or influence moves. In the rumor-refutation setting, accounts are subjects, audiences are sub-nodes, and information flow is represented through edges and interaction behavior.

## Key Ideas

- Node-level measures such as degree, PageRank, and centrality identify accounts that occupy influential or connective positions.
- Edge and path structure help distinguish a highly visible account from an account embedded in a dense, cooperative information-sharing community.
- Community detection exposes groups of accounts that interact more closely with one another than with the rest of the network. High clustering is not required for an account to have broad reach.
- SNA becomes an evaluation framework when structural features are paired with content, behavioral, and audience indicators. The paper uses six criteria and 18 indicators rather than treating topology as the whole outcome.
- Network position is contextual: authority, audience composition, posting behavior, and cooperation can compensate for or amplify structural advantages.
- For misinformation intervention, network position is one input to agent selection rather than the objective itself. [[Competitive Concern Minimization]] finds that degree, PageRank, closeness, and initial concern rankings can vary in effectiveness across diffusion settings.
