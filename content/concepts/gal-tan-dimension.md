## Overview

The GAL-TAN dimension organizes socio-cultural political conflict between green, alternative, and libertarian positions at one end and traditional, authoritarian, and nationalist positions at the other. It complements the economic left-right axis by separating preferences about personal freedoms, social order, cultural authority, and related conflicts from preferences about state intervention and redistribution.

## Key Ideas

- GAL positions emphasize expanded personal freedoms and democratic participation, while TAN positions emphasize order, tradition, stability, and moral authority. Specific survey items and substantive interpretations vary across studies.
- The dimension need not align perfectly with economic left-right politics. A two-axis space can therefore identify left-TAN and right-GAL citizens whose preference combinations may be obscured by a single ideological scale.
- Position and salience are distinct. An organization can be placed on the dimension while devoting little advocacy attention to it, so studies of political supply should measure both when possible.
- Parties, interest groups, and citizens are not always measured identically. [[Making space: citizens, parties and interest groups in two ideological dimensions]] uses CHES expert placements for parties, adapted self-placements for groups, and a three-item European Election Study index for citizens; comparisons should retain this measurement caveat.
- The meaning and alignment of the socio-cultural axis vary across countries and over time. Western European party systems often run from left-GAL to right-TAN, but post-communist party systems can show different configurations.
