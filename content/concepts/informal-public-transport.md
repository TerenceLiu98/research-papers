## Overview

Informal public transport includes both flexible taxi-like services and shared minibuses or vans serving relatively stable corridors. For comparisons with formal bus networks, the shared corridor-based category is the relevant scope. Informality does not imply an absence of organization: operator agreements, competition, licensing, and local demand can constrain routes even without fixed stops or timetables.

## Key Ideas

- Stable corridors can coexist with local changes to avoid congestion or seek passengers.
- Structural efficiency concerns route geometry and connectivity; operational quality also requires frequency, reliability, safety, and availability.
- Shortest-path-relative detour profiles distinguish excess distance at route ends from excess distance in the middle.
- Profit incentives may help explain direct routes, but an observational comparison does not identify that mechanism or establish social optimality.
