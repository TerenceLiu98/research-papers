## Overview

Issue publics describe selective political engagement: a citizen may hold consequential opinions on one controversy while having little interest or formed opinion on others. Their boundaries vary by issue and need not coincide with a single hierarchy of general political sophistication.

## Key Ideas

- Distinguish expressing an answer when surveyed from having a meaningful prior concern about the issue.
- Memberships can overlap without being nested; concern about a narrow issue does not guarantee engagement with a broader one.
- Temporal stability and spontaneous expressions of concern provide evidence of membership, but neither is an infallible classifier. Chance consistency and genuine opinion change complicate inference.
- Restricting attitude comparisons to people engaged with both issues can increase measured constraint. Such pairwise restrictions change the population across comparisons and do not yield a single representative correlation matrix.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/the-nature-of-belief-systems-in-mass-publics|The Nature of Belief Systems in Mass Publics (1964)]]: develops the concept using panel response patterns and open-ended evidence of concern; maps eight partially overlapping publics.
