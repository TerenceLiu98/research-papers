## Overview

Multimodal Critical Discourse Analysis (MCDA) studies how language, images, sound, gesture, spatial arrangement, camera work, typography, and other semiotic resources combine to construct social meanings and sustain or challenge relations of power. It extends critical discourse analysis beyond verbal language: the object of analysis is not simply what a text says, but how modes work together to make particular identities, practices, and ideological positions appear natural, credible, or desirable.

## Key Ideas

- Meaning arises from relations among modes. Speech, framing, setting, editing, expression, and embodied action can reinforce one another or create tension.
- Semiotic choices are socially situated rather than neutral. What is foregrounded, backgrounded, categorized, aestheticized, or omitted affects how people and institutions are represented.
- Camera distance, angle, point of view, and gaze organize relations between represented participants and viewers, including intimacy, authority, detachment, and vicarious participation.
- Critical interpretation connects detailed formal choices to social practice and power, while keeping the distinction between observable features and inferred ideological effects explicit.
- Platform formats and creator performances shape apparent authenticity. First-person testimony and informal production can still be curated, selective, and aligned with broader commercial or political narratives.
- Analytical dimensions should fit the material. For Laowai Wanghong vlogs, Li, Wu, and Fitzgerald distinguish symbolic stage, performance, interpretation, and gaze; these categories are a study-specific operationalization, not an exhaustive MCDA taxonomy.

## Important Papers

- Machin (2013), "What Is Multimodal Critical Discourse Studies?": outlines the critical study of ideology through visual and other semiotic resources.
- [[papers/selling-chinas-story-the-multimodal-semiotics-of-positive-symbolic-communication|Selling China's Story: The Multimodal Semiotics of Positive Symbolic Communication in National Image Building through Laowai Wanghong's Vlogs]]: combines MCDA with symbolic communication to analyze how foreign vloggers construct a selective positive national image through settings, performances, speech, and camera perspectives.
- Wang, Feng, and Wang (2024), "Modernity, Aesthetics, and Nation Re-branding in Olympics": analyzes how linguistic and visual resources construct national-image attributes in the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics opening ceremony.
