## Overview

Street-level discretion is the latitude frontline public officials have to interpret rules and make case-specific decisions within institutional constraints. In AI-supported administration, automation can change available information, permissible workflows, and the grounds for justifying decisions, as well as who retains final authority.

## Key Ideas

- **Granted, perceived, and used discretion differ.** Formal authority defines permitted latitude; perceived discretion is an official's subjective assessment of that latitude; used discretion concerns its exercise in practice. Evidence about one does not establish changes in the others.
- **Automation can constrain or enable.** Standardization can narrow judgment, while better information and decision support can expand officials' capacity to handle cases. Organizational rules and [[concepts/human-oversight-of-ai|Human Oversight of AI]] help determine which process occurs.
- **Information and justification are distinct resources.** In the Chinese tax study, interviews suggest that AI assists risk assessment and supplies organizationally defensible grounds for decisions. Human judgment remains salient in contested cases, sanctions, and field verification.
- **Institutional context bounds interpretation.** Regulatory work emphasizes legality, evidence, and defensibility; service-oriented work may depend more heavily on relational and contextual knowledge. The tax study proposes this distinction as a boundary condition without directly comparing those settings.
- **User competence can condition perceptions.** [[concepts/algorithm-literacy|Algorithm Literacy]] may affect how officials interpret and selectively use recommendations. Exploratory component-level interactions should not be treated as general causal laws.
