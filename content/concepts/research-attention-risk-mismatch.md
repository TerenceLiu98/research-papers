## Overview

Research attention-risk mismatch occurs when the amount or breadth of scholarship devoted to a place or hazard is disproportionate to the risk borne there. High-risk, low-attention cases are potential knowledge gaps, while low-risk, high-attention cases may reflect greater scientific capacity, data availability, institutional priorities, or historical specialization. A mismatch is a relationship between operational measures, not proof that a particular publication allocation is optimal or unjustified.

## Key Ideas

- **Define attention and risk explicitly.** Attention may be measured by publication counts, normalized research output, thematic breadth, or study locations. Risk may refer to hazard frequency, people affected, expected loss, exposure, or vulnerability. Different choices can change which places appear mismatched.
- **Use a stated benchmark.** A residual from a model predicting attention from risk is one approach: a positive residual denotes more attention than predicted and a negative residual denotes less. The result is conditional on the model form, time window, data coverage, and treatment of zero values.
- **Volume and knowledge structure are distinct.** A country can receive many studies yet have narrow coverage of hazard types or governance pathways. Conversely, specialization may be appropriate when physical conditions strongly favor one hazard.
- **Capacity can mediate observed attention.** Wealth, scientific infrastructure, data access, language, and international partnerships can shape where research is produced. Associations with these factors do not by themselves identify causal mechanisms.
- **Bibliographic absence is not knowledge absence.** English-language databases often omit gray literature, local-language work, government practice, and community knowledge. A measured gap may partly be an observation gap.
- **Future risk can invalidate historical alignment.** Climate change, urbanization, and population growth can create emerging risk frontiers, so attention calibrated only to past disaster impacts may remain poorly targeted.
- **Policy use requires diagnosis.** Funding and collaboration may address capacity constraints, while open data may address measurement constraints. Neither automatically resolves political, institutional, or thematic barriers.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/global-flood-research-patterns-disparities-and-risk-attention-mismatches-revealed-by-large-language-models|Global flood research patterns, disparities, and risk-attention mismatches revealed by large language models]]: estimates a publication residual from flood-affected population and reports mismatched attention for 53.1% of countries, alongside thematic and flood-type disparities.
