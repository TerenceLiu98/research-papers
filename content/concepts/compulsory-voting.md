## Overview

Compulsory voting makes electoral participation a legal obligation. Enforcement varies, and compelled attendance remains distinct from casting a valid vote. Its normative justifications include civic duty, equality of participation, democratic legitimacy, and potential consequences for party competition.

## Key Ideas

- Penalties for abstention change the costs of participation relative to nonparticipation. State capacity and actual enforcement determine whether a mandate substantially raises turnout.
- Alienated voters can otherwise threaten to stay home when every party is too distant from their preferences. Effective compulsory participation can weaken this threat and reduce parties’ incentives to move toward their ideological flanks.
- This convergence mechanism is conditional on electoral institutions and voter behavior. It is developed for majoritarian competition and cannot simply be transferred to proportional representation.
- Invalid ballots separate attendance from effective electoral support. More turnout does not guarantee a corresponding increase in valid proximity-based votes.
- Platform moderation, affective polarization, and support for democracy are different outcomes. Improvement in one does not establish improvement in all three.
