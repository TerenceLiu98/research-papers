## Overview

Gamson’s Law links a party’s contribution of legislative seats to its numerical share of cabinet ministries. Approximate proportionality is an empirical pattern, not a claim that every cabinet allocates portfolios exactly in proportion to seats.

## Key Ideas

- Exact proportionality equates the two share compositions; deviations can reward smaller parties relative to legislative size.
- Numerical portfolio counts do not themselves measure differences in ministry importance.
- Both sides of the relationship are compositions. Estimated proportionality depends on how a model handles boundedness and within-cabinet dependence.
- An average association can coexist with substantial variation across bargaining episodes.

## Important Papers

- [[papers/refining-gamson-the-isometric-log-ratio-transformation-and-portfolio-proportionality-in-multiparty-governments|Refining Gamson]] reports weaker average proportionality and detectable government-level heterogeneity using ILR regression.
