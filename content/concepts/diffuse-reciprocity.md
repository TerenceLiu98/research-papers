## Overview

Diffuse reciprocity extends responses to cooperative or conflictual encounters beyond the original interaction partner. In intergroup cooperation models, experience with one out-group member can change willingness to cooperate with other members of that group, allowing behavior to spread across encounters.

## Key Ideas

- The response generalizes across partners instead of requiring repayment by the same person in the same dyad.
- Positive and negative encounters can both propagate: experiencing cooperation can encourage future cooperation, while experiencing defection can sustain mistrust.
- Diffuse reciprocity differs from social imitation. The former updates behavior from an agent's own encounters; the latter updates behavior from observing other agents and their outcomes.
- In the Peterson, Galesic, and Hammond model, increased contact among treated, more cooperative agents helps transmit intervention effects through reciprocity. Within-group imitation provides a separate transmission channel.
- Whether these channels preserve gains depends on surrounding behavior. A predominantly uncooperative environment can erode participants' initial improvements.
