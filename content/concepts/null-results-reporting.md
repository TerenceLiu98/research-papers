## Overview

Null results reporting makes findings available even when researchers do not pursue conventional publication. Its purpose is to improve the evidence available for cumulative research, including information about interventions and designs that did not yield statistically significant effects.

## Key Ideas

- **Disclosure complements preregistration.** Registering a design does not reveal its eventual findings.
- **Interpretation requires diagnosis.** A report should examine power, measurement, implementation, spillovers or contamination, and theoretical assumptions. Nonsignificance alone does not demonstrate absence of a meaningful effect.
- **Costs and incentives matter.** Brief templates can reduce writing effort; lab and departmental standards can establish expectations for disclosure.
- **Discoverability matters.** Searchable registries and preprint repositories help other researchers locate and cite results.

## Related Concepts

Publication bias motivates disclosure of findings otherwise left out of the accessible literature. Preregistration records intended designs, while null results reporting discloses outcomes and discusses their interpretation. Statistical power helps distinguish inconclusive estimates from evidence against effects of substantive interest.
