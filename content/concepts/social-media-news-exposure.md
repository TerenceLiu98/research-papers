## Overview

Social media news exposure concerns the news content that appears to users through a platform and the mechanisms that place it there. Exposure is broader than consumption or public engagement: a post can appear on screen without the user opening, endorsing, sharing, remembering, or believing it. Treating the feed as one undifferentiated channel can conceal how user choice, social ties, recommendation systems, and paid targeting create different news diets.

## Key Ideas

- Selective exposure occurs when users follow or subscribe to news sources and receive those sources' posts.
- Incidental exposure occurs when news appears through friends, groups, communities, or pages encountered for reasons other than an explicit subscription to the linked source.
- Algorithmic exposure occurs when a platform recommends news based on predicted relevance or prior behavior, such as a "Suggested for you" post.
- Targeted exposure occurs when an advertiser pays the platform to deliver a news-related post to an audience selected by specified attributes.
- Exposure, attention, consumption, and engagement are distinct outcomes. On-screen visibility is only a proxy for attention, opening a link is stronger evidence of consumption, and public reactions can differ from private interactions.
- The mechanisms can produce different source-quality and ideological distributions. [[What News Do People Get on Social Media? Analyzing Exposure and Consumption of News through Data Donations]] reports that selective exposure was least politically balanced and had the largest share of posts from sources labeled as repeatedly spreading misinformation, while targeted exposure had the largest share from unrated under-the-radar sources.
- Measuring actual exposure requires access to what appears in a feed. [[Data Donation]] is one way to obtain these records without relying solely on surveys, public posts, or clicked links.
